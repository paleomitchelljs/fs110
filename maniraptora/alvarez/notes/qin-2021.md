# Qin et al. 2021, "Growth and miniaturization among alvarezsauroid dinosaurs"

Qin, Zhao, Choiniere, Clark, Benton & Xu. *Current Biology* 31:3687-3693, 23 Aug 2021. doi 10.1016/j.cub.2021.06.013. Published 6 July 2021, corrected 29 July 2021. The PDF ends with the correction (pp. 15-16, *Curr Biol* 31:3705-3706). Read it before quoting the OU result. See below.

File: `papers/1-s2.0-S0960982221008083-mainext.pdf`, 16 pages. Makovicky's ref 2.

## Claim

- Definition: "Sustained miniaturization, here defined as a drop in body size of at least two orders of magnitude from ancestors to descendants."
- "alvarezsauroid body mass underwent rapid miniaturization from around 110 to 85 mya and that there was a phylogenetic radiation of small-sized alvarezsauroids in the Late Cretaceous."
- Cause, hedged: "might have been driven by ecological changes during the Cretaceous Terrestrial Revolution, more specifically by a shift to the myrmecophagous ecological niche."

## Numbers

- "The inferred ancestral body mass of alvarezsauroids is around 23 kg."
- With juveniles left in: "8 kg versus 23 kg for the corrected dataset."
- "From 110 to 85 Ma (Albian to Santonian), mean body size along the alvarezsauroid stem plunged rapidly to a very small mean body mass of <5 kg."
- "the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model is preferred, with lowest AICc score of 46.580, an AICc weight of 0.744, and p = 0.012 when compared to the next-best-fit model, the Brownian motion model."
- OU read as "the optimal body mass was very low relative to other theropods."
- Lineage richness triples at about 90 Ma.

Table 1 (adult masses, kg, 95% CI): *Albinykus* 1.07; *Bannykus* 29.13 (subadult); *Bonapartenykus* 71.55; *Ceratonykus* 0.26; *Haplocheirus* 41.00 (juvenile, estimated from growth curve); *Linhenykus* 2.00; *Mononykus* 4.66; *Nemegtonykus* 3.31; *Parvicursor* 0.15; *Patagonykus* 33.60; *Qiupanykus* 0.50; *Shishugounykus* 6.01; *Shuvuuia* 3.05 (juvenile); *Tugulusaurus* 11.51; *Xixianykus* 0.74; *Xiyunykus* 17.26; *Achillesaurus* 30.00; *Alvarezsaurus* 28.00. *Aorun* isn't in the table.

## The correction

Two errors, found by Roger Benson.
- Rate heterogeneity was run on untransformed mass. On log mass there is "no inference of rate shifts." The sentence about little innovation after node 27 is deleted.
- Branch lengths. "we arbitrarily chose the 'equal' method of the strap package, which indeed returns a preference for our data fitting an OU model." "using the 'mbl' argument to calculate branch lengths using the 'palaeotree' package returns a preference for a BM model." So "we can no longer robustly support selective pressure for smaller body sizes. However, our qualitative observations about the decrease in alvarezsauroid body mass remain unaffected."

The main text in this PDF still says OU is preferred with weight 0.744. The correction is the reason [Makovicky 2026](makovicky-2026.md) fitting models across 41,200 differently time-scaled trees matters: that is the sensitivity the correction found, done on purpose.

## Juveniles

- *Aorun* holotype: "even younger, being likely a hatchling." *Haplocheirus* and a *Shuvuuia* specimen: juveniles.
- "we suggest that an ontogenetic stage check is essential when making a body size dataset, especially in the case of early-branching members of the target group."
- "In cases where some taxa are represented only by very young specimens, like the holotypes of Aorun zhaoi and Haplocheirus sollers, they should either be removed (eliminate distinct outliers) or their adult body masses could be estimated."

## Histology

*Xixianykus* and *Albinykus*: similar size, different growth. *Xixianykus* age 11 at death, *Albinykus* 3. "the main factor producing size variance among alvarezsauroids is annual growth rate rather than longevity."

## Diet

Leans on termites in mid-Cretaceous Burmese amber. Hedged at the end: "The coincidence of substantial and rapid size reduction and enhancement of apparent adaptations for digging seen in Late Cretaceous alvarezsauroids may indicate an insectivore feeding habit, but more details from ethological and functional research are needed."

## Against Makovicky 2026

- Qin: OU preferred, "rapid miniaturization," then withdrawn as not robust in the correction. Makovicky: white noise and lambda, "no support for evolutionary miniaturization."
- Qin keeps *Haplocheirus* (41 kg) and *Tugulusaurus* inside the clade. Makovicky's ED Fig 7a puts both outside.
- Qin drops *Aorun* as a hatchling. Makovicky has *Aorun* at the root at about 1 kg (ED Fig 9b by eye).
- Both choices pull the root down in Makovicky. The deck asks how much, with a toy model, and gives no verdict.
