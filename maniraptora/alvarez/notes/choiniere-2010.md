# Choiniere et al. 2010, "A basal alvarezsauroid theropod from the early Late Jurassic of Xinjiang, China"

Choiniere, Xu, Clark, Forster, Guo & Han. *Science* 327:571-574, 29 Jan 2010. doi 10.1126/science.1182143.

File: `papers/science.1182143.pdf`, 5 pages. The text layer interleaves the title, columns and figure text on p1, so quotes from p1 have to stay inside one column.

## Claim

- "A new theropod from the earliest Late Jurassic of western China represents the earliest diverging member of the enigmatic theropod group Alvarezsauroidea and confirms that this group is a basal member of Maniraptora." "It extends the fossil record of Alvarezsauroidea by 63 million years."
- *Haplocheirus sollers*, IVPP V15988, upper Shishugou Formation, 158.7-161.2 Ma (Oxfordian). About 140 cm as preserved, 190-230 cm estimated.
- "The basal phylogenetic position and early temporal position of Haplocheirus imply that Alvarezsauroidea originated in Asia rather than South America."
- Ghost lineage: "a minimum ghost lineage (35) of ~63 million years."
- Stratigraphic fit improves "by an average of 14%, from a mean fit of 0.41 to 0.48."
- Size: "Haplocheirus is the largest alvarezsauroid known from complete material." Its "basal phylogenetic position suggests a pattern of miniaturization for the Alvarezsauroidea."
- Diet: "recurved, serrated teeth and caniniforms that suggest carnivory was the primitive condition for the clade."
- Hand: "fully functional," some grasping, but McIV short and slender already.
- Placement: avialan constraint costs seven steps; ornithomimosaur constraint one step.

## Against later papers

- [Qin 2021](qin-2021.md) calls the holotype a juvenile.
- [Makovicky 2026](makovicky-2026.md) ED Fig 7a puts *Haplocheirus* outside Alvarezsauroidea. The one-step ornithomimosaur cost here is a reminder of how little separated those placements in 2010.
