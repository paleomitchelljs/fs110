# Lü et al. 2018, "A new alvarezsaurid dinosaur from the Late Cretaceous Qiupa Formation of Luanchuan, Henan Province, central China"

Lü, Xu, Chang, Jia, Zhang, Gao, Zhang, Zhang & Ding. *China Geology* 1:28-35. doi 10.31035/cg2018005. Not cited by Makovicky; *Qiupanykus* isn't in its tree.

File: `papers/1-s2.0-S2096519219300047-main.pdf`, 8 pages.

## Claim

- *Qiupanykus zhangi*, 41HIII-0101. Partial postcranium.
- "The skeleton of the new specimen is preserved in association with eggshells." The shell is 1.8 mm thick and matches the oviraptorid eggs from the same area.
- Egg mass from shell thickness: about 1136 g, and "the egg could not be laid by Qiupanykus."
- "The alvarezsaurid skeleton associated with eggshell fragments may indicate that these eggs were broken by the strong thumb-claws of the former and that alvarezsaurid dinosaurs may be egg-eaters."
- They list three possibilities themselves, starting with burial "by coincidence."
