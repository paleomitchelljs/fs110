# Xu et al. 2018, "Two Early Cretaceous fossils document transitional stages in alvarezsaurian dinosaur evolution"

Xu, Choiniere, Tan, Benson, Clark, Sullivan, Zhao, Han, Ma, He, Wang, Xing & Tan. *Current Biology* 28:2853-2860, 10 Sep 2018. doi 10.1016/j.cub.2018.07.057. Makovicky's ref 11.

File: `papers/PIIS0960982218309874.pdf`, 12 pages.

## Claim

- *Xiyunykus pengi* (Tugulu Group, Xinjiang) and *Bannykus wulatensis* (Inner Mongolia), both Early Cretaceous, both subadult.
- Gap: "A 70-million-year ghost lineage exists between them and the Late Jurassic putative alvarezsaurian Haplocheirus [12], which preserves so few derived features that its membership in Alvarezsauria has recently been questioned [13]."
- Answer: places "Haplocheirus firmly within Alvarezsauria." Also groups *Aorun* and *Tugulusaurus* inside.
- Forelimb: "evolved slowly and in a mosaic fashion during the Cretaceous." *Bannykus* humerus 38% of femur.
- "Bannykus and Xiyunykus share many of the fossorial features of the forelimb of parvicursorines but retain a plesiomorphically longer forelimb."
- Extreme arm reduction "occurred late in alvarezsaurian evolution, and at small body mass (estimated around 24 kg; Figure 4A)."
- Regression (Fig 4A): log10(arm length) = 0.30 log10(body mass) + 2.12, p < 0.001, N = 28.

## Biogeography

- "dispersal ..., rather than vicariance ..., characterizes the early biogeographic history of the group, given that all of these taxa are Asian." "alvarezsaurians originated in Asia and remained there throughout the Cretaceous."
- The road not taken, in their words: "The alternative vicariance hypothesis would involve alvarezsaurians spreading throughout Pangaea before the Aptian, with different species ending up on different continents as Pangaea broke apart." That is close to what [Makovicky 2026](makovicky-2026.md) recovers.
- Method: S-DIVA in RASP. "The direct land link between Asia and South America is considered unavailable during the Jurassic and Cretaceous."
- "Our results illustrate the potential of increased taxon sampling to change biogeographic inferences."
