# Xu et al. 2011, "A monodactyl nonavian dinosaur and the complex evolution of the alvarezsauroid hand"

Xu, Sullivan, Pittman, Choiniere, Hone, Upchurch, Tan, Xiao, Tan & Han. *PNAS* 108:2338-2342, 8 Feb 2011. doi 10.1073/pnas.1011052108. Makovicky's ref 20.

File: `papers/pnas.201011052.pdf`, 5 pages.

## Claim

- *Linhenykus monodactylus*, IVPP V17608, Wulansuhai Formation, Inner Mongolia. Basal parvicursorine.
- "Phalanges are retained only on the most medial digit (digit II), making Linhenykus the only known functionally monodactyl nonavian dinosaur." The paper numbers the digits II-IV; Makovicky numbers the same digits I-III.
- "The unexpected combination of features seen in the hand of Linhenykus points to a complex mosaic pattern of manual evolution in alvarezsauroids, with loss of the presumably vestigial outer digits being decoupled from change in the form of digit II."
- Biogeography: "the Alvarezsauroidea originated in Asia, and at least three dispersal events subsequently occurred: one from Asia to Gondwana, one from Gondwana to Asia, and finally one from Asia to North America." That is the "back-and-forth dispersal" [Makovicky 2026](makovicky-2026.md) cites as ref 20.
- Their own caveat: "the Jurassic fossil record of alvarezsauroids is scant, and this group is currently unknown from the Lower Cretaceous. A more stringent test of the biogeographic hypothesis awaits further data from these poorly represented time periods."
