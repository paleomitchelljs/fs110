# Pennaraptora deck plan (draft)

Goal: show students how to combine several lines of evidence on one question: what each line can and can't show, where each is strong or thin, and how the lines support, constrain or contradict each other. Each section is built around one question, not around a paper.

Length: about twice the alvarezsaur deck, with a break between Part 1 and Part 2.

## How "strong/weak" gets shown without verdicts

Slides state the size of the evidence and the assumptions behind it as plain facts, and students judge the weight. Examples: "1 tooth", "3 eggs", "7 taxa, 12 characters", "crocodile timing assumed", "raw color olive-green". Chips can be questions ("Same clutch?"). The judgments ("thin", "suggestive") go in the readme as speaker notes. This follows the earlier rule of no verdicts on slides.

Each section follows the same pattern:
1. The question, with one image.
2. Lines of evidence, one per slide: claim (short lens), the paper's own figure, and a fact chip giving sample size or assumption.
3. Interaction: where lines constrain each other or conflict (two figures side by side).
4. Small matrix: lines × "what it can show / what it can't."

## Part 1

### 0. The groups
A short tour: one specimen image each for troodontids, dromaeosaurids, oviraptorosaurs (+ scansoriopterygids), and avialans. Each group comes back later; no catalog of taxa.
- Troodontids: *Mei* (Xu & Norell 2004), *Gobivenator* (Tsuihiji 2014), *Jianianhualong* (Xu 2017)
- Dromaeosaurids: *Velociraptor* (Bindellini 2025), *Zhenyuanlong* (Lü & Brusatte 2015), *Austroraptor* (Novas 2009)
- Oviraptorosaurs: skulls (Ma 2020), tails (Persons 2014), *Yi* (Xu 2015)
- Avialans: Chicago *Archaeopteryx* (O'Connor 2025), *Fujianvenator* (Xu 2023)

### 1. Phylogeny: which group is closest to birds?
- One new specimen moves *Archaeopteryx* into Deinonychosauria (Xu 2011; bootstrap <50%, Bremer 2).
- The same matrix family grows over time and the answer changes: three-way polytomy, anchiornithines in Troodontidae (Brusatte 2014, 150 taxa × 853 characters); Deinonychosauria sister to birds, anchiornithines as the first birds (Pei 2020). Put the three trees side by side.
- How strong any of these is: "a single step separates several different paravian phylogenies" (Hartman 2019); support "very low within Paraves" (Pei 2020). Fact chips: Bremer 1-2.
- Independent line: an egg-only tree (Zelenitsky & Therrien 2008) recovers oviraptorids → troodontids → birds. Fact chips: "7 taxa, 12 characters".
- Reproduction (Varricchio & Jackson 2016): "Reproduction in troodontid theropods concurs with this clade representing the sister taxon to birds."
- Interaction: eggs and skeletons are separate data, but egg characters are few and partly assumed (adult contact coded "absent" for sauropods by assumption).
- Short arms in big dromaeosaurids and troodontids (Zheng 2010, Lü & Brusatte 2015, Xu 2011 *Linhevenator*, Novas 2009) set up the flight question: winged but not flying.

### 2. Ecology and behavior
- Sleep: two *Mei* specimens in the same posture (Xu & Norell 2004; Gao 2012).
- Groups: oviraptorosaur bonebed (Funston 2016); *Deinonychus* young and adults ate differently, so not a wolf pack (Frederickson 2020; n = 10 + 10 teeth). Trackways still wanted (Li et al. 2008).
- Display: tail forms (Persons 2014), *Anchiornis* color (Li 2010), long tail feathers (Clark 2026).
- Diet: troodontid diet contested (Fan 2026); oviraptorosaur skull niche partitioning (Ma 2020); convergence with *Berthasaura* (Pierossi 2025). Good example of skull shape, teeth, gut contents and isotopes each giving a different answer.

### 3. Reproduction: did oviraptorosaurs and troodontids brood? (split out; the strongest section)
Lines, each with its own figure:
- Adult on clutch posture (Bi 2021; Jin 2020)
- Eggshell porosity → open nest (Tanaka 2015) — boundary result for oviraptorosaurs (0.513/0.487)
- Clutch geometry vs body size (Tanaka 2018)
- Egg color (Wiemann 2017) — raw olive-green, corrected blue-green
- Incubation length from embryo tooth lines (Varricchio 2018) — 1 tooth
- Embryo stage across a clutch (Varricchio 2002; Yang 2019; Bi 2021)
- Partial burial and plover analogue (Hogan & Varricchio 2023)
- Egg traits on a tree (Zelenitsky & Therrien 2008; Varricchio & Jackson 2016 five stages)

Interaction slide: Bi 2021 vs Yang 2019, same observation (asynchrony), opposite mechanism. Matrix: rows = lines, columns = "adult present / eggs warmed / eggs exposed / hatching timing".

**Break**

## Part 2

### 4. Feathers: what were pennaceous feathers for before flight?
- Color and display: *Anchiornis* (Li 2010); *Microraptor* iridescence (**need**).
- Asymmetry as a flight signal: *Jianianhualong* (Xu 2017); feather number and asymmetry calibrated on living birds (Kiat & O'Connor 2024; their Fig 2B photo strip).
- Feathers with no locomotor payoff: *Caudipteryx* forelimb feathers >100 mm (Dececchi 2016) — display or brooding.
- Big winged non-fliers: *Zhenyuanlong* (Lü & Brusatte 2015) — wings on an animal too big to fly.
- Tail fans and ornaments (Persons 2014; Clark 2026).
- Deep origin of feathers (**optional**).

### 5. Flight: how many times, and from what?
- Membrane wings: *Yi* (Xu 2015) → poor gliders (Dececchi 2020); climbing ability (Sorkin 2020).
- Running with wings: *Caudipteryx* (Talori 2018).
- Microraptorines (Poust 2020).
- WAIR, flap-running, leaping: nothing passes before Paraves (Dececchi 2016, Fig 2).
- Flight thresholds on a tree: green in three places (Pei 2020, Fig 2).
- Arm/leg ratios: long-armed fliers nested among short-armed relatives (Hartman 2019, Table 3, Fig 18).
- Feathers: anchiornithines and *Caudipteryx* flightless, one early origin (Kiat & O'Connor 2024).
- Shoulder girdle: change before birds, parallel refinement within early birds (Wu 2024, Fig 1).
- *Archaeopteryx*: bauplan (O'Connor 2025); wing bones like burst-flapping birds (Voeten 2018).
- Interaction slide: same fossils, three answers on number of origins (Pei, Kiat & O'Connor, Hartman), with Pei's own result that one origin + 6-11 losses costs the same tree length. *Archaeopteryx* is where the lines agree (Voeten, Pei, Dececchi).

### 6. Brains and the origin of birds
- Brain volume: *Archaeopteryx* sits among non-avian maniraptorans; bird-like indices evolved more than once (Balanoff 2013). Its "flight-ready brain" argument borrows from the skeleton.
- Flight-ready brain shape in non-avian paravians (Bronzati 2025).
- Brain shape in stages; crown-bird shape only near Aves (Torres 2021, Fig 3); the *Archaeopteryx* wulst retracted.
- Body size shrinking along the bird line for 50 My (Lee 2014, Fig 2B) vs a rate jump at Avialae after slow assembly (Brusatte 2014, Fig 2).
- Ancestral bird mass swings 10.9 → 2.4 kg with outgroup choice (Torres 2021): sampling sets ancestral states.
- Early avialan diversity: *Fujianvenator* long legs (Xu 2023); Chicago *Archaeopteryx* (O'Connor 2025).
- Closing matrix across the whole deck: questions × lines of evidence.
