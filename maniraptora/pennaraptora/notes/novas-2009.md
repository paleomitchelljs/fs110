# Novas et al. 2009, "A bizarre Cretaceous theropod dinosaur from Patagonia and the evolution of Gondwanan dromaeosaurids"

Novas, Pol, Canale, Porfiri & Calvo. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 276:1101-1107 (online 16 Dec 2008). doi 10.1098/rspb.2008.1554.

File: `papers/CONICET_Digital_Nro.1ff24baa-b5aa-44f0-bf40-0645ee8d819c_A.pdf`, 7 pages (CONICET repository copy). Read in full; both figures viewed.

## Claim

- *Austroraptor cabazai*, MML-195, Allen Fm. (Campanian-Maastrichtian), Río Negro. Partial: skull pieces, five cervicals, two dorsals, humerus, one manual ungual, pubic shaft, femur, tibia, foot bits.
- "approximately 5 m long." Skull "roughly 80 cm long as reconstructed." Body length and mass in Table 1 (p3) come from Turner et al. 2007 regressions; the text extraction of the table is garbled, so read the values off the PDF before quoting.
- Snout: maxilla depth 21% of length; 24 maxillary and 25 dentary teeth, "small, conical shaped ... devoid of mesial and distal carinae and denticles." Enamel "fluted, a condition resembling that of spinosaurid theropods."
- Arms: "The humerus is short and robust, representing approximately 46 per cent of femoral length." Deltopectoral crest reverts "to the (plesiomorphic) cranial orientation."
- Lacrimal: "the caudal extremity of the lacrimal is horizontally expanded into a fan-shaped process that largely contributes to the craniodorsal roofing of orbit ..., a bizarre condition yet unrecorded in other theropod dinosaurs."
- Recovers a monophyletic Unenlagiinae (*Unenlagia*, *Neuquenraptor*, *Buitreraptor*, *Rahonavis*, *Austroraptor*).
- Disparity: "By the end of the Cretaceous period, unenlagiine diversification included giant, short-armed forms (i.e. Austroraptor) alongside with crow-sized, winged representatives (i.e. Rahonavis)."
- Ecology: "towards the latest Cretaceous, large dromaeosaurids shared the role of large predators (approx. 5 m long) with abelisaurid ceratosaurians."

The skull is reconstructed largely from the maxilla and dentary, and the body is a silhouette with a handful of bones. The 5 m figure and the "spinosaur-like" snout both rest on fragments.
