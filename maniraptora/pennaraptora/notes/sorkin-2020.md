# Sorkin 2020, "Scansorial and aerial ability in Scansoriopterygidae and basal Oviraptorosauria"

Sorkin. *Historical Biology* (online 14 Dec 2020). doi 10.1080/08912963.2020.1855158.

File: `papers/sorkin2020.pdf`, 14 pages. Read in full; all six figures viewed.

## What kind of paper

A literature review by a single author at a community college, "supported by the author's personal funds only," with no new specimens or analysis: "the author did not consider his own phylogenetic analysis of Pennaraptora to have been relevant or practical." It argues for Gregory Paul's hypothesis that flightless oviraptorosaurs and deinonychosaurs descend from climbing, flying ancestors ("secondary flightlessness").

## Claim

- The key step: "This uncertainty about the exact phylogenetic position of Scansoriopterygidae among basal pennaraptorans suggests that scansoriopterygid morphology was close to the ancestral morphology of Pennaraptora." Same argument for Anchiornithinae and Paraves.
- "All known scansoriopterygids were capable of scansorial locomotion and gliding, but not powered flight."
- Therefore "the most recent common ancestor of Pennaraptora was also a scansorial four-winged glider," which "supports the evolution of pennaceous feathers as a flight adaptation and the 'from the trees down' hypothesis of bird flight evolution."
- Reads *Zhenyuanlong* as evidence that short-feathered specimens (*Sinornithosaurus*) are preservation artefacts: "Had soft tissue preservation been poorer, no trace of remiges would have been left." So "unless well-preserved short pennaceous forelimb feathers indicate otherwise, it can be assumed that all members of Microraptorinae possessed fully developed wings."
- On *Tianyuraptor* and *Zhenyuanlong*: independent size increase and arm shortening is "equally parsimonious" with Pei et al.'s large-ancestor scenario, and preferred because "evolutionary history of Avialae includes multiple instances of flight loss but none of flight acquisition by members of a secondarily flightless lineage."
- Hypothetical ancestor 'Oviraptorovolans' (from Sorkin 2014), and *Scansoriopteryx* as "a 'proto-Oviraptorovolans'?"
- Cites [Dececchi et al. 2020](dececchi-2020.md) as confirming gliding ("a capacity confirmed by wing loading estimates"), without their "poor glider" and "dead-end" conclusions.

## Push-back

- Phylogenetic uncertainty does not imply ancestral morphology. A clade whose position is unstable can be highly derived; scansoriopterygids are among the most aberrant theropods known (elongate digit III/IV, styliform element). The paper's main inference rests on this step.
- The *Scansoriopteryx* evidence is two juvenile specimens, "possibly hatchlings," mass ~7 g; climbing proportions in hatchlings are weak evidence for adult or ancestral ecology.
- The Dececchi citation is selective.
- Useful mainly as a clear statement of the "secondarily flightless" position, to set against [Dececchi et al. 2020](dececchi-2020.md) and [Lü & Brusatte 2015](lu-brusatte-2015.md)'s retention-for-display reading.
