# Torres, Norell & Clarke 2021, "Bird neurocranial and body mass evolution across the end-Cretaceous mass extinction: the avian brain shape left other dinosaurs behind"

Torres, Norell & Clarke. *Science Advances* 7:eabg7099 (2021). doi 10.1126/sciadv.abg7099. Open access (PMC8324052).

File: `papers/torres2021.pdf`, 9 pages. Main text read in full; all six figures viewed.

## Why it's in this folder

It extends the brain story past *Archaeopteryx* to near-crown birds and the end-Cretaceous extinction. Outside Pennaraptora's non-avian part, but it closes the "brains and the origin of birds" section.

## Claim

- Specimen: AMNH FARB 32773, a nearly complete skull of *Ichthyornis dispar* (~70 Ma, toothed stem bird), CT-scanned; partial endocast (crushed side to side).
- Brain shape: *Ichthyornis* "lacks the expanded cerebrum and ventrally shifted optic lobes characteristic of extant birds"; it "had an Archaeopteryx-like brain shape." So the last phase of bird brain reshaping "occurred near the origin of, and is synapomorphic for, extant birds."
- Fig 3 lays out phases: linear brains in tyrannosaurs; expanded cerebrum and cerebellum with tilted midbrain in non-avian maniraptorans (*Zanabazar*, *Incisivosaurus*); further cerebellum expansion near Avialae (*Archaeopteryx*); full avian shape only in crown birds.
- Wulst: *Ichthyornis* has "an incipient wulst," confirmed in uncrushed scan data. "A similar feature was tentatively identified in Archaeopteryx but was later shown to be a taphonomic artefact."
- Palate: evidence of a segmented, neognath-like palate, a feature thought to arise within modern birds.
- Body mass: ancestral crown-bird mass depends on which stem birds are included: 10.9 kg (only *Archaeopteryx* as stem), 7.7 kg (well-placed stem birds), 2.4 kg (all, including fragmentary latest Cretaceous birds). No support for "small body masses contributed to the unique survival of extant birds."
- Conclusion: "The origin of Aves is marked by larger, reshaped brains indicating selection for relatively large telencephala and eyes but not by uniquely small body size."

## Push-back and pairing

- One partial, crushed endocast; the brain-shape call rests on optic lobe position relative to the cerebrum's widest point.
- The body-mass result swings fourfold with the choice of outgroups, and the authors say the poorly placed fragments "likely ... severely bias inferred ancestral trait values." A clean example of sampling deciding an ancestral state; same lesson as the size trend in [Lee et al. 2014](lee-2014.md).
- Direct correction to [Balanoff et al. 2013](balanoff-2013.md), who reported a possible wulst imprint in *Archaeopteryx*.
- Pairs with [Bronzati et al. 2025](bronzati-2025.md): brain shape vs volume, and which features track flight.
