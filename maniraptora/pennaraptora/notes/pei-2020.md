# Pei et al. 2020, "Potential for powered flight neared by most close avialan relatives, but few crossed its thresholds"

Pei, Pittman, Goloboff, Dececchi, Habib, Kaye, Larsson, Norell, Brusatte & Xu. *Current Biology* 30:4033-4046 (2020). doi 10.1016/j.cub.2020.06.105. Open access.

File: `papers/PIIS0960982220309994.pdf`, 23 pages (main text pp1-14, STAR Methods after). `papers/PIIS0960982220309994 (1).pdf` is a byte-identical copy. Main text read in full, methods skimmed; all four figures viewed.

## Claim

- Two parts: a new tree, then flight measures mapped onto it.
- Tree: the Theropod Working Group (TWiG) coelurosaur matrix, updated with nine new dromaeosaurids (31 in total, including *Zhenyuanlong*), *Archaeopteryx* re-examined under laser-stimulated fluorescence, and an automated pipeline for "wildcards" (taxa that fit many places because data are missing; some dromaeosaurids are "missing over 90% of their scoring entries").
- Result: "dromaeosaurids + troodontids (Deinonychosauria) as the sister taxon to birds" and "Anchiornithinae as the earliest diverging birds." *Tianyuraptor* and *Zhenyuanlong* come out as the earliest microraptorines, implying "a decrease in body size and an increase in relative forelimb length across Microraptorinae."
- Support is low: "Group support ... was generally very low within Paraves, indicating that nodes are supported with relatively high levels of character conflict." In other datasets too, "Bremer supports are typically between 1 and 2 for most paravian nodes."
- Flight measures: wing loading (threshold ≤2.5 g/cm² in living flapping fliers) and specific lift (threshold 9.8 N/kg), each with a maximum and minimum estimate, reconstructed at ancestral nodes for 43 vane-feathered pennaraptorans. Three muscle-power settings.
- Assumptions stated: bird-like body density; maximum lift coefficient 1.6 (steady state, no unsteady tricks); no slotted wingtips; some anaerobic muscle power.
- Results: all avialans (including anchiornithines) and five dromaeosaurids (*Bambiraptor*, *Buitreraptor*, *Changyuraptor*, *Microraptor*, *Rahonavis*) pass wing loading. Fewer pass specific lift; *Microraptor* and *Rahonavis* are the strongest non-avialan candidates. "Powered flight potential evolved ≥3 times: once in birds and twice in dromaeosaurids."
- Near misses: "a broad range of these ancestors neared the wing loading and specific lift thresholds," suggesting "broad experimentation with wing-assisted locomotion before theropod flight evolved."
- Duration: success in some runs depended on anaerobic muscle; "low muscle endurance would necessitate short-ranged aerial behaviors." *Xiaotingia* "never achieves sufficient lift."

## Push-back

- The "≥3 origins" result can't be separated from "1 origin with losses" on tree length: with flight forced to arise once, "the trees obtained have the exact same length ... with flight originating only once at the Paraves, and becoming secondarily lost between 6 to 11 times." The authors prefer multiple origins; parsimony alone doesn't decide.
- The tree under the flight mapping has low support in exactly the region that matters (Paraves). If troodontids or anchiornithines move, the count of origins changes. This is the clearest example of one line of evidence (the tree) setting the result of another (ancestral flight potential).
- *Microraptor* drives the dromaeosaurid result, which is why they run three mass/wing-area versions (Allen 2013, Chatterjee & Templin 2007, Dyke 2013).
- Contrast with [Dececchi et al. 2016](dececchi-2016.md), a shared-author predecessor that measured flapping-based behaviors (WAIR, flap-running) rather than flight thresholds, and [Kiat & O'Connor 2024](kiat-oconnor-2024.md), who argue from feather shape that *Anchiornis* and kin were flightless.
