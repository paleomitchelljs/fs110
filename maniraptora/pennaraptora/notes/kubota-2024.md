# Kubota et al. 2024, "Early Cretaceous troodontine troodontid (Dinosauria: Theropoda) from the Ohyamashimo Formation of Japan reveals the early evolution of Troodontinae"

Kubota, Kobayashi & Ikeda. *Scientific Reports* 14:16392 (2024). doi 10.1038/s41598-024-66815-2.

File: `papers/s41598-024-66815-2.pdf`, 14 pages. Read in full; all five figures viewed. Supplementary figures not in the folder.

## Claim

- *Hypnovenator matsubaraetoheorum*, MNHAH D1033340, Ohyamashimo Fm., Tambasasayama, Hyogo, Japan; early-middle Albian (112.1-106.4 Ma). Name: "'hypno', refers to 'sleep' in ancient Greek," honoring amateurs Kaoru Matsubara and Takaharu Ohe. Japan's only confirmed troodontid.
- Found awkwardly: the first block (forelimb, knee) "was discovered in crushing rocks from the Ohyamashimo Formation during the construction of a public park" in 2010; the heel block came from the same locality in 2011. "despite several excavations, the theropod-bearing horizon has not been relocated."
- Material: postcranial only: arm, hand, partial legs and feet, gastralia, two caudals. Diagnosed by four autapomorphies of hand and foot bones.
- Placement: "the oldest and one of the most basal troodontines, forming a clade with Gobivenator mongoliensis." The matrix is 503 taxa x 700 characters; CI 0.072.
- Posture: "a loosely folded forelimb lateral to the gastralia, tightly folded ankles, and unbent pedal digits positioned under the gastralia, resembling the sleeping style of ... Mei and Sinornithoides." So "the sleeping posture of small-bodied maniraptorans is prevalent not only in environments with volcanic and eolian events or alluvial systems but also in fluvial systems."
- Hand claws: geometric morphometrics show claws I and III "functionally similar," whereas digit I dominates in non-troodontine troodontids: "digit III functioned as effectively as digit I in Hypnovenator."
- Feet: "the oldest troodontine with an asymmetrical arctometatarsus, extending the record by 35 million years." Metatarsal IV/II width ratio 250%. Mosaic of grasping joints (ginglymoid toes) and running features (asymmetrical arctometatarsus); compared to ostriches' two weight-bearing toes, convergently.

## Push-back

- The "sleeping posture" is read from two blocks recovered from construction rubble a year apart, with no head, neck or most of the tail, and a horizon that can't be found again. The folded limbs could have other taphonomic causes. Calling it the *Mei* posture is a stretch next to [Xu & Norell 2004](xu-norell-2004.md) and [Gao et al. 2012](gao-2012.md), which have the whole curled animal.
- The claw-function result rests on two claws from one individual (ungual II is fragmentary).
