# Pierossi et al. 2025, "Convergent evolution among non-carnivorous, desert-dwelling theropods as revealed by the dentary of the noasaurid *Berthasaura leopoldinae* (Cretaceous of Brazil)"

Pierossi, Delcourt, Leme, Casali, Martins, Manzig & Langer. *Palaeontology* 68(4):e70014 (2025). doi 10.1111/pala.70014.

File: `papers/Palaeontology - 2025 - Pierossi - Convergent evolution among non‐carnivorous  desert‐dwelling theropods as revealed by the.pdf`, 18 pages. Read in full; all six figures viewed.

## Why it's in this folder

*Berthasaura* is a noasaurid ceratosaur, not a pennaraptoran. The paper is here for its comparison with oviraptorids: toothless, deep lower jaws evolved twice, in desert theropods on different continents.

## Claim

- Specimen: MPCO.V 0121, a left dentary from the Cruzeiro do Oeste site, Caiuá Group, Early Cretaceous desert of south-central Brazil. Same site as the *Berthasaura* holotype, about 5% smaller.
- Lost teeth: CT shows pits on the biting edge leading into vertical canals with "no sign of any denser material (i.e. tooth remains)." Read as "alveolar vestiges," so "its teeth were lost during ontogeny, as previously reported for another noasaurid, Limusaurus." The specimen is placed at roughly *Limusaurus* stage III, younger than the holotype.
- Morphometrics: 2D landmarks on 65 maxillae and 52 dentaries (69 species), no landmarks on teeth so toothless jaws can be included. Time-scaled supertree, ancestral diet reconstruction, disparity and rate tests, convergence tests (Ct1-Ct4).
- Diet: carnivory ancestral, with at least two shifts away: once in Maniraptoriformes (with a return to carnivory in Paraves), once in noasaurids.
- Dentary: PC1 alone explains 55.2% of shape. "Oviraptorosaurs have such an aberrant dentary that its shape is statistically different from those of all other taxonomic groups." The only non-oviraptorosaur near them is *Berthasaura*.
- Convergence: *Berthasaura* and oviraptorids (*Citipati*, *Huanansaurus*, *Khaan*, *Banji*, *Yulong*) show "significant morphological convergence," both "dorsoventrally deep" with "a large mandibular fenestra." Also a maxilla convergence between the long-snouted tyrannosaurid *Qianzhousaurus* and troodontids.
- Explanation: "adaptations to feed on the tough parts of xerophytic plants, which are important food sources in desertic environments."

## Push-back

- The diet labels are borrowed, mostly from Zanno & Makovicky 2011; anything not covered there is "considered as 'carnivores'." Troodontid diet is coded carnivore though contested; see [Fan et al. 2026](fan-2026.md).
- The desert-plant explanation is offered without plant evidence from either site; the authors say xeric plant fossils are "relatively rare" and lean on *Classopollis* pollen elsewhere. Two desert faunas, one convergent pair: suggestive, not tested.
- They note the 2D side-view landmarks miss the oviraptorid symphysis, "a wider and deeper shelf that could affect jaw functional performance." The convergence is in profile only.
- *Berthasaura* is coded from a figure of the holotype, not the new specimen.
