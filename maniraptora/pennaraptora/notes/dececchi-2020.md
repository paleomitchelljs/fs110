# Dececchi et al. 2020, "Aerodynamics show membrane-winged theropods were a poor gliding dead-end"

Dececchi, Roy, Pittman, Kaye, Xu, Habib, Larsson, Wang & Zheng. *iScience* 23:101574 (2020). doi 10.1016/j.isci.2020.101574.

File: `papers/dececchi2020.pdf`, 35 pages (article ~12 pp, then methods, references, and supplementary figure on p23). Read the article in full; all six figures, the graphical abstract and supplementary Fig S1 viewed. Tests [Xu et al. 2015](xu-2015.md).

## Claim

- Re-examines *Yi qi* STM 31-2 with laser-stimulated fluorescence (LSF) and models *Yi* and *Ambopteryx* aerodynamically, against *Archaeopteryx* and *Microraptor*.
- The membrane: "Enhanced white light and LSF images do not reveal the parallel striations reported by Xu et al. (2015) but do image subparallel feather filaments running across them." The patches "show a negligible response to LSF." They keep the membranes anyway, given *Ambopteryx* and *Epidendrosaurus*, but "Our findings suggest a more restricted patagial extent than originally modeled."
- Styliform element: "although the left hand has been supinated 180° from its natural position and the ulna displaced, the styliform element remains articulated to the carpus ..., suggesting the styliform element was tightly integrated into the carpus."
- No powered flight: "Powered flight requires extensive pectoral musculoskeletal adaptations ... No evidence of these are present in scansoriopterygids." Deltopectoral crest 19% of humerus in *Yi*, 22% in *Ambopteryx*, vs 29% *Microraptor* and 34% *Archaeopteryx*.
- No ground launch: minimum take-off speeds "between 1.1 and 2.9 times the maximum possible sprint speed" for *Yi*; "2.3–3.9 times" for *Ambopteryx*. "In no case with or without flapping assistance was leaping sufficient to achieve launch."
- Gliding possible only with the bigger wing models: bat and pterosaur models fall "comfortably within the range seen in extant and extinct gliders"; maniraptoran and frog models "lie well outside known glider wing loadings." Yet the maniraptoran model is the one they say "should be prioritized" given the imaging. Their best guess is "a mixed bat and maniraptoran model."
- Poor gliders: "significantly higher glide speed, sinking speed, and circle radius ... than either Microraptor or Archaeopteryx ... gliding flight in scansoriopterygids would require higher speeds, higher launch points, and be less precise."
- Ecology: perhaps specialists for "quickly crossing small gaps in the canopy." "Scansoriopterygids therefore appear to have been a failed experimental lineage of early arboreal gliders unable to compete with the rapidly evolving mammalian gliders and avialan fliers of the time."
- Big picture: "at least two, but more likely three or more attempts at flight (both powered and gliding) by small pennaraptoran theropods during the Mesozoic."
- Limits: no wing cross-section known; assumes membrane control like bats and pterosaurs, which "may overestimate flight performance."

There is a tension worth showing students: the wing model they favor on anatomy (maniraptoran) is the one that fails as a glider; the models that pass (bat, pterosaur) are the ones the specimen least supports. "Poor glider" is the generous reading.

## Figures

The paper's figures are vector or high-resolution; quality is high throughout.

- **Graphical abstract (p1)**, three-line verdict (red X ground launching, red X WAIR, green check short-distance gliding) over a maniraptoran-model wing drawing and "Scansoriopterygid gliding was a DEAD-END." Too much like an advert for a lecture; the wing drawing alone could be cropped.
- **Fig 1 (p3)**, soft-tissue map of *Yi*: grey/blue skeleton with membrane patches M1-M5, claw sheath C1 and skull halo S1 in color-coded fills, legend below. Clear map of where the "membrane" is and how little there is. Crops well with the legend.
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, white light vs LSF pairs for patches M1-M3. Blue-purple LSF panels; striations faint. For experts.
- **Fig 3 (p5)**, M4 and M5 with red arrowheads marking feather filaments crossing the "membrane." Useful for the reinterpretation, but small and dark.
- **Fig 4 (p6)**, wrist in white light and LSF (orange/blue), with two colored line reconstructions of radiale angle. Busy.
- **Fig 5 (p8)**, four wing models (bat, maniraptoran, pterosaur, frog) on a *Yi* forelimb, with membrane, feathers and abduction angles in colored fills. Clean vector. Pairs with Xu 2015 ED Fig 7.
- **Fig 6 (p9)**, boxplots of wing loading: gliding fishes, fossil reptiles, reptiles, mammals, then *Yi*/*Ambopteryx* under four models, with black silhouettes and a green band for gliding mammals. Vector, large type, white background. The paper's best slide figure: the maniraptoran and frog boxes sit visibly above every living glider. Crops whole.
- **Fig S1 (p23)**, orange-fluorescing claw sheath under LSF. Striking color but a side point.
