# Li et al. 2010, "Plumage color patterns of an extinct dinosaur"

Li, Gao, Vinther, Shawkey, Clarke, D'Alba, Meng, Briggs & Prum. *Science* 327:1369-1372, 12 Mar 2010. doi 10.1126/science.1186290.

File: `papers/Li-et-al-2010-Science.pdf`, 5 pages (first page is the tail of an unrelated Titan paper). Read in full; all five figures viewed. SOM not in the folder.

## Claim

- *Anchiornis huxleyi*, BMNHC PH828, Late Jurassic, Daxishan, Jianchang County. Part and counterpart in three blocks. Placed "within Paraves as a part of Troodontidae" (the 2009 placement; later analyses move it).
- Method: 29 SEM samples across the body. "eumelanosomes that produce black and gray colors are long and narrow, whereas those that produce rufous red and brown colors are short and wide." Four melanosome variables in a discriminant analysis against modern feathers. "Twenty-four samples were assigned a color with >0.9 probability." Three ambiguous (0.56-0.74), three unpigmented.
- Limit stated: "Other molecular pigments, such as carotenoids and porphyrins, also produce plumage colors but are not preserved morphologically; thus, we cannot address their possible effects here."
- Result: "the body was gray and dark and the face had rufous speckles. The crown was rufous, and the long limb feathers were white with distal black spangles."
- The rufous crown rests on one sample: "the longest feathers from the center of the crest (sample 25) indicate a brown in close proximity to rufous."
- Evolution: within-feather patterning (spots, spangles) "is presently coincident with the origin of elongate pennaceous feather structure in the most recent common ancestor of Maniraptora." Among-feather variation optimized "as ancestral to at least Coelurosauria."
- Function: "the first evidence for plumage color patterns in a feathered nonavian dinosaur suggests that selection for signaling function may be important in the early evolution of feathers." Lists alternatives: sexual signaling, threat displays, flushing prey (redstarts, fantails), wear resistance in wingtips.

## Figures

All embedded at 150 ppi; small in pixels. Usable at half-slide, soft at full-slide.

- **Fig 1 (p3)**, specimen photo (A), a grey-silhouette explanatory drawing (B) with numbered red/blue sample dots, and six SEM panels (C-H) linked by yellow callout wedges. Busy. The specimen photo shows striking dark-tipped limb feathers; the SEMs show sausage-shaped melanosomes. Crop A alone, or one SEM (C or H) to show "what a melanosome looks like."
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, skull with crest, sample dots, four SEMs. Small and dark; the crest is barely visible. Skip.
- **Fig 3 (p4)**, discriminant-analysis scatter: extant black/brown/gray clusters as colored ellipses, fossil sample numbers overplotted. Clean but tiny type; axis labels "spherical ... cylindrical" help. Readable if zoomed; teaches how the color call is made.
- **Fig 4 (p4)**, DiGiorgio color plate: grey-black *Anchiornis* with red crest and black-spangled white wings and leg feathers. The iconic image. 713x392 px source; fine at half-slide. Tail reconstructed from a different specimen (caption says so).
- **Fig 5 (p5)**, cladogram of coelurosaurs with feather drawings, *Sinosauropteryx* to crown birds, marking where among-feather and within-feather color patterning appear. Greyscale, black line art, readable labels. Good summary figure; the node labels are small.
