# Voeten et al. 2018, "Wing bone geometry reveals active flight in *Archaeopteryx*"

Voeten, Cubo, de Margerie, Röper, Beyrand, Bureš, Tafforeau & Sanchez. *Nature Communications* 9:923 (2018). doi 10.1038/s41467-018-03296-8. Open access (downloaded from nature.com).

File: `papers/voeten2018.pdf`, 9 pages. Main text read in full, methods skimmed; both figures viewed.

## Claim

- Data: synchrotron cross-sections at mid-shaft of the humerus and ulna of three *Archaeopteryx* specimens (5th/Eichstätt, 7th/Munich, 9th/"Chicken Wing"), compared with humeri and ulnae from 69 archosaur species of known locomotion: birds with different flight styles, non-flying birds, crocodiles, dinosaurs (*Compsognathus*), and two pterosaurs.
- Logic: bone walls are shaped by the loads they carry (beam theory). Thin walls and high resistance to twisting mark flapping wings.
- Cortical thickness: "successfully discriminates between known non-volant and volant forms, and confidently indicates that Archaeopteryx was volant" (threshold ~0.60).
- Torsional resistance: the three specimens "unanimously ally with birds that incidentally employ flapping flight to evade predators or cross physical barriers" (pheasant-like burst flight), "through which we interpret that Archaeopteryx actively used its wings to take to the air."
- Not a modern stroke: *Archaeopteryx* lacks a keeled sternum, the supracoracoideus pulley and the shoulder range for raising the wing high. So it used "a more anterodorsally posteroventrally oriented flight stroke than used by modern birds," closer to the "grabbing" motion of maniraptorans.
- Consequence: "avian powered flight must have originated before the latest Jurassic."
- Side result: bone vascular density "in the range of modern birds," contrary to an earlier report of nearly avascular bone; differences between specimens read as age.

## Push-back and pairing

- Three specimens, with the 5th and 7th showing "brittle deformation ... splintering of the bone cortex"; the 9th is the best preserved. Cross-sections were reconstructed, with "supplemented fragments" in white.
- Bone geometry tells what loads the bones carried, not how; the authors use it to argue for flapping over gliding. Gliders with limb-supported membranes are a poor comparison for a bird wing, so the main contrast is among birds.
- Pairs with [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md) (all avialans pass wing loading) and [Dececchi et al. 2016](dececchi-2016.md) (can't reject WAIR in *Archaeopteryx*): three independent lines (bone shape, body proportions, behavior models) agree that *Archaeopteryx* could flap off the ground, briefly. Contrast with [Kiat & O'Connor 2024](kiat-oconnor-2024.md) on feathers, and [O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025-archaeopteryx.md) on the Chicago specimen.
