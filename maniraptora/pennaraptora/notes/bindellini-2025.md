# Bindellini et al. 2025, "*Velociraptor*: the state of the art 100 years after the discovery of this iconic dinosaur"

Bindellini, Moscarella, Makovicky, Manucci & Romano. *Italian Journal of Geosciences* 144(3):460-486 (2025). doi 10.3301/IJG.2025.22.

File: `papers/460.pdf`, 27 pages. Read in full; all ten figures viewed. Supplementary appendices (velociraptorine systematics, character diagnostics) not in the folder.

## What kind of paper

A centenary review: history of finds, taxonomy, a re-run of two published matrices, anatomy, feathers, diet, locomotion, metabolism, social behaviour, neurology, pathology, environment, palaeoart and pop culture. Ends with the authors' own life reconstruction.

## Claim

- Find: AMNH 6515, "a complete –albeit laterally crushed– skull" and a hand claw, collected 11 Aug 1923 at Flaming Cliffs by Peter Kaisen. Named by Osborn 1924: "velox" ('fast') and "raptor" ('predaceous' or 'plunderer').
- Taxonomy is unsettled: "A notable gap in our current understanding of Velociraptor is the absence of an up-to-date, comprehensive diagnosis." One matrix (Czepiński 2023) finds a single autapomorphy, denticle size; another (Wang et al. 2022) finds 12, one miscored. "'Velociraptor' osmolskae" should get a new genus. MPC-D 100/982 is a third, unnamed species ("V. vadarostrum" is a thesis name, "a nomen nudum").
- Postcranium has no autapomorphies: "no autapomorphies have been identified in the literature, nor do any postcranial traits optimise as such."
- Size: "up to two metres and stood around 1 m tall at the hip"; "closer in size to a wild turkey."
- Feathers: no *Velociraptor* specimen preserves them. The quill-knob ulna (MPC-D 100/981, Turner et al. 2007) is "insufficient to support referral to this genus"; treat as "Velociraptorinae indet. or ?Velociraptor." Feathers are inferred from relatives: wing-like arrangements in *Zhenyuanlong* and *Microraptor* render it "parsimonious that they were also present in Velociraptor."
- Sickle claw: grip and pin, not slash. Bishop 2019: claw forces "too weak to slash the soft tissue of prey."
- Diet: "an opportunistic carnivore with a broad dietary range, engaging in both predation and scavenging." Evidence: Fighting Dinosaurs (MPC-D 100/25), *Protoceratops* bones with bite marks, a pterosaur bone in the gut of MPC-D 100/54. Long shallow maxilla suggests "preying on animals much smaller than itself."
- Pack hunting: "direct evidence ... remains elusive." *Deinonychus* tooth isotopes show juveniles and adults ate differently, "a distinct behaviour from cooperative social mammals."
- Metabolism: nasal-cavity proxy puts it between endotherm and ectotherm lines, without confidence intervals; other proxies point higher. Expect "a 'mosaic' of adaptations."
- Neurology: partial endocast MPC-D 100/976. Big flocculi; hearing centered ~2,368 Hz, upper limit ~3,965 Hz, like a raven.
- Pathology: MPC-D 100/976 has unhealed tooth punctures on the frontals from a same-size individual, probably fatal.
- Environment: Djadokhta dunes, semi-arid, sandstorm burials.
- Pop culture: Crichton's raptors are *Deinonychus*-sized, from Paul's 1988 lumping of the two genera. "For over 20 years, no palaeoartist has depicted this animal with partial feathering, in line with fossil evidence" (sic; context implies the opposite; read the passage before quoting).

Inconsistencies: the concluding remarks cite "an enlarged olfactory bulb," but the only endocast is a hindbrain-only braincase; the body text gives no forebrain data. The header reads "Submitted: 15 November 2025 Accepted: 08 July 2025."

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, holotype skull AMNH 6515 in both sides (a, b), hand claw (c), and Osborn's 1924 drawings (d). Sharp tan bone on white. (a) is the classic skull photo; Osborn's drawing (d) is a good "how it was first published" image.
- **Fig 2 (p6)**, MPC-D 100/982 skeleton, both sides, with 10 cm bar. A tangled, articulated skeleton on white; impressive, hard to read.
- **Fig 3 (p7)**, the quill-knob ulna (after Turner et al. 2007) with close-up and line drawing showing six knobs. Clear; the key "feather" evidence figure. Note the specimen's genus referral is disputed.
- **Fig 4 (p7)**, colored skeletal reconstruction showing which bones come from AMNH 6515, MPC-D 100/982 and 100/985, with a 50 cm bar. Clean and useful for a "composite skeleton" point (see [skeleton-figures-name-specimens] memory: this one names its specimens).
- **Fig 5 (p9)**, two majority-rule trees side by side, clades shaded in pastel blocks, silhouettes. Readable at slide size only when cropped to one tree; shows that the two matrices disagree.
- **Fig 6 (p10)**, the stiff-tailed MPC-D 100/986 tail, dorsolateral. Tall narrow image; striking for the ossified rods.
- **Fig 7 (p12)**, the furcula (after Norell et al. 1997), greyscale photo and drawing. Small; historically important.
- **Fig 8 (p19)**, timeline of *Velociraptor* art: Caselli 1975, Burian 1975, Stout 1981, Paul 1988, Yamamoto 2000, Wierum 2017, as greyscale drawings along a dated line. Good teaching figure for how depictions changed; clean.
- **Fig 9 (p20)**, Raul Martin's Fighting Dinosaurs painting (from Chiappe 2003). Strong image; greyscale reproduction in this PDF.
- **Fig 10 (p21)**, photogrammetric cast skeleton beside the authors' feathered head and body reconstruction (rufous-brown, dark mask). Photographic quality; crops well as a modern-look slide.
