# Persons et al. 2014, "Oviraptorosaur tail forms and functions"

Persons, Currie & Norell. *Acta Palaeontologica Polonica* 59(3):553-567 (2014; online Jan 2013). doi 10.4202/app.2012.0093.

File: `papers/app.2012.0093.pdf`, 16 pages (BioOne cover page first). Read in full; all eleven figures viewed.

## Claim

- Oviraptorosaur tails are short but built differently from bird tails: "posteriorly persistent and exceptionally wide transverse processes, anteroposteriorly short centra, and a high degree of flexibility across the pre-pygostyle vertebral series."
- Digital muscle models (*Ajancingenia*, *Khaan*, *Nomingia*): "none of the three modeled oviraptorosaurs had a M. caudofemoralis that was exceptionally small compared to other non-avian theropods." Tails were "short but stocky."
- Pygostyles: a second *Nomingia* specimen "confirms that the fused terminal vertebrae of the type specimen were not an abnormality"; pygostyles also in *Citipati* and *Conchoraptor*.
- Against secondary flightlessness: birds lost tail muscle as flight took over; oviraptorosaurs kept it. "This is not consistent with the morphology predicted by the secondarily flightless hypotheses." "there is no reason to suspect that most oviraptorosaurs diverged from the standard femoral-retraction-dominated locomotor style, or to evoke a flying ancestor." The bird-like features "appear to be convergences." *Caudipteryx* is "a partial exception."
- Swimming rejected: short tail, low spines, no lateral compression, arid habitats.
- Display: "the tails of oviraptorosaurs were also highly specialized to serve as visual display structures." Feather fans in *Caudipteryx* and *Similicaudipteryx* plus pygostyles in advanced forms imply fans "throughout the group." Big tail-moving muscles could swing and twist the fan "with a degree of muscular dexterity beyond that of most other theropods." Prediction: "the caudal morphology of oviraptorosaurs may prove to be sexually dimorphic," untestable for now.
- Closing: "combined with the prevalence of cranial crests among the oviraptorosaurs, suggests the group had a propensity for visual exhibition."

A direct counter to [Sorkin 2020](sorkin-2020.md)'s "flightless oviraptorosaurs from flying ancestors." The display reading rests on inference from muscle size plus fans known only in two early taxa.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p3)**, two simplified cladograms: oviraptorosaurs inside Avialae vs sister to Paraves. Clean text trees; good for setting up the debate.
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, outline drawings of an *Ajancingenia* caudal vertebra in several views. Line art, clear but technical.
- **Figs 3-5 (p6)**, photos of the new pygostyles (*Nomingia*, *Citipati*, *Conchoraptor*). Tan bone on dark; small. The pygostyle is visible; fine for "they had a pygostyle."
- **Fig 6 (p7)**, cross-section diagram of a theropod tail with muscles color-coded (caudofemoralis red, longissimus, ilio-ischiocaudalis). Clean, colored; a good teaching diagram for tail muscles.
- **Fig 7 (p8)**, digital models of *Khaan* tail: skeleton (grey), caudofemoralis (red), full muscle (red/pink), lateral and dorsal. Clear stepwise build.
- **Figs 8-9 (p10)**, same models for *Ajancingenia* and *Nomingia*. Repetitive.
- **Fig 10 (p12)**, tail and pelvic skeletons of *Anchiornis*, *Archaeopteryx*, *Jeholornis*, *Confuciusornis*, *Khaan* as line drawings. Clean comparison showing different routes to a short tail.
- **Fig 11 (p14)**, Sydney Mohr painting: a male *Ajancingenia* fanning a tail of black, white and orange feathers at a female among cycads. Attractive life image; label it as the hypothesis it illustrates.
