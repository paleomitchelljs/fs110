# Li et al. 2008, "Behavioral and faunal implications of Early Cretaceous deinonychosaur trackways from China"

Li, Lockley, Makovicky, Matsukawa, Norell, Harris & Liu. *Naturwissenschaften* (online 2007, print 2008; volume and pages not on this copy). doi 10.1007/s00114-007-0310-7.

File: `papers/Lietal2007Naturwissenschaften.pdf`, 7 pages (online-first copy). Read in full; supplement not in the folder.

## Claim

- Tianjialou Fm. (Barremian-Aptian), Junan, Shandong. New ichnotaxon *Dromaeopodus shandongensis* (foot length 26-28.5 cm), plus a smaller *Velociraptorichnus* trackway (~10 cm). Ichnofamily Dromaeopodidae.
- Trackmaker: two-toed prints (digit II held up), digits III and IV subequal and parallel. "Three features ... consistent with the anatomy of articulated deinonychosaur foot fossils to the exclusion of those of any other bipedal Mesozoic taxon." Size points to a large dromaeosaurid, "comparable to *Achillobator*."
- Claw: tracks "provide incontrovertible evidence that the large digit II claw was consistently held in a retracted position."
- Group: "Trackways 1–6 occur together on a single bedding plane"; "all are closely spaced and indicate an identical direction of travel." "The tracks do not overlap, footprint size is almost uniform between trackways, and 'intertrackway' spacing ... is regular and less than a single stride length." No mud cracks, so "emplaced within a very short time span." Conclusion: "a group of six individuals moving together as a coherent group."
- Their alternative: a shoreline or channel can line animals up ("physically controlled"); other dinosaurs on the next bed walk parallel but opposite. They answer that the even spacing holds "regardless of possible physiographic 'control.'"
- Against Roach & Brinkman 2007 (Komodo-style solitary mobbing): earlier evidence for *Deinonychus* groups was "four partial, largely disarticulated skeletons" at a *Tenontosaurus* and "could also result from distinct, time-averaged death or scavenging events." The tracks show "nonagonistic group behaviors ... not limited to random, agonistic encounters."
- Their own limits: "Whether such an aggregation ... represents a common or rare (or perhaps seasonal) event cannot be determined from a single find." "Whether this behavior was specifically hunting cannot be ascertained." All walking, not running.

Tracks are the only line in the deck that records live animals moving at the same moment, which is what bonebeds and the *Deinonychus*-*Tenontosaurus* site can't do. The weak points: one bedding surface; the maker is inferred from foot shape and size, not a body fossil; and group travel is not pack hunting.

Pairs with [Frederickson et al. 2020](frederickson-2020.md), [Funston et al. 2016](funston-2016.md), [Tsukiji et al. 2021](tsukiji-2021.md).
