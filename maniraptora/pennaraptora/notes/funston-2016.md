# Funston et al. 2016, "The first oviraptorosaur (Dinosauria: Theropoda) bonebed: evidence of gregarious behaviour in a maniraptoran theropod"

Funston, Currie, Eberth, Ryan, Chinzorig, Badamgarav & Longrich. *Scientific Reports* 6:35782, 21 Oct 2016. doi 10.1038/srep35782.

File: `papers/srep35782.pdf`, 13 pages. Read in full; all eight figures viewed.

## Claim

- An *Avimimus* bonebed, Nemegt Fm. (early Maastrichtian), Mongolia, "the first oviraptorosaur bonebed described and the only recorded maniraptoran bonebed from the Late Cretaceous." Found in 2006 already dug into by poachers; excavated 2006-2016.
- *Avimimus* is 160 of 177 accessioned specimens (90.4%). Others: oviraptorid bits, embryonic hadrosaur bones, a bird, lizard, mammal, eggshell.
- MNI 18 from tibiae, "the largest monodominant assemblage of maniraptorans yet reported."
- Ages: adults by fused tibiotarsus. "All tibiotarsi longer than 246 mm (n = 17) are fused, and none shorter than 246 mm are fused." Fused tibiotarsi vary "by less than 10% (246 mm – 280 mm)." Only one tibia (~202 mm) is below 80% of the largest. Small bones of other animals are present, so "the dearth of juvenile Avimimus is real, rather than the result of winnowing."
- Taphonomy: bones unweathered, no tooth or insect marks, hydraulically sorted, oriented NE-SW with the channel. Reading: a mass death, rapid burial, then reworking into a river channel. Hindlimb overrepresentation "may point to a miring event," but "the cause of death is ambiguous."
- Behaviour: herbivore-like jaws, so not pack hunting. Too many adults for a family. Possibilities: synchronized breeding with fast growth; age-segregated groups; lekking ("consistent with, but not indicative of"); flocking or communal roosting. "the specific behaviour that the Avimimus death assemblage represents cannot be assessed."
- New anatomy: premaxilla and nasals (short snout with vertical front), a "neurocranial unit" fusing more bones "than that of any bird," edentulous dentaries.
- Wider claim: "a potential trend of increasing gregariousness in dinosaurs towards the end of the Mesozoic," adding oviraptorosaurs to hadrosaurs, ceratopsids and others.

The age structure rests on size and fusion, with no histology ("this assertion cannot be tested"). The authors are careful about what behaviour the bed shows.
