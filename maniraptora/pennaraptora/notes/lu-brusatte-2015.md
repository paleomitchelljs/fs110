# Lü & Brusatte 2015, "A large, short-armed, winged dromaeosaurid (Dinosauria: Theropoda) from the Early Cretaceous of China and its implications for feather evolution"

Lü & Brusatte. *Scientific Reports* 5:11775, 16 July 2015. doi 10.1038/srep11775.

File: `papers/10.1038@srep11775.pdf`, 11 pages. Read in full; all five figures viewed. Pairs with [Zheng et al. 2010](zheng-2010.md) (*Tianyuraptor*).

## Claim

- *Zhenyuanlong suni*, JPM-0008, Yixian Fm., Sihedang, Liaoning. One slab in three pieces. Sub-adult. ~126.6 cm as preserved, "closer to 165 cm" with the missing half of the tail. Femur 193.4 mm.
- Forelimb:hindlimb 0.48, "the shortest of any Liaoning dromaeosaurid"; *Tianyuraptor* 0.53; "more than 0.80 in every other Liaoning dromaeosaurid specimen." Humerus:femur 0.626.
- They concede the difference from *Tianyuraptor* is weak: the limb-ratio difference "is within the range of intraspecific variation in two paravian taxa known from multiple specimens, Microraptor and Archaeopteryx."
- The headline: "Although the arms of Zhenyuanlong are short, they supported large and complex wings comprised of pennaceous coverts, primaries, and secondaries, some of which are asymmetric." Right wing ~800 cm² preserved, left 1120 cm². "approximately 10 primaries and 20 secondaries." Remiges "more than twice the length of the humerus."
- Coverts short "as in modern birds, not elongate and covering much of the wing as has been hypothesized for ... Archaeopteryx and ... Anchiornis."
- No leg feathers, with a taphonomic caveat: "the hindlimbs are part of the second major piece of the slab and no feathers are visible on this piece ... it cannot be totally ruled out that hindlimb feathers were present in life."
- Three readings of the wings. They reject volancy as "unlikely" and favor retention: "the larger and short-armed Zhenyuanlong evolved from more volant ancestors and maintained ... the integument through the inertia of common descent or for other selective reasons." Display is floated: "such large wings ... were useful for display purposes, and possibly even evolved for this reason and not for flight."
- Phylogeny: Liaoning dromaeosaurids collapse into a polytomy with Dromaeosaurinae + Velociraptorinae. "Liaoning dromaeosaurids may simply be a grade ... and short arms may have evolved multiple times in this grade." All dromaeosaurid clades "Bremer supports of 1 and jackknife supports of less than 50%."
- Warning about proxies: "we should be cautious in using only one or a few of these feathered paravians as a proxy for the ancestral morphological, functional, and behavioural conditions of birds ... it is unclear whether Microraptor alone can give much insight into the origin of flight."

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, whole holotype on a pale slab. Excellent photo: dark skeleton on cream matrix, head-to-tail in one frame, feather halo visible on arms and tail at slide size. The best single image in the paper; works full-slide as is. Small scale bar and label at bottom; crop to just the animal if wanted.
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, skull photo (A) and line drawing (B). Photo is dark and compressed; the drawing is clean and labeled. Fine for anatomy, not a lecture showpiece.
- **Fig 3 (p5)**, five postcranial close-ups with scale cards in frame (forearm, hindlimbs, thorax, pelvis, girdles). No labels, cluttered with colored scale cards. Weak for slides; A (the short forearm) is the only one worth cropping.
- **Fig 4 (p7)**, integument: small overview (A) with grey highlights and arrows to tail (B), left arm (C), right arm (D), covert close-up (E). The wing feathers are faint; B and E show feathers best. Composite layout with arrows is busy; crop E or B singly.
- **Fig 5 (p8)**, reduced-consensus tree, bold italic names, brace marking "Liaoning dromaeosaurids," two silhouettes. Clean and large-type; reads well on a slide. Note it shows the polytomy that is the paper's point.
