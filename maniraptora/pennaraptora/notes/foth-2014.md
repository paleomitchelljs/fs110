# Foth, Tischlinger & Rauhut 2014, "New specimen of *Archaeopteryx* provides insights into the evolution of pennaceous feathers"

Foth, Tischlinger & Rauhut. *Nature* 511:79-82 (2014). doi 10.1038/nature13467.

File: `papers/nature13467.pdf`, 12 pages (letter plus extended data). Read the letter in full; extended data viewed.

## Claim

- The 11th skeletal *Archaeopteryx*: body, neck, leg and tail feathers preserved, mostly as imprints. Skull incomplete, left wing missing.
- Plumage: pennaceous body feathers to the head; long symmetrical feathers on the tibiotarsus ("trousers," 40-45 mm, 51-58% of tibiotarsus length), short ones on the tarsometatarsus. 12 primaries with coverts half their length, "as in extant birds," against a Berlin-based reading of few primaries and long coverts. Robust rachises, against rachis-diameter arguments for weak flight. Lateral tail feathers asymmetrical.
- Mapping feather types on their tree (troodontids sister to Avialae; *Anchiornis*, *Xiaotingia*, *Eosinopteryx*, *Pedopenna* just outside): pennaceous body feathers, remiges and rectrices go back to Pennaraptora. Asymmetrical remiges arose separately in *Microraptor* and in *Archaeopteryx* + Pygostylia. Long metatarsal feathers "evolved three times independently."
- Against a gliding start for leg feathers: "an initial aerodynamic function, as previously hypothesized, can be rejected because the presence of this character is extremely variable within Paraves," and they are mostly symmetrical and on the tibia. Only *Microraptor*'s look aerodynamic. "These results contradict the hypothesis that the flapping flight of modern birds was preceded by a four-winged gliding stage."
- Conclusion: "it seems plausible that the evolution of this feather type (especially in the wing, hindlimbs and tail) was primarily driven by display functions." Pennaceous feathers were "an exaptation that was repeatedly, and probably convergently, recruited for aerodynamic functions."
- Their hedge: the "true" origin of pennaceous body feathers may be deeper, since basal coelurosaur body feathers are poorly preserved.

The argument is from variability across a tree: a trait that comes and goes is read as not under flight selection. That depends on the tree (theirs puts troodontids next to birds) and on absences being real, not preservational, the point Xu & Barrett make about *Psittacosaurus*. "Display" is what remains once insulation and flight are set aside; there is no direct test of it here.
