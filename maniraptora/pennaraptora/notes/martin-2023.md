# Martin et al. 2023, "Variability of bone microstructure and growth lines in the evolution of troodontids and dromaeosaurids"

Martin, Currie & Kundrát. *Acta Zoologica* 105:135-175 (2024; online 2023). doi 10.1111/azo.12467.

File: `papers/Acta Zoologica - 2023 - Martin - Variability of bone microstructure and growth lines in the evolution of troodontids and.pdf`, 41 pages. Read the introduction, methods, the growth-analysis section and discussion in full; skimmed the per-taxon histology descriptions; all thirteen figures viewed.

## What kind of paper

A review of every published deinonychosaur (and *Archaeopteryx*) limb-bone histology: nine troodontids (26 samples), plus dromaeosaurids. It redraws each published section as a color-coded schematic and computes "relative growth rates" from zone thickness divided by bone length.

## Claim

- Coverage: "Around 35% of troodontid species ... and 30% of dromaeosaurid species ... have been subject to osteohistological studies of the limb bones."
- Terminology problem: authors use "subadult" (somatic) and "sexually mature" interchangeably for the same tissue. Proposed definitions: no notable change across zones = juvenile; "first notable changes" in tissue, zone thickness, vascularity or fibers = sexually mature, with stacked peripheral LAGs = subadult; a full OCL of tightly packed LAGs = adult.
- Reclassifications under those rules: *Wulong*, *Anchiornis* IVPP V14378, *Buitreraptor* (three specimens), one *Deinonychus* and one *Troodon* are juveniles. *Talos*, *Balaur*, *Sinornithosaurus* "haoiana", *Halszkaraptor*, *Daliansaurus*, *Liaoningvenator* and others are subadults. Only *Changyuraptor*, two *Deinonychus*, *Mei long* and two *Troodon* count as adults. They reject *Balaur* being "fully grown individuals (contra Brusatte et al., 2013)."
- Reinterpretations: *Mei long* fibula has an OCL; "poorly vascularized fibrolamellar bone" in *Philovenator* is "an oxymoron."
- Four headline conclusions: (1) "troodontids evolved general osteohistology closer to basal avialans than to dromaeosaurids"; (2) reticular (fast-growth) vasculature tracks maturation timing in troodontids; (3) "the first growth slowdown occurred later (in more seasons) in small deinonychosaurs (e.g. Changyuraptor, Sinornithosaurus) than in bigger forms (e.g. Buitreraptor and Talos)"; (4) locomotion affected leg growth, "semi-aquatic species growing faster than pure terrestrial ones" (*Halszkaraptor*).
- Small vs large: "small forms of deinonychosaurs had a higher growth rate and shorter somatic maturation period than bigger forms," with exceptions (*Daliansaurus*, *Talos*).

## Push-back

- Conclusion (3) conflicts with the discussion two pages earlier, which says "the transition between juvenile and adult osteohistology occurred sooner (in fewer seasons) in potential gliders and flyers (Changyuraptor, Sinornithosaurus, and Archaeopteryx) than in runners (e.g. Liaoningvenator, Talos or Troodon)." The same taxa are said to slow later and mature sooner; check which is meant before quoting.
- The "relative growth rate" divides zone thickness by bone length across different elements, taxa and ontogenetic stages, often one sample per taxon; the locomotion claim (4) rests on a single *Halszkaraptor* tibia.
- Conclusion (1) leans on *Anchiornis*, whose troodontid status they themselves call "not yet resolved."

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p6)**, world map with sampled taxa as colored dots by clade. Simple context.
- **Fig 2 (p9)**, generalized long-bone cross-section, color-coded by tissue (FLB, PFB, lamellar, ICL, OCL) with vascular patterns (longitudinal, circumferential, reticular, radial, oblique) labeled. The best teaching figure: a one-slide glossary of bone histology. Crops whole.
- **Figs 3-7 (pp11-19)**, schematic redrawings of every published section in a common color code (green FLB, yellow PFB, purple/blue cancellous, red OCL, grey cracks), troodontids then dromaeosaurids. The value is consistency: sections from different papers become comparable. Individually small; pick one or two for a slide.
- **Figs 8-9 (pp26-28)**, cumulative relative growth vs years, one line per bone or taxon, LAGs as vertical lines. Busy multi-line Excel-style charts; hard to read.
- **Fig 10 (p32)**, before/after schematics reinterpreting *Mei* fibula and *Philovenator* femur. Clear for the reinterpretation point only.
- **Fig 11 (p35)**, number of LAGs vs body length for ulnae and tibiae, a handful of points. Sparse.
- **Fig 12 (p36)**, schematic linking locomotion (gliders, ground-dwellers, semi-aquatic) to relative growth and LAG counts. Diagrammatic; rests on few data.
- **Fig 13 (p37)**, flow diagram of the proposed evolution of deinonychosaur bone tissues. Text boxes; dense.
