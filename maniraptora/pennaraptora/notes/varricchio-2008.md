# Varricchio et al. 2008, "Avian paternal care had dinosaur origin"

Varricchio, Moore, Erickson, Norell, Jackson & Borkowski. *Science* 322:1826-1828 (2008). doi 10.1126/science.1163245. Erratum 7 August 2009 (in the file).

File: `papers/varricchio2008.pdf`, 4 pages (report plus erratum page). Read in full; supplement not in the folder.

## Claim

- Question: who cared for the eggs in *Troodon*, *Oviraptor* and *Citipati*? Two lines: clutch volume against adult body mass, and the bone histology of adults found on clutches.
- Clutches: "Complete egg clutches for *Troodon* and the oviraptorids contain 22 to 30 large eggs." "Total clutch volumes far exceed those of extant crocodilians, more closely matching those of polygamous ratites with similar adult size."
- Regressions on "433 extant archosaurs" in four groups: crocodile-maternal, bird-maternal, bird-biparental, bird-paternal. "No dinosaurs were included in the fitting of any regression lines." The three dinosaurs fit bird-paternal best.
- Numbers, as corrected in the 2009 erratum ("Erroneous Akaike information criterion values were calculated using base 10 rather than the natural logarithm"): Akaike weights "bird-paternal, 0.698; bird-maternal, 0.174; crocodile-maternal, 0.093; and bird-biparental, 0.036," so the dinosaurs are "4.0, 7.5, and 19.5 times more likely" to fit paternal. The erratum: "These corrected values do not alter but instead lend greater support to the original interpretation."
- Histology: *Troodon* MOR 748 (femur, tibia, metatarsal) and *Citipati* IGM 100/979 (femur): no medullary bone, no resorption cavities. "A variety of additional elements from six other brooding maniraptoran adults ... similarly display no reproductive tissues." So the adults were "males or postreproductive or nonreproductive females." Their own phrasing of the strength: histology "provides no evidence to falsify paternal care."
- Conclusion: "Paternal care in both troodontids and oviraptorids indicates that this care system evolved before the emergence of birds and represents birds' ancestral condition." Possibly "within a polygamous mating system" (communal nests).

The argument has three legs: n = 3 dinosaur points on a regression built from living birds; absence of a tissue (medullary bone) that Jin 2020 later found missing in a female with eggs inside; and the analogy to ratites. Birchard et al. 2013 reran the scaling with 1254 species and could not separate maternal from paternal care. Students should see that the clutch-volume line was challenged on its comparative data, not on the fossils.
