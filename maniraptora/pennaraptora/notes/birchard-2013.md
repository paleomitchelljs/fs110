# Birchard, Ruta & Deeming 2013, "Evolution of parental incubation behaviour in dinosaurs cannot be inferred from clutch mass in birds"

Birchard, Ruta & Deeming. *Biology Letters* 9:20130036 (2013). doi 10.1098/rsbl.2013.0036.

File: `papers/rsbl20130036.pdf`, 5 pages. Read in full; supplement not in the folder.

## Claim

- Target: Varricchio et al. 2008, which "failed to account for factors known to affect egg and clutch size in living bird species." Also notes the method was already being reused to sex the parent of *Massospondylus*.
- Two assumptions they name in Varricchio's logic: "phylogenetic relatedness and developmental maturity do not introduce any major bias."
- Data: clutch mass and female body mass for 1254 bird species, 28 orders, "more than three times the number of species used previously." Differences from Varricchio's data: ostrich coded biparental, not paternal; megapodes dropped (mound eggs laid over a season are not one clutch); average rather than maximum clutch size (maximums inflated by brood parasitism); dinosaur egg volume converted to mass.
- Result 1: "Maternal and paternal parental care types show the same relationship between CM and FBM." The dinosaurs fall in that shared band.
- Result 2: developmental maturity matters: precocial species lay heavier clutches than altricial. "Data for theropod dinosaurs were within the prediction interval for precocial bird species but outside that for bi-parental species."
- Result 3: within the one order with all three care types (Charadriiformes), "species with paternal care had smaller clutch masses than other care types," the opposite of Varricchio's pattern. Ratites and paternal care are "basically one and the same," so the paternal line is a ratite line.
- Conclusion: "for avian CM, developmental maturity and phylogeny are the dominant effects and the effect of parental care is quite small or non-existent." "scaling of clutch mass or volume against body mass does not represent a viable technique for inferring the type of parental care in birds or dinosaurs. Our data support the hypothesis of precocial young."
- Ancestral care for living birds by parsimony and likelihood: biparental (likelihood 0.55 vs paternal 0.19).
- Their own hedge on the precocial result: cautious about larger dinosaurs, since birds never reached their sizes.

Same fossils, same kind of plot, more and differently coded birds: the inference flips from "paternal" to "can't tell; precocial." The cleanest pair in the deck for showing that a comparative line is only as good as its comparison set.

Pairs with [Varricchio et al. 2008](varricchio-2008.md).
