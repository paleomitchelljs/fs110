# Xu & Barrett 2025, "The origin and early evolution of feathers: implications, uncertainties and future prospects"

Xu & Barrett. *Biology Letters* 21:20240517 (2025). doi 10.1098/rsbl.2024.0517. Open access (CC BY). Review.

File: `papers/rsbl.2024.0517.pdf`, 9 pages. Read in full.

## Claim

- Consensus sequence in theropods: monofilaments (*Sciurumimus*, *Yutyrannus*, *Beipiaosaurus*), branched (*Dilong*, *Sinosauropteryx*), then "Pennaceous feathers with symmetrical vanes and asymmetrical vanes appeared at the bases of Pennaraptora and Paraves, respectively." Melanosome diversity rises "at the base of Pennaraptora."
- Flight "might have appeared around the cladogenesis of Pennaraptora," with long arms, brain expansion and higher metabolism at the same node.
- Open question: "it remains unknown if 'true' feathers originated at the base of Avemetatarsalia or within Theropoda." Avemetatarsalian origin: "as early as 245 Ma," needs many losses. Theropod origin: Early Jurassic, needs filaments in pterosaurs and ornithischians (*Tianyulong*, *Kulindadromeus*, *Psittacosaurus*) to be independent.
- Preservation: *Psittacosaurus* "is represented by hundreds of well-preserved specimens, but only one of these preserves monofilamentous integumentary appendages." No integument data for early theropods, sauropodomorphs, lagerpetids, silesaurids.
- Molecular claims hedged: low feather-keratin in *Anchiornis* and *Shuvuuia*, "but it has been questioned whether immunohistochemical methods are applicable."
- Definitions: a follicle is the test for a true feather (turkey "beards" fail it), and no fossil filament has a known follicle.

Useful as the feathers-section opener: the stages are agreed; where they start is not, and the deciding data are mostly absences, which is what the *Psittacosaurus* count shows.
