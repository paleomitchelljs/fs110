# Fan et al. 2026, "Diet of bird-like troodontid dinosaurs: synthesis of a contentious clade"

Fan, Miller & Pittman. *Biological Reviews* 101:1760-1778 (2026; online 13 Feb 2026). doi 10.1002/brv.70145.

File: `papers/Biological Reviews - 2026 - Fan - Diet of bird‐like troodontid dinosaurs  synthesis of a contentious clade.pdf`, 19 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed.

## What kind of paper

A literature review, organized by line of evidence: gut contents and pellets, microwear, shed teeth, digging traces, tooth form and microstructure, skull and jaw, hind limb, finite-element models, stable isotopes. Ends with a summary flowchart (Fig 7).

## Claim

- Overall: "Environmental, morphological, and biogeochemical analyses suggest that the iconic North American troodontid, Troodon, was likely to have been omnivorous, potentially preying on both plants and small animals, such as mammals and baby dinosaurs."
- *Troodon* taxonomy: they keep the name but treat it as "a broad designation for North American troodontids"; "it is plausible that Troodon could be considered a wastebasket taxon."
- Direct evidence is thin and they say so. *Jinfengopteryx* gut contents ("eggs, developing ovarian follicles, seeds, or nuts") are "a single data point." Mammal-bearing pellets at Egg Mountain lack any tie to *Troodon* bodies: "the troodontid origin of these suspected gastric pellets cannot be confirmed." Summary: "dubious evidence for ingestion of both plant and animal material."
- Microwear (*Troodon inequalis*): "puncture-and-pull" scratches like other theropods, few pits, so "a diet of relatively soft foods."
- Shed teeth with baby hadrosaurs at Horseshoe Canyon sites: troodontid teeth over half the theropod teeth at L2000. Their alternative: "baby hadrosaurs and Troodon may have shared a food source," e.g. soft plants at nesting grounds.
- Serration loss evolved repeatedly (*Mei*, *Byronosaurus*, *Xixiasaurus*, *Urbacodon*, *Jinfengopteryx*). New proposal: unserrated teeth crack less, so "the loss of denticles may allow the teeth to crack less, last longer, and reduce overall turnover rate." "this mechanism of serration loss in troodontids has not been proposed before."
- Skull: weaker, small-prey skulls than dromaeosaurids; strong anterior jaw for torsion, uses debated.
- Foot: troodontid sickle claw less hinged, metatarsus cursorial; best analogue the red-legged seriema, which pins prey with its digit-II claw. "we consider seriema-like low-force pinning behaviour more likely in troodontids."
- Isotopes (Cullen & Cousens 2024, Oldman Fm., N = 5): troodontids fall "between primary and secondary consumers"; "omnivory being a true signal rather than a compromise of noise."
- Analogues: coyote (up to 40% plants), maned wolf, sun bear, civet, badger, emu, junglefowl.
- Ancestral diet: "Deinonychosaurians and paravians appear to have been ancestrally omnivorous"; early deinonychosaurs probably "animal-dominated omnivory," with troodontids drifting plantward and dromaeosaurids toward hypercarnivory.
- "Troodontids were almost certainly less predatory than their close relatives the dromaeosaurids."

## Push-back

- The conclusions call *Troodon* "most likely a plant-dominated omnivore," but the decisive evidence for the plant side is five isotope samples from one site, and the body text leans on "omnivore" without "plant-dominated." The word does more work in the conclusion than in the evidence.
- Nearly everything is about *Troodon*, itself a probable mix of species; the ancestral-diet reconstruction extends from that.
- The flowchart (Fig 7) tallies conclusions from studies of very different strength as equal boxes.

- **Fig 7 (p14)**, flowchart of every line of evidence and its verdict in green (herbivory/omnivory), red (carnivory), grey (indeterminate). Dense but readable cropped; good for "how a review weighs evidence," with the caveat above.
