# Varricchio et al. 2002, "Embryos and eggs for the Cretaceous theropod dinosaur *Troodon formosus*"

Varricchio, Horner & Jackson. *Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology* 22(3):564-576 (2002). doi 10.1671/0272-4634(2002)022[0564:EAEFTC]2.0.CO;2.

File: `papers/varricchio2002.pdf`, 14 pages (Taylor & Francis cover page plus 13 scanned journal pages). Read in full; all eight figures viewed. The pages are 300 dpi scans of the print journal, so every figure is a greyscale halftone with scan grain; captions are OCR'd text with run-together words.

## Claim

- MOR 246, a clutch from Egg Island, Two Medicine Formation, Montana, first described by Horner & Weishampel (1988) as eggs and embryos of the small ornithischian *Orodromeus*. This paper reassigns them to *Troodon*.
- Clutch: "described and mapped as a clutch of 19 eggs," but "Only 16 egg bottoms can be recognized." Most eggs "stood angled within the sediments with their narrow end down." Eleven of 16 show bone.
- MOR 246-11 is the only egg with articulated bones: pelvis, folded hind limbs, and a skull "upside-down, pointing caudally between the legs." Skull about 50 x 22 mm; femur 34.9 mm; tibia 46 mm.
- Identification: 31 apomorphies listed (Table 2), from Dinosauria down to Troodontidae and *T. formosus*: pneumatic quadrate, closely set basal tubera, high tooth count, and a pinched metatarsal III between larger II and IV, seen in cross-section (Fig 3E). "These features clearly separate the embryos from all dromaeosaurids."
- Why the first ID was wrong: *Troodon* taxonomy was then confused (some treated it as an ornithischian), and *Orodromeus* bones are abundant at the egg sites. "The erroneous taxonomic assignment of Troodon eggs closely parallels the problems with those of Oviraptor," whose eggs were once given to *Protoceratops*. Lesson: identify eggs "strictly on well-preserved, in-situ embryonic material and not associated skeletons."
- Development: ossification matches avian stages 35-38, "roughly 85-90% through incubation." Uniform bone size across the clutch suggests one developmental stage, "consistent with, although not proof of," delayed incubation and synchronous hatching.
- Embryo limb proportions differ from adults (tibia/femur 1.36 vs 1.13; metatarsal IV/femur 0.96 vs 0.71): "longer-limbed hatchlings and juveniles."
- Eggs: *Prismatoolithus levis*, elongate and asymmetric, 12-16 cm, ~450 cm³. Shell has a mammillary layer, a prismatic layer about 6x thicker, and possibly a third external layer. Avian-like features "may represent synapomorphies": asymmetric shape, simple narrow pores, distinct layers, barrel-shaped cones with blocky calcite.
- Consequence: *Continuoolithus canadensis* eggs, once tentatively *Troodon*, "must represent another dinosaur taxon, most likely a theropod."
- Nest-site prey: abundant *Orodromeus* remains at the egg sites "likely represent prey items used by Troodon during the lengthy egg-laying and brooding periods," though few bones bear tooth marks.

## Push-back

- The prey claim rests on co-occurrence, with the authors noting few tooth marks. *Orodromeus* may have lived and died there independently (some are juveniles at nests of their own).
- The synchronous-hatching hint comes from randomly exposed bone diameters on break surfaces (2.3-3.5 mm). Compare [Yang et al. 2019](yang-2019.md), who argue the opposite (asynchrony) for oviraptorids.
- Later work splits "*Troodon formosus*": van der Reest & Currie 2017 named *Latenivenatrix* and revived *Stenonychosaurus*; [Varricchio et al. 2018](varricchio-2018.md) keep *Troodon* for this embryo material.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p4 of the PDF; journal p566)**, photo of egg MOR 246-11 on black background, with femur, tibia and metatarsals labeled, cm ruler. The paper's best image: a real embryo leg folded inside a broken egg. Halftone grain visible but clear at slide size. Crop to the egg.
- **Fig 2 (p5)**, eight small photos of isolated bones (basioccipital, maxilla, cervical vertebrae, humerus, scapula) with tiny letters. Murky greyscale on grey matrix; poor for slides.
- **Fig 3 (p6)**, ilium/femur/pubis block (A), fibula (B, C), articulated leg with dentary (D), and the metatarsal cross-section (E). (A) and (B, C) are crisp; (E) is the diagnostic image (three metatarsals, the middle one pinched) but small and dark.
- **Fig 4 (p8)**, complete egg MOR 299 and the bottom third of MOR 247, on black. Shows the asymmetric, elongate shape. Usable.
- **Figs 5-8 (pp9-11)**, eggshell SEM and polarized thin sections (layers, pore cast, possible protein fibers, mammillary cones vs an ornithoid-ratite shell). Specialist images; Fig 6 (layers in plain and polarized light) is the only one a class could read with help.
