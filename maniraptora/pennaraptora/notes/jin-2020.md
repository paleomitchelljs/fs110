# Jin et al. 2020, "An oviraptorosaur adult-egg association from the Cretaceous of Jiangxi Province, China"

Jin, Varricchio, Poust & He. *Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology* 40:e1739060 (2020). doi 10.1080/02724634.2019.1739060.

File: `papers/jin2019.pdf`, 17 pages (T&F cover page first). Read in full; all seven figures viewed.

## Claim

- ZMNH M8829, Nanxiong Fm., Nankang, Jiangxi. Found during construction blasting in 2011. Back half of an oviraptorosaur (gastralia, pelvis, hind limbs, tail) with "one egg within the pelvic canal and the other just posterior to it, ventral to the anterior caudal vertebrae." Probably an oviraptorid, close to *Tongtianlong* and *Jiangxisaurus*.
- How the eggs got there: the body lies on its side in a possible death pose; eggs "likely extruded during buildup of abdominal gases during decomposition." Compared to a photo of a calf extruded from a drowned cow (Fig 7).
- Two eggs at the same stage support "monoautochronic ovulation, i.e., iterative laying of two eggs at daily or greater intervals": two working oviducts, one egg each, laid in pairs. "The narrow fit of the egg in the pelvic canal argues against the hypothesis that theropods laid eggs in bound pairs."
- Egg economics: each egg ~500 g, adult 47-70 kg. Birds that size lay a 1,040-1,390 g egg. "each egg 36–48% the size predicted for a modern bird," but two at a time puts total output "on par with the reproductive output of extant birds."
- Eggs exit blunt end first, unlike most bird eggs; may be postmortem or real.
- Maturity: three LAGs, no EFS, open neurocentral sutures: "this reproductively active individual was several years old but still growing," sexual before somatic maturity, as in other maniraptorans.
- No medullary bone in a female carrying shelled eggs. "caution is required in using 'medullary bone' or its absence as a gender indicator in dinosaurs." And: "may challenge the avian homology of medullary tissue" claimed in *T. rex*, *Allosaurus*, *Tenontosaurus*.
- First female with articulated chevrons: first chevron full-size, and chevron shapes like a "male" *Khaan*, so chevrons are poor sex indicators.

A careful paper. The medullary-bone point bears on [Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md), whose brooding adult lacks it and is therefore possibly male.
