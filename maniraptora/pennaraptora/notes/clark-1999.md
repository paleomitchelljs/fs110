File: `papers/_v2_dspace_ingest_pdfSource_nov_N3265.pdf`, 36 pages, scanned (OCR is rough). Read the introduction, geologic setting, nest, posture, and "Implications of the specimen for oviraptorid behavior" in full; anatomy sections skimmed. All figures viewed.

## Claim

- IGM 100/979, Djadokhta Fm., Ukhaa Tolgod. The full description of the specimen announced by Norell et al. 1995 (*Nature*); later referred to *Citipati osmolskae*. No skull. Mislabeled "100/972" in the 1995 figure.
- The egg thief history: the *Oviraptor* holotype (AMNH 6517) lay on a nest whose eggs were called *Protoceratops*; Osborn "speculated that the adult animal had been preserved while robbing the nest (although there was no evidence to indicate predation)." An oviraptorid embryo in the same egg type (Norell et al. 1994) made the association "explicable as evidence of parental care, rather than predation."
- Posture: "The body would have completely covered the nest, with the abdominal region over the center, and both limbs are placed in positions nearly symmetrical across the midline"; arms "spread out around the nest perimeter."
- Sample: four oviraptorid skeletons on nests "were among the first 30 adult specimens of this family collected, a ratio of 13.3%"; among the Djadokhta structureless sandstones, "among only 17 adult specimens collected before 1996, a ratio of 23.5%." The four: AMNH 6517 (*Oviraptor*, Bayn Dzak), IVPP V9608 (Bayan Mandahu, Dong & Currie 1996), IGM 100/979, and an unprepared Ukhaa Tolgod skeleton (MAE 95-97).
- Not laying: "If the animal had been in the act of laying an egg or preparing to do so, it is expected that one or two would be preserved here [the pelvis]." The pelvis is incomplete, so "this assessment uncertain."
- Nest open: "The direct apposition of the skeleton on the nest in IGM 100/979 provides strong evidence that the nest was not completely covered." But "it is not possible to determine precisely how much of the nest was buried."
- Other oviraptorids in the same beds "are not in this position, lying instead on their sides." "We are unaware of any nonavialan dinosaur specimens other than IGM 100/979 and IVPP V9608 preserved in precisely this posture."
- Crocodylians: "not reported to lie directly on eggs (which are buried) or with the body centered on the nest."
- Their limit on heat: sitting on nests "does not necessarily imply that they were endothermic and provided heat to the eggs on a regular basis ... the assumption that the behavior associated with brooding and the act of brooding itself was correlated in oviraptorids is just that—an untestable assumption."
- Method: "demonstrating the capability of a behavior is not equivalent to demonstrating that the behavior was present." On egg arrangement, adult manipulation vs precise laying: "It is unclear if there is any evidence that could falsify either of these hypotheses."
- Open questions they list: "we do not know the sex of the adults on the nests, their precise genealogical relationship to the eggs ..., how often and for how long adults attended the nest."

A model of careful hedging, and it sets up the three later questions the deck covers: heat (Bi 2021 isotopes answer what Clark called untestable), sex (Varricchio 2008, Jin 2020), and burial (Tanaka 2015, Hogan 2023). The 23.5% depends on what counts as the sample; say so.

Pairs with [Fanti et al. 2012](fanti-2012.md), [Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md), [Varricchio et al. 2008](varricchio-2008.md).
