# Xu et al. 2015, "A bizarre Jurassic maniraptoran theropod with preserved evidence of membranous wings"

Xu, Zheng, Sullivan, Wang, Xing, Wang, Zhang, O'Connor, Zhang & Pan. *Nature* 521:70-73 (online 29 Apr 2015). doi 10.1038/nature14423.

File: `papers/Xuetal2015Yiqi.pdf`, 14 pages: letter pp. 1-4, Extended Data Figs 1-10 pp. 5-14. Read in full; all figures and ED figures viewed. Supplementary Information not in the folder. Tested aerodynamically by [Dececchi et al. 2020](dececchi-2020.md).

## Claim

- *Yi qi*, STM 31-2, Tiaojishan Fm., Mutoudeng, Hebei, Callovian-Oxfordian; Daohugou/Yanliao Biota. Farmer-collected; "provenance and authenticity have been confirmed by multiple lines of evidence including sedimentology, taphonomy and computed tomography." ~380 g. Scansoriopterygid.
- Name: "Yi (wing) and qi (strange)... pronunciation ... roughly 'ee chee'."
- The rod: "an anomalous, slightly curved, distally tapered, rod-like structure ..., whose length considerably exceeds that of the ulna, associated with each wrist and apparently extending from the ulnar side of the carpus." EDS shows it is "a bone (or possibly a skeletal element composed of calcified cartilage)." Termed a "styliform element," likened to the wrist rod of *Petaurista* flying squirrels, the bat calcar, and the pterosaur pteroid.
- Membrane: "Several patches of membranous soft tissue are exposed around the styliform element and digits in both hands ... a sheet-like appearance ... in some areas displays prominent ripple-like striations." Contains "only small phaeomelanosomes (long axis 300–400 nm)."
- Feathers: no pennaceous feathers. Limb feathers "a unique paintbrush-like morphology: the proximal three-fourths of each feather is a wide (about 1.2 mm), undifferentiated shaft-like structure, whereas the distal part is composed of numerous near-parallel filaments." Read as "secondary loss of normal pennaraptoran pinnate feathers."
- The inference: "Because other amniotes that possess a styliform element invariably utilize this structure to support an aerodynamic membrane ..., the occurrence of a styliform element in Yi is a strong indication that membranous aerodynamic surfaces and some degree of aerial capability were present."
- Their own limits: "the flight apparatus of Yi cannot be confidently reconstructed due to the incomplete preservation of the wing membrane in the only known specimen, combined with uncertainties regarding the styliform element's orientation and connection to the wrist." Gliding favored over flapping, but "the mode of aerial locomotion that is most likely for Yi remains uncertain."
- Fallback: "even if our inference that Yi was an aerially adapted taxon with membranous wings proves incorrect, the styliform element and membranous tissue patches will stand as highly unusual features."

The membrane is patches, the rod's orientation is unknown, and the wing shape is a choice among models (ED Fig 7). The argument is by analogy to living gliders.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, specimen photo (a) above a grey-shaded line drawing (b, light grey feathers, dark grey membrane), with skull, tooth, hand and styliform close-ups (c-f). The photo is dark slab with a dark animal; the body is hard to read. The drawing (b) is the usable panel: it shows the rods and membrane patches. Small labels.
- **Fig 2 (p3)**, seven soft-tissue close-ups: feathers on skull, humerus, tibia (a-c), an isolated feather (d), drawing of the brush-like feather (e), membrane with yellow arrows (f), membrane vs feathers above/below digit II (g). Dark, grainy; (g) is the most convincing for "sheet, not filaments." (f) needs the arrows.
- **Fig 3 (p3)**, cladogram on the left, with a black skeletal silhouette of *Yi* and two grey planform reconstructions (bat-like and long-rod) on the right. The silhouettes are the strongest image in the paper for a slide; crop the right half. The tree's tiny italic type is unreadable at slide size.
- **ED Fig 1 (p5)**, field photos of the locality and excavation. Context only.
- **ED Fig 2-3 (pp6-7)**, slab and counterslab, fragment matches. Authenticity evidence; not slide material.
- **ED Fig 4 (p8)**, CT volume: a featureless grey blob ("little density contrast"). Useless.
- **ED Fig 5 (p9)**, more integument photos, dark.
- **ED Fig 6 (p10)**, four EDS spectra (bone, rod, feathers, matrix). Clear orange bars; shows the rod matches bone (Ca, P peaks) and the matrix doesn't. Good for "how do you know it's bone."
- **ED Fig 7 (p11)**, three candidate wing models for *Yi* (bat, maniraptoran, frog) and wings of bat, pigeon, pterosaur, flying squirrel, all in matched grey and white with the styliform element in yellow. Clean, consistent style, white background. The best teaching figure: shows the comparison and the uncertainty at once. Crops well as a whole or by row.
- **ED Fig 8-9 (pp12-13)**, SEM sample map and melanosome micrographs. Greyscale, grainy.
- **ED Fig 10 (p14)**, full strict consensus. Type too small.
