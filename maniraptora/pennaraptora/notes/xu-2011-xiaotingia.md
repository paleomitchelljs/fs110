# Xu et al. 2011, "An *Archaeopteryx*-like theropod from China and the origin of Avialae"

Xu, You, Du & Han. *Nature* 475:465-470 (2011). doi 10.1038/nature10288.

File: `papers/An_Archaeopteryx-like_theropod_from_China_and_the_.pdf`, 6 pages. Read in full; all four figures viewed.

## Claim

- *Xiaotingia zhengi*, STM 27-2, an articulated skeleton missing parts of the pelvis, hindlimbs and most of the tail; Tiaojishan Formation, Late Jurassic, Liaoning. Fused vertebral sutures: "probably adult." ~0.82 kg. Faint feather traces all over; femur feathers >55 mm, "consistent with the tetrapterygian condition."
- Main point: features thought to define birds, "including long and robust forelimbs, actually characterize the more inclusive group Paraves."
- Tree: adding *Xiaotingia* "shifts Archaeopteryx to the Deinonychosauria," with *Anchiornis* and *Xiaotingia* in Archaeopterygidae at the base of Deinonychosauria. Shared features with deinonychosaurs: large promaxillary fenestra, T-shaped lacrimal, a dentary groove widening backward, a highly extensible second toe.
- Support is weak and stated so: "It should be noted that our phylogenetic hypothesis is only weakly supported by the available data." Deinonychosauria including Archaeopterygidae has "a bootstrap value less than 50% and a Bremer support value of 2."
- Cause given: "widespread homoplasy," many characters "functionally significant." "If this new phylogenetic hypothesis can be confirmed ... current assumptions regarding the avialan ancestral condition will need to be re-evaluated."

## How later papers treat it

- [Brusatte et al. 2014](brusatte-2014.md): *Archaeopteryx* back at the base of Avialae; *Xiaotingia* and *Anchiornis* basal troodontids.
- [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md): *Xiaotingia* in Anchiornithinae, the earliest avialans; *Xiaotingia* "never achieves sufficient lift."
- [Balanoff et al. 2013](balanoff-2013.md) cite this result as one reason to test whether *Archaeopteryx*'s brain is uniquely intermediate.

So one specimen moved the iconic taxon out of birds, and later, larger analyses moved it back while relocating *Xiaotingia* itself twice. The clearest example in the folder of how a tree with Bremer support of 1-2 responds to one new taxon.
