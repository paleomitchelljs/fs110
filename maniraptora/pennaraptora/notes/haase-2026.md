# Haase et al. 2026, "Metabolic features of enantiornithines (Avialae: Ornithothoraces): evidence supporting a semi-precocial/precocial developmental trajectory"

Haase, Knoll, Voeten, Sanchez, Li, Bert, de Araújo, Serrano, Terol, Marugán-Lobón & Cubo. *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 13:260574 (2026). doi 10.1098/rsos.260574.

File: `papers/rsos.260574.pdf`, 17 pages. Read in full; all six figures viewed. Data and scripts on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.22058889).

## Claim

- Retrodicts metabolic rates for enantiornithines from bone proxies: vascular density for resting metabolic rate (RMR), nutrient-foramen size (blood flow, Q̇) for peak metabolic rate (PMR). Phylogenetic eigenvector maps against living hatchlings across the altricial-precocial range.
- Specimens: Las Hoyas hatchling MPCM-LH-26189 (~2.09 g), Jehol "hatchling" IVPP V15686 (~27.85 g; from [O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025.md)), Jehol subadult IVPP V17968, and Las Hoyas adults *Concornis*, *Iberomesornis*, *Eoalulavis*.
- Logic: "altricial hatchlings exhibit similar RMR and PMR values, while precocial hatchlings have much higher PMR/RMR ratio values." Malleefowl hatchling PMR is "more than four times the RMR."
- Result: the Las Hoyas chick's PMR/RMR is 2.38 (1.93-3.33). Classification probabilities "0.49 for precociality, 0.44 for semi-precociality and 0.06 for altriciality."
- Flight not right away: "MPCM-LH-26189 clearly had a largely cartilaginous sternum, insufficiently robust to support functional flight muscles ... and lacked well-developed feathers." "hatchlings that still exhibit incomplete sternal ossification, such as MPCM-LH-26189, show that early flight was unfeasible." Instead, climbing "using their wing claws in much the same way as hoatzin ... chicks do," then perhaps WAIR.
- Growth: "metabolic capabilities ... increased dramatically during early post-hatching ontogeny." Las Hoyas adults reach PMR ~5x RMR.
- Limits they state: few altricial species sampled; blood-flow equation "based on cursorial birds"; Jehol vs Spain size comparison has n = 45 vs 4.

## Push-back

- The two "hatchlings" are treated "to represent the same ontogenetic stage" though one is ~13x the mass of the other (2.09 g vs 27.85 g). The paper also calls IVPP V15686 a "subadult" with "apparent fusion of the hindlimb elements" in Methods before treating it as a hatchling; [O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025.md) describe it as early immature with femora only, no hindlimb distal to the femur.
- The developmental-mode call rests on one fossil ratio with a wide interval sitting across two categories.
- Direct conflict with [O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025.md), who read forelimb bone maturity as "super-precocial flight." Haase et al. say the sternum rules flight out at hatching. A clean pair for a "same fossils, different readings" slide.

## Figures

All vector, colorful, white background. Generally dense.

- **Fig 1 (p8)**, time tree of extant hatchlings colored by developmental mode (green altricial through blue precocial), with RMR dot-and-whisker plot on the right and the two fossil chicks highlighted. Long, tiny taxon labels. The color legend is the useful part.
- **Fig 2 (p9)**, same for PMR, with many more taxa. Too dense.
- **Figs 3-4 (pp9-10)**, small trees for adult PMR retrodictions. Sparse; technical.
- **Fig 5 (p11)**, RMR and PMR across ontogeny (hatchling vs post-juvenile), red Spanish and blue Chinese symbols on grey bird silhouettes. Simple two-panel plot; shows the rise in PMR with age. Small.
- **Fig 6 (p12)**, violin plots of PMR/RMR for precocial (green), semi-precocial (orange), altricial (blue) living hatchlings, with bird silhouettes and the fossil chick as a magenta star at 2.38. The paper's best slide figure: one star, three distributions. The y-axis runs to 13 because of a duckling outlier, which compresses the violins; would need a note.
