## Claim

- LDNHMF2008, Nanxiong Fm. (Maastrichtian), Ganzhou, Jiangxi. A partial adult oviraptorid (no skull) sitting on a clutch of at least 24 eggs (*Macroolithus yaotunensis*, ~215 x 85 mm) in three stacked rings, outer diameter 78 cm. Some eggs contain embryos. "the first non-avialan dinosaur fossil known to preserve an adult skeleton atop an egg clutch that contains embryonic remains."
- Why it matters: earlier nesting oviraptorids (*Citipati*, *Nemegtomaia*) could be read as dying while laying or guarding. The objections: varying clutch size, partly buried eggs with little contact, and "no embryonic remains have been found within the eggs."
- The adult is fully grown: humerus with an EFS of "at least nine lines of arrested growth." No medullary bone, which "raises the possibility that the attending individual may have been male," but medullary bone is rare in dinosaurs, so female not ruled out.
- Embryos are late-stage: "Because the embryos died at late developmental stages, the adult clearly had tended the nest for an extended period and did not perish in the act of egg laying."
- Incubation temperatures from oxygen isotopes of embryo bone and eggshell: "36–38°C for C2 and C3 and 30–32°C for C8," in the range of brooding birds; reptiles incubate at 26-32°C.
- Asynchronous hatching: embryos differ in size and ossification (femur 5.5 cm in C5 vs 4 cm in B9), upper ring ahead of middle ring. Proposed mechanism: upper eggs nearer the adult got more heat. "The discovery of asynchronous hatching in oviraptorosaurs is unexpected given its late appearance even among crown birds."
- Conclusion: brooding hypothesis strongly supported; "the evolution of reproductive biology along bird-line archosaurs was a complex rather than a linear and incremental process."
- Their own caveats: "it cannot be completely excluded that the developmental failures of some eggs lead to the ontogenetic variation"; "a complete reconstruction of the developmental variation pattern ... is currently not possible." They also note their hatching scenario "is not entirely consistent with data from living birds."

The temperature estimate rests on three eggs and a model; the asynchrony could equally reflect some embryos dying earlier. The brooding conclusion is strong; the asynchronous-hatching one is a reasonable but thin inference.

Pairs with [Yang et al. 2019](yang-2019.md) (asynchronous hatching from eggshell color and porosity), [Jin et al. 2020](jin-2020.md), and [Tanaka et al. 2018](tanaka-2018.md).
