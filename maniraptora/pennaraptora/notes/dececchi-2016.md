# Dececchi, Larsson & Habib 2016, "The wings before the bird: an evaluation of flapping-based locomotory hypotheses in bird antecedents"

Dececchi, Larsson & Habib. *PeerJ* 4:e2159 (2016). doi 10.7717/peerj.2159. Open access.

File: `papers/peerj-2159.pdf`, 41 pages. Abstract, introduction, methods summary, results summary and conclusion read; long methods and per-taxon results skimmed. All five figures viewed.

## Claim

- Question: could early feathered wings have helped with three proposed ground-based routes to flight? Flap-running (wings add speed), WAIR (wing-assisted incline running: flapping to run up steep slopes, as chukar chicks do), and wing-assisted leaping.
- Method: physics models calibrated on living birds (chukar, brush turkey), applied to 45 specimens of 24 non-avian theropods (60 g to 18 kg) plus five birds, mapped along the line from Coelurosauria to birds. Three late embryos (*Troodon* MOR 246-1, *Citipati*) are included to test whether juveniles could WAIR when adults could not.
- Assumptions set generously: forelimb muscle 10% of body mass, which they call "likely significant overestimations for non-paravians"; bird-level burst power. *Archaeopteryx* pectoral muscle has been estimated at "only 0.5% of its body mass."
- Result: "None of these behaviours were found to meet the biomechanical threshold requirements before Paraves." No "continuous trend of refinement" toward birds. WAIR can't be rejected "in large winged, small-bodied taxa like Microraptor and Archaeopteryx." Flap-running gave at most a 17% speed gain.
- Key variable: "near flight" behaviors "are most sensitive to wing area."
- Consequence: big vaned forelimb feathers in *Caudipteryx*, *Similicaudipteryx*, *Yixianosaurus* ("greater than 100 mm in length") were "a significant energetic investment for structures that we estimate to have had minimal locomotory benefits," so "display or brooding were likely significant evolutionary driver[s] for pennaceous feather growth."
- Mosaic: flight-related characters (long feathered arms, sideways-facing shoulder socket, ossified sternum) appear unevenly, so "the evolution of the flight stroke was not continuous in this clade, nor driven by a single overall driver."

## Push-back and pairing

- Thresholds matter: WAIR is scored against a minimum of 6% body-weight support. They answer the objection that 1-5% might still help: such values "are not testable." A fair reply, but students should see that the conclusion depends on where the line is drawn.
- Contrast with [Talori et al. 2018](talori-2018.md), who found *Caudipteryx* wings could produce small forces while running: the two agree the forces are small and disagree on whether "small" matters.
- [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md) (shared authors) moves from behaviors to flight thresholds on a better tree and reaches a compatible result: near misses everywhere, passes only in a few paravians.
- Display/brooding as the driver links to [Persons et al. 2014](persons-2014.md) (tail display) and the brooding papers ([Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md)).
