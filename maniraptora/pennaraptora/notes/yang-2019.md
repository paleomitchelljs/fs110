# Yang et al. 2019, "Hatching asynchrony in oviraptorid dinosaurs sheds light on their unique nesting biology"

Yang, Engler, Lallensack, Samathi, Makowska & Schillinger. *Integrative Organismal Biology* 1:obz030 (2019). doi 10.1093/iob/obz030.

File: `papers/yang2019.pdf`, 55 pages. This is the accepted manuscript (double-spaced, figures at the end, abstract in four languages), not the typeset article. Read in full; figure pages 43-50 viewed. Pages 53-55 (headed "Egg I/II/III," presumably eggshell sections) render blank in this copy, and there is no whole-specimen photograph among the figure pages; check the published version for both.

## Claim

- SMNH-20140105, Nanxiong Group, Jiangxi. A partial oviraptorid clutch of three embryo-bearing eggs: a single egg (I) and a pair (II/III). Imaged with X-ray and neutron CT.
- Order of laying: "Geopetal features indicate that the pair of eggs was laid prior to the single egg."
- Development: egg III embryo has articulated, well-developed tail vertebrae, "near the hatching stage"; egg I has "scattered vertebrae that are ill-defined"; its metatarsal (0.9 cm) is slimmer than egg III's (1.1 cm). Eggshell erosion (embryos dissolve the inner shell for calcium) is "markedly more pronounced" in the pair.
- Conclusion: hatching asynchrony. Earlier evidence (Weishampel et al. 2008, four Mongolian eggs) could not show the eggs shared a clutch.
- Embryo faces the blunt end, toward the clutch center, so it hatched from the exposed end, matching a partially open nest.
- Mechanism: two options. With contact incubation, an incubating male might start before females finished the clutch (not seen in any polygamous bird). Without effective contact incubation, asynchrony comes from the time gap between laying rings. "As contact incubation can probably be excluded in oviraptorid dinosaurs (Yang et al. 2019), the second inference is preferred here."
- Big conclusion: "oviraptorid dinosaurs presented a unique reproductive biology lacking modern analogs, which is contrary to the predominant view that their reproductive biology was intermediate between that of modern crocodiles and birds." "caution is therefore warranted when including oviraptorids in analyses of the ancestral state of bird reproduction."
- Their own caveats: the eggs don't preserve matching bones, so "an estimation of the length of the gap ... is not possible"; asynchrony requires assuming no developmental arrest and no staggered deaths; egg lengths may differ because of compaction at different tilts.

## Push-back and pairing

- Three eggs, no matching bones, and development judged mainly by "level of articulation." The inference is suggestive.
- Direct disagreement with [Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md): Bi et al. infer contact brooding at 36-38°C and explain asynchrony by heat from the sitting adult; Yang et al. think contact incubation "can probably be excluded" and put asynchrony down to laying gaps. Both agree on asynchrony; they disagree on why. A good "same observation, rival mechanisms" slide.
