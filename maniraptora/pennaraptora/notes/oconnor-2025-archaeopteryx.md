## Claim

- 14th skeletal *Archaeopteryx*, FMNH PA 830, "nearly complete and uncrushed," missing only the left major digit ungual. Found in a talus pile before the 1990s, held by private collectors "outside Germany," bought by the Field Museum in 2022; probably from Köschinger Forst, ~40 km east of Solnhofen. Prepared under μCT and UV guidance.
- Small: humerus and femur ~96% and 98% of the Eichstätt specimen.
- Palate is a mosaic: "intermediate between troodontids and crownward Cretaceous birds." Shares pterygoid flange and palatine proportions with troodontids, which "may indicate that the historically recognized clade Deinonychosauria is paraphyletic and support a closer relationship between birds and troodontids." But vomer and lack of palatal processes "are distinctly early bird-like."
- Skull "shift to a less rigid cranial architecture, one of the first prerequisites for the evolution of cranial kinesis."
- Vertebrae: 9 cervicals, 14 dorsals, 5 sacrals, 24 caudals ("only 22 preserved previously"). Paired proatlases.
- Hand: skin outline on the major digit "suggests that it was not ligamentously bound to the minor digit along their entire lengths as previously suggested." Minor digit flexible distally.
- Feet: foot pads with "small, tightly packed, uniformly sized non-spiculate ... reticulate scales." Long phalanx pads, as in ground-foraging birds: "support hypotheses that Archaeopteryx spent considerable time on the ground." Overall "similar to pigeons and doves."
- Tertials, the headline: "Specialized inner secondary feathers called tertials are observed on both wings." "humeral tertials are absent in non-avian theropods with well-preserved forelimb feathers (for example, Anchiornis and Zhenyuanlong), which suggests humeral tertials initially evolved in paravians as a flight adaptation to close the gap between the wing and body."
- The ulnar tertial fan is oversized: in 218 living birds tertials are "11–56% that of the secondaries, compared to 85% in Archaeopteryx." Possible roles: protection from abrasion, "visual communication," or "the unique flight apparatus of this taxon."
- Body feathers are mostly open-pennaceous, as in other Mesozoic paravians; closed pennaceous only on the tibia (crural feathers), "supports interpretations that these feathers have or had (ancestrally) an aerodynamic function."

The tertials-as-flight-adaptation argument depends on absence of evidence in *Anchiornis* and *Zhenyuanlong*; the authors call it "current data." Note also: privately traded specimen, decades out of scientific reach.

## Figures

High resolution throughout (photos 415-600 ppi).

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, full slab photo (a), line drawing (b), left foot (c). The skeleton sits small on a large cream slab; at page size the bones are faint. The drawing is clean, labeled, with feather outlines. The source photo is 1720x3731 px; cropping to the skeleton gives a sharp, full-slide image. The best specimen photo of *Archaeopteryx* in this folder.
- **Fig 2 (p3)**, skull photo (a), drawing on grey (b), color-coded 3D CT models (c, d), and palates of eight taxa from *Dromaeosaurus* to *Tragopan* on a cladogram (e), bones in matching colors. (e) is a clean teaching figure for "mosaic," but palatal anatomy is advanced for an intro class. The 3D skull (c) is striking.
- **Fig 3 (p4)**, atlas-axis and last tail vertebrae, purple-tinted photos. Technical.
- **Fig 4 (p6)**, hand skin (a, b) and foot pads with scales (c-e), photos with orange-shaded drawings. Sharp; (c-e) foot pads are good for "what soft tissue survives."
- **ED Fig 1 (p9)**, CT models of individual palate bones. Technical.
- **ED Fig 2 (p11)**, three palate reconstructions (two old, one new) in color. Clean.
- **ED Fig 3 (p12)**, proatlas and atlas of four paravians in olive and pink. Clean, niche.
- **ED Fig 4 (p13)**, wrist close-up. Technical.
- **ED Fig 5 (p14)**, foot pads of arboreal, terrestrial, raptorial living birds vs fossils, with summary text boxes in color. Good comparative layout; works on a slide for the ground-foraging argument.
- **ED Fig 6 (p15)**, the specimen with feather tracts shaded in color (primaries, secondaries, tertials, scapulars, tail, body, crural). The best feather figure: shows where each tract lies. Use for "what are tertials."
- **ED Fig 7 (p16)**, UV and close-up photos of tertials and body feathers. Fine detail.
- **ED Fig 8 (p17)**, tertials in living birds (wagtail, sandpiper, crane, trumpeter) with red inset sketches. Clear and accessible.
- **ED Fig 9 (p18)**, Michael Rothman color reconstruction: black-and-white *Archaeopteryx* with the tertial fan, in a cycad forest. Attractive life image.
