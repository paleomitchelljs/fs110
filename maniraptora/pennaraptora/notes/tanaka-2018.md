# Tanaka et al. 2018, "Incubation behaviours of oviraptorosaur dinosaurs in relation to body size"

Tanaka, Zelenitsky, Lü, DeBuhr, Yi, Jia, Ding, Xia, Liu, Shen & Chen. *Biology Letters* 14:20180135 (2018). doi 10.1098/rsbl.2018.0135.

File: `papers/rsbl20180135.pdf`, 4 pages. Read in full (the discussion is partly garbled in extraction; read p4 in the PDF); both figures viewed.

## Claim

- Question: brooding by sitting on eggs is known in small oviraptorosaurs and troodontids; could big ones do it? Birds max out near 450 kg (elephant bird), possibly because "the weight borne by the eggs while brooding constrains maximum adult body size."
- Data: 40 elongatoolithid clutches in three egg sizes (*Elongatoolithus* <170 mm, *Macroolithus* 170-240 mm, *Macroelongatoolithus* ≥240 mm). Egg mass 197-6,594 g. Adult mass predicted from clutch volume, 41-1,563 kg, matching skeletons from *Nomingia* (37 kg) to *Gigantoraptor* (1,400-2,092 kg).
- All clutches are rings of radially inclined eggs; complete ones hold >30 eggs.
- The key result: the central hole grows faster than the clutch (slope 1.49). Inner diameter 2-21.6 cm in *Elongatoolithus*, 104-236 cm in *Macroelongatoolithus*. In giants the hole "occupies most of the nest area."
- Larger eggs have relatively thinner shells, so "were probably structurally weaker."
- Porosity: all low, like brooding birds, "6–69% of porosity values for living covered nesters"; discriminant analysis classes all but one as open nesters.
- Interpretation: "the smallest oviraptorosaurs probably sat directly on the eggs, whereas with increasing body size more weight was likely carried by the central opening, reducing or eliminating the load on the eggs and still potentially allowing for some contact during incubation in giant species." "This adaptation, not seen in birds, appears to remove the body size constraints of incubation behaviour."
- Caveat: if brooding was for heat, it "may have been less effective in large species because there may have been less contact with the eggs."

The giant-clutch result rests on three *Macroelongatoolithus* clutches; body masses come from a regression of clutch volume on living precocial birds and crocodiles.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, three clutch photos to scale (small, medium, a 3 m giant clutch as a photogrammetric image), then four scatterplots: clutch diameter vs egg mass, inner vs outer diameter (with black-and-white ring icons showing the hole growing), shell strength, and porosity vs egg mass with buried-vs-open nest box icons. Colored oviraptorosaur silhouettes mark size classes. The photos (a-c) make the point at a glance; plot (e) with ring icons is the teaching figure. Sharp vector plots.
