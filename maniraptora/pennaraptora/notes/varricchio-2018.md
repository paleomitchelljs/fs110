# Varricchio et al. 2018, "An intermediate incubation period and primitive brooding in a theropod dinosaur"

Varricchio, Kundrát & Hogan. *Scientific Reports* 8:12454 (2018). doi 10.1038/s41598-018-30085-6.

File: `papers/s41598-018-30085-6.pdf`, 6 pages. Read in full; the one figure viewed.

## Claim

- Specimen: a disarticulated tooth from MOR 246-11, a near-hatching *Troodon* embryo in a clutch of 19 eggs, Two Medicine Formation, Montana. Tooth 1.9 mm long, crown tip missing.
- Method: count daily growth lines (von Ebner lines) in the dentine, in a synchrotron section and confirmed in thin section. "31 intact lines with an average spacing of 3.3 ± 0.96 μm. Adding 8 more for the missing crown tip gives a total age of 39 days."
- Conversion: crocodiles start their functional teeth at 42-52% (mean 47%) through incubation, so "a Troodon incubation period of 74 days" (range 67-81), "midway between avian (44.4 days) and reptilian (107.3 days) values predicted by the Troodon egg mass (314 g)."
- Contrast: *Hypacrosaurus* and *Protoceratops* (Erickson et al.) came out reptile-like, about 208-209% of the avian prediction.
- Robustness: adding no missing lines, or double the estimate, gives 60 or 90 days, "still fall[ing] well between" the two models. 74 days lies outside the 95% interval for birds (max 61 days).
- Interpretation: shorter-than-reptile incubation "supports brooding," raising clutch temperature; but "the largely buried condition of Troodon clutches may have prohibited efficient brooding, necessitating longer incubation than that of modern birds."
- Cost: nest-building, laying and brooding "would likely require three months per year or more of nest-site residence."

## Push-back

- One tooth from one embryo. The whole estimate hangs on the crocodile timing of tooth formation (47%), which the authors flag: "uncertainty exists for when embryonic dinosaurs established their functional dentition and the validity of using the timing in modern crocodilians."
- Lines were hard to see; tubules "visually predominate the von Ebner lines at every focal depth." The count comes mainly from the scan.
- "Primitive brooding" in the title is an inference from the gap between 74 and 107 days; an intermediate period could also come from warm burial without brooding. The paper doesn't test that.
- Pairs with [Hogan & Varricchio 2023](hogan-2023.md), which reuses the 74-day figure, and [Tanaka et al. 2015](tanaka-2015.md) on partial burial.
