# Wu et al. 2024, "Transformation of the pectoral girdle in pennaraptorans: critical steps in the formation of the modern avian shoulder joint"

Wu, O'Connor, Wang & Zhou. *PeerJ* 12:e16960 (2024). doi 10.7717/peerj.16960. Open access (PMC10909347). Downloaded as a related find; not on the requested list.

File: `papers/pectoral2024.pdf`, 42 pages. Abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusions read; the per-clade comparisons skimmed. All seven figures viewed.

## Claim

- A comparative review (no new analysis) of the shoulder girdle (scapula, coracoid, furcula) across pennaraptorans, "including all purportedly volant taxa," drawn as matched line drawings.
- Before birds: "many pectoral girdle modifications appear during the evolution from basal pennaraptorans to paravians," such as the coracoid turning to face forward and the furcula-scapula joint moving. These "suggest a change in forelimb range of motion preceded the origin of flight in paravians."
- In early avialans: scapula and coracoid separate, the angle between them shrinks, the coracoid lengthens. Their variety suggests "each early avialan lineage evolved these features in parallel as they independently evolved more refined flight capabilities."
- Ornithothoracines (enantiornithines + ornithuromorphs): shoulder socket and acrocoracoid approach modern birds, suggesting "improved powered flight capabilities, such as ground take-off."
- Ornithuromorphs: a new coracoid-furcula joint completes the bony triosseal canal (the pulley for the upstroke muscle), "the complete acquisition of the modern flight apparatus."
- *Archaeopteryx* is placed in "a flight apparatus tentatively limited to gliding or weak flapping flight"; modern-style powered flight by the Early Cretaceous, "only 20 million years after" *Archaeopteryx*.
- Open questions stated: "It is still unclear what is the functional difference between a fused and separate scapula-coracoid joint."

## Use in the deck

- A skeletal line of evidence that sits between the others: it agrees with [Hartman et al. 2019](hartman-2019.md) and [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md) on parallel refinement within early birds, and gives the anatomical reason [Voeten et al. 2018](voeten-2018.md) needed a non-modern flight stroke for *Archaeopteryx*.
- Shares an author (O'Connor) with [Kiat & O'Connor 2024](kiat-oconnor-2024.md) but frames flight as evolving "at least once among non-avialan paravians" as well as in birds; worth noting the difference in emphasis between the two papers.
