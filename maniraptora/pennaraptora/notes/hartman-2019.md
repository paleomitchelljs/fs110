# Hartman et al. 2019, "A new paravian dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of North America supports a late acquisition of avian flight"

Hartman, Mortimer, Wahl, Lomax, Lippincott & Lovelace. *PeerJ* 7:e7247 (2019). doi 10.7717/peerj.7247. Open access (PMC6626525).

File: `papers/hartman2019.pdf`, 51 pages, 109 MB (high-resolution figures). Abstract, introduction, phylogenetic results, discussion and conclusions read; the long anatomical description skimmed. Figures 1-3, 17 and 18 viewed; the bone-by-bone figures (4-16) not reviewed.

## Claim

- *Hesperornithoides miessleri*, WYDICE-DML-001, Morrison Formation (Late Jurassic), Wyoming. A partial skull and skeleton of one adult or subadult, about 89 cm long, preserved lying down with the head beside the left hand; found in wetland or lake-margin deposits.
- "Limb proportions firmly establish Hesperornithoides as occupying a terrestrial, non-volant lifestyle." Humerus/femur ratio 0.56.
- Tree: an expanded TWiG-based matrix (strict consensus length 12,123 steps; CI 0.073). *Hesperornithoides* comes out as a troodontid near *Daliansaurus*, *Xixiasaurus*, *Sinusonasus*. But it moves easily: first dromaeosaurid is "just two steps longer"; in trees two steps longer where troodontids are avialans, it can be the first taxon closer to birds.
- On support: "a single step separates several different paravian phylogenies." They quote Turner et al. 2012 finding only one step needed to put troodontids in Avialae while calling Deinonychosauria "well supported," and warn: "Given the propensity of authors to reuse previous scorings and character constructions, repeated phylogenetic results may result in a false impression of confidence."
- "This does not mean anything goes": several arrangements are rejected by many steps (e.g. "basal paravian archaeopterygids, avialan microraptorians, Rahonavis closer to Pygostylia").
- Flight: arm-to-leg ratios (Table 3) show "unambiguously non-flying taxa at the base of all clades regardless of topology." Long-armed *Microraptor*, *Rahonavis* and *Archaeopteryx* each sit inside clades of short-armed relatives. Below a ratio of ~0.70, animals "lack the wing-loading to generate significant horizontal or vertical thrust."
- Conclusion: "a series of parallel appearances of non-avian aerodynamic locomotion within microraptorians, unenlagiids, archaeopterygids, and scansoriopterygids," none leading to bird flight; avian flight perhaps arose "as late as the Late Jurassic or Early Cretaceous," with *Zhongjianornis* and *Sapeornis* as the first clear fliers.
- Model: small terrestrial paravians used WAIR to reach trees, then glided or flap-descended, giving "several parallel experiments with aerial behavior, only one of which led directly to avian flight."

## Push-back and pairing

- The "flightless at the base" argument uses one measure (humerus/femur ratio) and a threshold from [Dececchi et al. 2016](dececchi-2016.md). It ignores feather area, which is what [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md) and [Kiat & O'Connor 2024](kiat-oconnor-2024.md) emphasize.
- The trees are the same family (TWiG) as Brusatte 2014 and Pei 2020, and the authors themselves show that 1-2 steps rearrange Paraves. Their flight conclusion is meant to hold "regardless of topology," which is the point to test with students: does it?
- Three-way disagreement on flight origins: Pei (≥3 origins incl. birds early), Kiat & O'Connor (one early origin, many losses), Hartman (parallel experiments, bird flight late). Same fossils, different lines (wing loading, feathers, arm ratios).
- Single specimen of the new taxon; the flight conclusion comes from the table of other taxa, not from *Hesperornithoides*.
