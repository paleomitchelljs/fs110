# Jasinski et al. 2020, "New dromaeosaurid dinosaur (Theropoda, Dromaeosauridae) from New Mexico and biodiversity of dromaeosaurids at the end of the Cretaceous"

Jasinski, Sullivan & Dodson. *Scientific Reports* 10:5105 (2020). doi 10.1038/s41598-020-61480-7.

File: `papers/s41598-020-61480-7.pdf`, 10 pages. Read in full; all three figures viewed.

## Claim

- *Dineobellator notohesperus*, SMP VP-2430, Naashoibito Member, Ojo Alamo Fm., Bisti/De-na-zin Wilderness, New Mexico. Maximum depositional age 66.5 ± 0.2 Ma; biostratigraphy suggests ~70-68 Ma. Collected 2008-2016.
- Name: "Diné, the Navajo word in reference to the people of the Navajo Nation, and the Latin suffix bellator, meaning warrior"; "noto ... southern"; "hesper ... western." Pronounced "dih NAY oh - BELL a tor."
- Material: ~20 scrappy elements: skull fragments, a tooth, caudals, a rib, humerus, ulna, metacarpal, a hand claw, partial femur and metatarsals, a foot claw. Velociraptor-sized.
- Quill knobs: "At least six protuberances, identified as ulnar papillae or quill knobs, lie along the ventral ulnar ridge," implying "12–14 secondary remiges." Third eudromaeosaur with quill knobs, after *Velociraptor* and *Dakotaraptor*.
- Feathers not for flight: "Their presence in non-volant dromaeosaurids of varying sizes further supports the notion that these feathers did not evolve exclusively for flight." Suggested uses: species recognition, sexual display.
- Grip: hand-claw flexor tubercle "approximately 93% height of articular surface," vs 77% *Velociraptor*, 55% *Deinonychus*. Foot claw 67% vs 20% *Velociraptor*.
- Tail: opisthocoelous proximal caudals, unknown in other dromaeosaurids, "may have allowed more mobility ... near the base," with the rest stiff.
- Injury: a gouge on the hand claw "close to, or at, the time of death"; "We speculate an altercation with another Dineobellator or other predatory theropod." A healed rib.
- Phylogeny: a velociraptorine, sister to *Tsaagan* + *Linheraptor*; implies Asia-to-North America dispersal then "vicariance" within Laramidia. The TWG dataset puts it in "a large polytomy."
- Hiatus: ~30 Myr gap in North American dromaeosaurids between *Deinonychus* (Albian) and *Dromaeosaurus* (Campanian), "due, in part, to bias."
- They flag that *Dakotaraptor* "is likely a chimera," its "furcula" "reidentified as part of a turtle plastron."

The behavioural inferences (grip, tail agility, fighting) rest on single fragmentary elements, and the diagnosis leans on features like opisthocoelous caudals in a specimen this incomplete. The second phylogenetic analysis gives no resolution. Useful as an end-Cretaceous record and a quill-knob example, less so for behaviour.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, bone close-ups on white: humerus, ulna, ulna with owl (*Megascops*) feathers placed along the quill knobs (C), caudals with a red-highlighted pit, tooth, claws with red-marked gouge. (C) is a striking teaching image of "what quill knobs held," but the feathers are a modern owl's, composited on; say so if used. Panels are small but clean.
- **Fig 2 (p3)**, black skeletal silhouette (after Scott Hartman) with the known bones in white and the actual bone photos arranged around it with leader letters, 1 m bar. Shows at a glance how little is known. Good "how much of the animal do we have" slide; clean.
- **Fig 3 (p6)**, time-calibrated tree of dromaeosaurids with colored range bars by continent (North America blue, Asia red, etc.), a grey "North American Dromaeosaurid Gap" band, silhouettes, and continent maps as a legend. Colorful and informative; type is small at full page but the gap band and colors read. Crops well.
