# Wang et al. 2025, "A new *Urbacodon* (Theropoda, Troodontidae) from the Upper Cretaceous Iren Dabasu Formation, China: implications for troodontid phylogeny and tooth biology"

Shuo Wang, Ding, Tan, Yang, Zhang & Lin Tan. *Cladistics* 41:104-134 (2025; online 2024). doi 10.1111/cla.12592.

File: `papers/Cladistics - 2024 - Wang - A new Urbacodon  Theropoda  Troodontidae  from the Upper Cretaceous Iren Dabasu Formation  China.pdf`, 31 pages. Read in full; all twenty figures viewed (ancestral-state figures 8-19 at thumbnail scale; they share one format).

## Claim

- *Urbacodon norelli*, LH PV38, Iren Dabasu Fm., Inner Mongolia; collected late 1990s by a Long Hao Institute-University of Chicago expedition. A 65 mm partial right dentary with 11 in-place replacement teeth plus one loose tooth. Named for Mark Norell. Sister to *U. itemirensis* (Uzbekistan); both sister to *Zanabazar*.
- The paper is mostly about teeth, via micro-CT. "Tooth attachment and replacement play significant roles in the feeding ecology of polyphyodont vertebrates, yet these aspects have remained largely unexplored in non-avialan paravians including troodontids."
- Replacement: "an alternating tooth replacement pattern ... with a maximum Zahnreihen-spacing estimated to be 3" (higher than *Tyrannosaurus* 1-2 and *Tarbosaurus* ~2), i.e. slower replacement. Pattern breaks between tooth positions 12 and 17.
- Teeth shed early: "functional teeth were shed even when the replacement teeth within the same alveolus reached only 10–20% of their predicted full size," the opposite extreme from *Liaoceratops* (80-90%).
- Why troodontids lack interdental plates: the septa between anterior teeth are young, fast-deposited alveolar bone, repeatedly resorbed as replacement teeth move up and forward. "This rapid turnover left insufficient time for an interdental plate to form." Prediction: same in other troodontids.
- Ancestral states: both methods find no serrations in the paravian common ancestor: "the absence of tooth denticles ... in the most recent common ancestor of paravians, even though serrations are common in the teeth of theropod dinosaurs." Serrations re-evolved in several deinonychosaur lines. Small, tightly packed anterior teeth in a common groove are linked characters unique to troodontids.
- Name history: "troodon" is Greek for "wounding tooth"; the first troodontid was a lone tooth, leading to "the inclusion of various undiagnostic 'wounding teeth' within this group."

One jaw fragment carries a new species, a phylogenetic placement and a developmental model. The tooth-replacement work is the durable part; the species and placement rest on a 65 mm dentary.

Ties to diet arguments in [Fan et al. 2026](fan-2026.md): troodontid tooth form is central to the herbivory debate.
