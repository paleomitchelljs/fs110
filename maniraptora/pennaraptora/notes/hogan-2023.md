# Hogan & Varricchio 2023, "Chthonic severance: dinosaur eggs of the Mesozoic, the significance of partially buried eggs and contact incubation precursors"

Hogan & Varricchio. *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 378:20220144 (2023). doi 10.1098/rstb.2022.0144. Part of a theme issue on nests.

File: `papers/rstb.2022.0144.pdf`, 9 pages. Read in full; all three figures viewed.

## What kind of paper

A short review plus hypothesis: how did dinosaurs get from crocodile-style guarding of buried eggs to bird-style sitting on exposed eggs? "Chthonic" = of the underground.

## Claim

- Most dinosaurs laid round, porous eggs in single-layer, fully buried clutches. Pennaraptorans changed this: "far less porous, more elongate eggs are arranged with additional complexity, and only partially buried." Oviraptorosaur eggs in rings 2-3 deep, tilted 35-40°; troodontid eggs near-upright, pointed end down.
- Evidence of partial burial (Fig 1): porosity higher on the lower part of each egg; a troodontid clutch with a mudstone layer marking a line part-way up the eggs; adults in contact with exposed tops.
- Living analogue: about 13 shorebird species (Charadriiformes) partially bury eggs. The Egyptian plover keeps eggs partly buried, sits on the exposed parts at night (clutch ~37.5°C), and in daytime buries them and wets its belly to cool them. Some don't turn their eggs, as oviraptorosaurs and troodontids probably couldn't.
- New terms: "strong contact incubation" (birds), "weak contact incubation" (adult on partially buried eggs; proposed for oviraptorosaurs and troodontids) and "indirect contact incubation" (warm adult heating a fully buried clutch through sediment).
- The hypothesis (Fig 3): an endothermic adult guarding buried eggs warms them a little through the sediment; shallower clutches benefit more; selection drives "shallower and shallower egg burial until the clutch is partially exposed"; then exposed surfaces gain more from contact, leading to fully exposed eggs.
- Experimental support (Hogan 2020): a heated surrogate kept buried emu eggs above ambient "even at depths of 15 cm"; the nearest reached 30.9°C vs 22.5°C air and 19.4°C ground. Caveats: infertile eggs, cool lab, peripheral egg layout.
- *Troodon* incubation ~74 days for a 314 g egg, "approximately halfway between what would be expected for a reptilian (107.3 days) or avian (44.4 days) egg."
- Limits they name: oxygen supply to buried, warmed embryos; perhaps eggshell ornament made air pockets.

A hypothesis paper; the indirect-incubation step has one lab experiment behind it. Gives useful vocabulary for the [Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md) vs [Yang et al. 2019](yang-2019.md) argument about whether oviraptorids warmed their eggs.
