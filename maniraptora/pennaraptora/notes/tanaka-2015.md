# Tanaka et al. 2015, "Eggshell porosity provides insight on evolution of nesting in dinosaurs"

Tanaka, Zelenitsky & Therrien. *PLoS ONE* 10(11):e0142829, 25 Nov 2015. doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0142829.

File: `papers/tanaka2015.pdf`, 23 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed.

## Claim

- Two nest types in living archosaurs: covered (eggs buried in soil or vegetation, heat from outside: crocodiles, megapodes) and open (exposed, brooded: most birds). Buried eggs need more pores for gas exchange.
- Method problem fixed: earlier work compared water-vapor conductance calculated from fossil pores with conductance measured from living eggs' water loss, "mutually incongruent" methods. Here, porosity relative to egg mass is computed the same way for 127 living species and 29 fossil taxa/ootaxa, then discriminant analysis (LDA, and a phylogenetic version, pFDA) predicts nest type.
- Result in living species: "eggs incubated in covered nests have a significantly higher eggshell porosity than those incubated in open nests."
- Fossils: titanosaurs (*Megaloolithus*), *Lourinhanosaurus* and probably ornithischian *Cairanoolithus* = covered. Oviraptorosaurs (*Macroolithus yaotunensis* etc.), *Troodon* (*Prismatoolithus*), moas = open.
- Evolution: "covered nests are likely the primitive condition for dinosaurs (and probably archosaurs)"; "open nests first evolved among non-avian theropods more derived than Lourinhanosaurus and were likely widespread in non-avian maniraptorans, well before the appearance of birds."
- Nuance: early open nesters "still partially buried their eggs in sediment. Open nests with fully exposed eggs only became widespread among Euornithes."
- Payoff: open nests and brooding "may have freed theropods from the ground-based restrictions inherent to covered nests," allowing nests in trees, on cliffs, in caves.

## Push-back

- The phylogenetic analysis is weak for the key group. pFDA classifies 0% of living covered nesters correctly (Table 6) and puts *Macroolithus* at 0.513 open vs 0.487 covered at the optimal lambda; across lambda 0.08-0.52 oviraptorosaurs flip to covered (Fig 6). The authors favor the conventional LDA for that reason.
- Ratites, with branched pores, are excluded from the living dataset.
- The open-nest inference for oviraptorosaurs is supported by other lines ([Wiemann et al. 2017](wiemann-2017.md), [Tanaka et al. 2018](tanaka-2018.md), brooding adults), so the conclusion stands better than this method alone would.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p7)**, 3D block diagrams of shell with many vs few pores, and three thin sections (crocodile, kiwi-like bird, *Troodon*) with white arrows at pores. The block diagrams explain porosity at once; the thin sections are small.
- **Fig 2 (p10)**, log porosity vs log egg mass for living covered (black) and open (white) nesters, two regression lines, block icons. Clean; the two clouds separate.
- **Fig 3 (p13)**, same plot with fossils added as colored diamonds and silhouettes (titanosaur, *Lourinhanosaurus*, oviraptorosaur, *Troodon*, moa). The paper's main slide figure: dinosaurs fall on either line. Readable.
- **Fig 4 (p14)**, misclassification rate vs Pagel's lambda, a step line. Technical; supports the caveat.
- **Fig 5 (p15)**, boxplots of discriminant scores for covered vs open nesters with fossil taxa labeled. Simple.
- **Fig 6 (p16)**, horizontal bars per fossil taxon colored orange (covered) or green (open) across lambda 0-1. Shows the oviraptorosaur bar switching color. Useful precisely because it shows the uncertainty.
- **Fig 7 (p17)**, two cladograms (archosaurs; bird orders) with nest-type squares and silhouettes at tips. Dense but legible when cropped to (A); summarizes the covered-to-open transition.
