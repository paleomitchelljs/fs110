# Ma et al. 2020, "The skull evolution of oviraptorosaurian dinosaurs: the role of niche partitioning in diversification"

Ma, Brusatte, Lü & Sakamoto. *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* 33:178-188 (2020). doi 10.1111/jeb.13557.

File: `papers/jevbio0178.pdf`, 11 pages. Read in full; all three figures viewed. Supplementary tables (most of the data) not in the folder.

## Claim

- 2D geometric morphometrics of oviraptorosaur cranium, upper beak, mandible and lower beak, plus 13 functional characters of the jaw (mechanical advantage, robustness, occlusion). Only subadult and adult skulls; perinate-only taxa excluded.
- Where skulls vary: "the snout is particularly variable." Oviraptorid snouts are downturned; "the downturned snout of oviraptorids may have been an adaptation for a powerful bite."
- Form and function: "there is a strong and significant correlation between mandibular function and mandible/lower beak shape." The cranium does not correlate with jaw function, perhaps because it serves "feeding, neurosensory and social display" at once.
- Phylogenetic signal: strong in mandible and upper beak (K > 1); none detected in the cranium, possibly "a lack of statistical power owing to small sample size (N = 11)."
- Beaks like birds: "the mechanisms governing beak shape in birds are similar to those in oviraptorosaurs, despite the independent evolution of a toothless beak in these two clades."
- Niche partitioning between clades: "The two derived oviraptorosaur clades, oviraptorids and caenagnathids, are significantly separated in morphospace and functional space, indicating that they partitioned niches." *Gigantoraptor* plots with oviraptorids despite being a caenagnathid.
- Within clades: Ganzhou (Nanxiong Fm.) oviraptorids spread widely across morphospace, "supporting the hypothesis that their coexistence was facilitated by dietary-related niche partitioning." Caenagnathids partitioned by size instead: *Gigantoraptor* mandible 46 cm long vs *Caenagnathasia* dentary width 1.56 cm, in the same formation.
- Payoff: "The diversity of skull form and function was likely key to the diversification and evolutionary success of oviraptorosaurs in the latest Cretaceous."

Small samples (11 crania, 15-19 mandibles), 2D lateral outlines only, and the crest region left out. "Niche partitioning" is inferred from separation in shape space, not from diet evidence.
