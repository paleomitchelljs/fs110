# Zheng et al. 2010, "A short-armed dromaeosaurid from the Jehol Group of China with implications for early dromaeosaurid evolution"

Zheng, Xu, You, Zhao & Dong. *Proc. R. Soc. B* 277:211-217 (online 19 Aug 2009). doi 10.1098/rspb.2009.1178.

Files: `papers/zheng2009.pdf` and `papers/A_Short-Armed_Dromaeosaurid_from_the_Jehol_Group_o.pdf` are the same article, two downloads; the embedded images are identical (200 ppi). 7 pages. Read in full; all three figures viewed. Pairs with [Lü & Brusatte 2015](lu-brusatte-2015.md).

## Claim

- *Tianyuraptor ostromi*, STM1-3, Yixian Fm., Dawangzhangzi, Lingyuan. Sub-adult. Tail 960 mm preserved, ~4.8x femur. "STM1–3 has no soft tissues preserved."
- Diagnosis: "length of the middle caudal vertebrae more than twice that of the dorsal vertebrae, a small and extremely slender furcula, and an elongated hindlimb about three times as long as the dorsal series."
- Arm short: "The forelimb is very short and slender compared to the hindlimb (53% of the hindlimb length)." Humerus ~65% of femur. Furcula "about one-fifth of the femoral length." A similar-size *Velociraptor* has arm/leg ~0.75.
- Function: "The significantly shortened forelimb of Tianyuraptor suggests a lack of aerodynamic function, which is further supported by the transversely wide coracoid ... and the small furcula."
- Phylogeny unsettled within their own results: basal microraptorine in 6 of 30 MPTs; basal to other Laurasian dromaeosaurids in the other 24. If microraptorine, "the more derived long-armed microraptorines might have independently developed some aerial capability."
- Secondary arm shortening is homoplastic: "a short radius is optimized as an autapomorphy of several paravian taxa, such as the Troodontidae, Tianyuraptor, and a clade containing most Laurasian dromaeosaurids."
- Closing line: "The presence of long-armed, four-winged Microraptor, short-armed Tianyuraptor, and various intermediate forms in the Early Cretaceous Jehol Biota ... indicates a great diversity of morphology, locomotion and ecology being present early in dromaeosaurid evolution."

Five years later *Zhenyuanlong*, with arm:leg 0.48, has large pennaceous wings. The "no aerodynamic function" inference from short arms survives, but the implied featherless or small-winged picture does not; Lü & Brusatte make this point. The two papers make a clean contrast pair.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, whole skeleton photo (a) above a line drawing (b). Photo is a tiled mosaic of slab pieces with visible joins, brown bones on tan; the long tail reads well, the body is a jumble. Drawing is clean black line on white but small labels. Weaker than *Zhenyuanlong* Fig 1. The drawing crops alone and works as an outline of the proportions.
- **Fig 2 (p3)**, five elements: skull (a), dorsals (b), furcula/sternal plate/coracoid with an arrow at the small furcula (c), ilium (d), ischium (e). All brown-on-brown, no labels; the furcula in (c) is hard to find even with the arrow. Not slide material.
- **Fig 3 (p4)**, simplified strict consensus, plain cladogram with names only. Clean but generic; small serif type. Usable only if the phylogenetic position matters.
