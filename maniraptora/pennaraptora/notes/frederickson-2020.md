# Frederickson et al. 2020, "Ontogenetic dietary shifts in *Deinonychus antirrhopus* (Theropoda; Dromaeosauridae): insights into the ecology and social behavior of raptorial dinosaurs through stable isotope analysis"

Frederickson, Engel & Cifelli. *Palaeogeography, Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology* 552:109780 (2020). doi 10.1016/j.palaeo.2020.109780.

File: `papers/10172318.pdf`, 8 pages. This is the **uncorrected proof** (watermarked "UNCORRECTED PROOF", "xxx" page numbers). Read in full; all three figures viewed.

## Claim

- The idea tested: Ostrom (1969) proposed pack hunting to explain several *Deinonychus* found with one *Tenontosaurus*. Alternative (Roach & Brinkman 2007): an "agonistic" Komodo-dragon-like mob, not a cooperative pack.
- Logic: in pack-hunting mammals, young eat what the group eats; in asocial reptiles, young eat different (smaller) prey and shift as they grow. Carbon isotopes in tooth enamel record diet.
- Samples: *Deinonychus* teeth in two size bins (<4.5 mm and >9 mm crown height), 10 from each of two microsites (Antlers Fm., Oklahoma; Cloverly Fm., Montana). This used up "29% (V1075) and 28% (V706) of the identified D. antirrhopus material." Controls: goniopholidid crocodile teeth in three sizes (n = 5, 5, 6), and *Tenontosaurus* teeth from two individuals.
- Results: crocodiles shift with size, "like their modern counterparts." *Deinonychus*: small teeth δ13C mean −8.99‰ (n = 10), large −10.38‰ (n = 10), the same pattern at both sites. *Tenontosaurus* young and adults don't differ (p = .19), as expected for a herbivore.
- Conclusion: "juvenile and adult D. antirrhopus from both formations likely consumed different prey"; adults are isotopically closer to *Tenontosaurus*; "D. antirrhopus was not a complex social hunter by modern mammalian standards."
- But not Komodo either: young dromaeosaurs are found with adults (a *Utahraptor* site with a baby, a juvenile and an adult), so hunting strategies were probably "on par with many of the avian raptors," perhaps with "ratite-like parental care."
- Their own limit: "The low sample size of D. antirrhopus teeth available for isotopic analysis hinders immediate further statistical analysis."

## Push-back

- Tooth size as age: shed teeth are not tied to individuals, and small teeth may also come from the front of an adult's jaw. The bins (<4.5, >9 mm) leave a gap to reduce overlap, but the assumption is central.
- The abstract gives the medium crocodile class mean as −10.56‰; the results give −11.20‰ (SD 0.84). Probably a proof error; check the published version.
- *Tenontosaurus* comes from a different bonebed and only two individuals.
- A good teaching case: the test excludes one model (wolf pack) but leaves several social arrangements open, and the authors use a separate line (juveniles found with adults) to argue against the opposite extreme. Pairs with the oviraptorosaur bonebed in [Funston et al. 2016](funston-2016.md).
