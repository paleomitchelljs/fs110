# Li et al. 2012, "Reconstruction of *Microraptor* and the evolution of iridescent plumage"

Li, Gao, Meng, Clarke, Shawkey, D'Alba, Pei, Ellison, Norell & Vinther. *Science* 335:1215-1219 (2012). doi 10.1126/science.1213780.

File: `papers/li2012.pdf`, 6 pages (a reprint with a cover page; the first text page is the end of an unrelated lunar paper). Read in full; supplement not in the folder.

## Claim

- BMNHC PH881, *Microraptor*, western Liaoning. 26 samples (~1 mm²) from feathered areas; 20 had melanosome imprints.
- Comparison set: melanosomes from living birds (n = 168 samples). Iridescent feathers have longer, narrower melanosomes than black ones: "~1160 versus 1000 nm," "~211 versus 279 nm," aspect ratio "~5.4 versus 3.5." Quadratic discriminant analysis "predicted color of extant bird feathers with 82% accuracy."
- Fossil: melanosomes "predicted as either iridescent or black ... with 58 to 100% probabilities." Ten samples show "sheetlike, end-to-end orientation." Reconstruction: "black with a glossy, weakly iridescent sheen."
- Limits: keratin not preserved, so no specific hue; other pigments "cannot be excluded"; six samples without melanosomes treated as preservational.
- Side claim: glossy plumage conflicts with reading *Microraptor* as nocturnal from its scleral ring.
- Tail: the longest feathers are "a midline pair," so the fan is "appreciably narrower" than earlier lift-generating reconstructions; "the graduated tail shape with a pair of long, midline feathers may be consistent with an ornamental or signaling function." Based on PH881 plus "eight previously published specimens."
- Origin: possibly ancestral to Paraves, but "multiple origins of iridescence in early Paraves must be considered"; iridescence "could evolve easily."

Fits the display slide: a signal-colour claim from one specimen and a living-bird classifier, plus a tail shape that weakens an aerodynamic reading and supports display. Students should see that "iridescent" is a classification with stated error (82% on living birds), not an observation.

Pairs with [Li et al. 2010](li-2010.md) (*Anchiornis* colour, same method family), [Persons et al. 2014](persons-2014.md), [Clark et al. 2026](clark-2026.md).
