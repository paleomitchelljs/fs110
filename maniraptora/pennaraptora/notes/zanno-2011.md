# Zanno et al. 2011, "A new troodontid theropod, *Talos sampsoni* gen. et sp. nov., from the Upper Cretaceous Western Interior Basin of North America"

Zanno, Varricchio, O'Connor, Titus & Knell. *PLoS ONE* 6(9):e24487, 19 Sep 2011. doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0024487.

File: `papers/plostrood2.pdf`, 20 pages. Read in full; all fifteen figures viewed (at thumbnail scale for the bone plates).

## Claim

- *Talos sampsoni*, UMNH VP 19479, Kaiparowits Fm., Grand Staircase-Escalante, Utah, late Campanian. Partial postcranium: no skull. ~38 kg by distal tibia width. Name: "the mythological, fleetfooted protector of Crete, often depicted as winged, who succumbed to a wound on" (the heel/ankle in the myth; the extracted text cuts off), a nod to the injured foot.
- "the first definitive North American troodontid from the Late Cretaceous to be named in over 75 years."
- *Troodon* as a wastebasket: *T. formosus* rests on "a single tooth" and "may represent a nomen dubium"; it is "reported to have inhabited most of western North America" across ~20 Myr and 4000 km. "We find the likelihood that these specimens represent a single species of troodontid theropod to be low." Prediction: "the taxonomic diversity of Late Cretaceous coelurosaurians including troodontids in North America is presently underestimated."
- Age and size: three or more LAGs, "minimally, in its fourth year of growth," perhaps sixth; growth slowing but not stopped. A same-length *Troodon* tibia shows two growth lines, so "Talos sampsoni would likely have plateaued at a significantly smaller size than Troodon formosus."
- The injury: left pedal phalanx II-1 has a bony collar (callus) and a drainage opening (cloaca), "consistent with suppurative osteomyelitis," after trauma; healing took "weeks to months." Only the sickle-claw digit is affected.
- Functional reading: supports use of digit II in "prey acquisition/manipulation and/or conspecific aggression," and "the lack of extensive remodeling across the remainder of the hind limb is consistent with the hypothesis that the hypertrophied digit II ... was not extensively employed in locomotion."
- Phylogeny: in a derived Late Cretaceous clade with *Troodon*, *Saurornithoides*, *Zanabazar*, but in three positions across six trees; "little current phylogenetic support for any particular MPT."

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p3)**, skeletal silhouette (black/grey) with the preserved bones in red. Clean, readable at a glance; shows the missing skull. Good slide image.
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, stratigraphic column of the Kaiparowits Fm. with dated ash beds and specimen levels. Clear but specialist.
- **Fig 3 (p5)**, skeletal elements of *Talos* and *Troodon* side by side. Brown bones on white, small.
- **Fig 4 (p6)**, four regression plots of metatarsal and phalanx proportions, *Troodon* (grey) vs *Talos* (red) with confidence bands. Clean; *Talos* sits off the *Troodon* line. Usable for "how you separate two species from foot bones."
- **Figs 5-12 (pp7-12)**, bone plates: vertebrae, pelvis, femur, tibia, tarsals, metatarsals, foot phalanges. Brown on white or grey, well lit, with stipple drawings in Fig 12. Reference only.
- **Fig 13 (p13)**, micro-CT renderings of the injured toe bone: gold-colored surface models, purple outline of the original cortex, cross-sections with green arrows at the callus boundary. The paper's most striking image; shows healing clearly. Crop the renders (A, C, F-H) and one cross-section.
- **Fig 14 (p14)**, fibula histology with LAG arrows. Dark, rust-colored, small; hard to read at slide size.
- **Fig 15 (p15)**, cladogram with dashed alternatives and colored support values, *Talos* in red. Small.
