# Xu et al. 2011, "A short-armed troodontid dinosaur from the Upper Cretaceous of Inner Mongolia and its implications for troodontid evolution"

Xu, Tan, Sullivan, Han & Xiao. *PLoS ONE* 6(9):e22916, 7 Sep 2011. doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0022916.

File: `papers/ponetrood.pdf`, 12 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed.

## Claim

- *Linhevenator tani*, LH V0021, Wulansuhai Fm. (Campanian), Bayan Mandahu, Inner Mongolia. Skull, six dorsals, scapula, humerus, ischia, femur, nearly complete foot. "badly weathered" (weathering stages 2-3). Femur ~240 mm, mass ~23 kg: "a relatively large troodontid."
- Short arms: "the humeral/femoral length ratio of about 0.4 indicates that this taxon is probably among the shortest-armed non-avian theropods." Comparisons: *Austroraptor* 0.46, *Compsognathus* 0.44, *T. rex* 0.29, *Aucasaurus* 0.35. Previously measured troodontids 0.52-0.65.
- But a strong arm: big deltopectoral crest with a lateral fossa, giving large moment arms; motions "with obvious potential utility in predation or in other possible activities such as digging and climbing." They hedge: "It is uncertain how L. tani actually used its forelimbs."
- Sickle toe convergence: in *Linhevenator* and *Troodon*, digit II has a short II-2 with a big heel and a large ungual, "more characteristic of the Dromaeosauridae." "dromaeosaurids and derived troodontids may have used their pedes for predation in a similar way, resulting in the independent evolution in each case of a highly specialized second pedal digit."
- A possible trend: if *Troodon* also had very short arms, "greatly reduced arms may be characteristic of a highly derived troodontid clade," like tyrannosauroids and alvarezsauroids. "many more data ... will be needed."
- Mosaic: shares cranial traits with perinate rather than adult *Byronosaurus*, though adult.

The arm ratio uses one humerus and one estimated femur; no forearm or hand preserved. The "among the shortest-armed" headline is reasonable but rests on two bones.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, the specimen as a block of jumbled, weathered brown bone with a few labels (20 mm bar). Hard to read; not slide material.
- **Fig 2 (p3)**, skull and jaw in several views, brown bone on a grey background, labeled. Weathered and grooved; an honest image of a poorly preserved skull.
- **Fig 3 (p4)**, teeth under the microscope, cream on black, denticles visible. Crisp; a good "troodontid tooth" image with big hooked denticles.
- **Fig 4 (p4)**, dorsal vertebrae. Skip.
- **Fig 5 (p5)**, scapula and the short, robust humerus in four views. The humerus panels could support the short-arm point if set beside the femur, but they are not shown together.
- **Fig 6 (p6)**, pelvis and hindlimb, including the foot and digit II. Moderately clear; the digit II ungual panel is the one to crop for the convergence point.
- **Fig 7 (p8)**, plain cladogram of troodontids. Small.
