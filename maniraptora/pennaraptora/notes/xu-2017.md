# Xu et al. 2017, "Mosaic evolution in an asymmetrically feathered troodontid dinosaur with transitional features"

Xu, Currie, Pittman, Xing, Meng, Lü, Hu & Yu. *Nature Communications* 8:14972, 2 May 2017. doi 10.1038/ncomms14972.

File: `papers/ncomms14972.pdf`, 12 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed. Supplementary Information not in the folder.

## Claim

- *Jianianhualong tengi*, DLXH 1218, Yixian Fm., Baicai Gou, Liaoning. Named for a sponsoring company ("Jianianhua") and Ms Fangfang Teng "who secured the specimen." Adult by vertebral fusion. ~112 cm with tail restored; ~2.4 kg.
- Why it matters: earlier feathered "troodontids" (*Anchiornis*, *Xiaotingia*, *Eosinopteryx*, *Jinfengopteryx*) have had their troodontid status questioned, so "feathers have yet to be found in an unquestionable troodontid taxon." *Jianianhualong* "is clearly a troodontid," confirming "feathers of modern aspect in this key theropod group."
- Plumage: large pennaceous feathers on arms, legs and tail, the tail a "frond-like feathery tail, a primitive morphotype." Mid-tail feathers "~120 mm long."
- The asymmetry claim rests on one feather: "one lateral caudal feather has relatively strong asymmetry, with the trailing vane about twice as wide as the leading vane." Arm-feather symmetry "cannot be determined."
- Inference: "Assuming the most parsimonious explanation in which feather asymmetry only evolved once, this first troodontid record suggests that feather asymmetry was ancestral to Paraves." They add the caveat: with scansoriopterygid and dromaeosaurid states entered as equivocal, "the reconstructed ancestral condition for paravian arm feather symmetry is also equivocal."
- Function hedged: asymmetry may have helped "locomotion of Jianianhualong on the ground and/or through the air, although in isolation these two aforementioned characteristics do not support either passive gliding or active, powered flight."
- Mosaic: "forelimbs and a pelvis closely resembling those of basal troodontids, but a cranium and hindlimbs that are more similar to those of derived troodontids." *Sinusonasus* shows the reverse pattern. Possible "modular evolution," likened to *Darwinopterus* and Hox domains; they concede it "might have been produced by independent evolution of characters rather than the concerted evolution of characters within modules."
- No ossified sternum, no uncinate processes. Phylogeny: strict consensus of 3,066 trees, intermediate among troodontids.

"Ancestral to Paraves" is a large claim from one tail feather in a privately secured specimen whose arm feathers can't be scored.
