# O'Connor et al. 2025, "Description and osteohistology of two early immature enantiornithines (Aves: Ornithothoraces) from the Early Cretaceous Jehol Biota"

O'Connor, Atterholt, Bailleul, Wang, Kuo & Zhou. *Geobios* 90:103-122 (online Aug 2024). doi 10.1016/j.geobios.2024.05.004.

File: `papers/1-s2.0-S0016699524000640-main.pdf`, 20 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed.

## Claim

- Two hatchling-size enantiornithines, IVPP V15686 (humerus 17 mm) and IVPP V15575 (humerus 15.7 mm), Jiufotang Fm., ~120 Ma. Tibia sampled in both, humerus in V15575.
- Why they're immature: "incomplete ossification of the sternum ..., absence of fusion in certain compound elements (e.g., the carpometacarpus, tibiotarsus, tarsometatarsus, pygostyle ...), and the heavily striated and pitted surfaces of the bones."
- Terms, after Griffin et al. 2021: "morphologically immature/mature" and "osteohistologically immature/mature" kept separate. "Morphologically early immature is here defined as the complete absence of fusion between the four sternal anlagen and incomplete ossification of the pygostyle or a proportionately elongate pygostyle." "Osteohistologically early immature is defined as a complete lack of ICL and OCL in combination with a relatively high porosity."
- Decoupling: "IVPP V15575, although slightly smaller in size than IVPP V15686, is osteohistologically more mature but morphologically less mature."
- Fusion sequence: the pygostyle "must begin to coalesce very early in post-hatching ontogeny," before the sternum. "The synsacrum is the next compound bone to form, followed by the carpometacarpus, tibiotarsus and tarsometatarsus, which only become fused in mature individuals."
- Precocial flight: "the shift towards greater maturity in the forelimb relative to the hindlimb observed in IVPP V15575 probably reflects the apomorphic evolution of locomotor super-precociality observed in enantiornithines." In living birds without precocial flight "the bone tissue of the femur is typically more mature than the humerus at hatching."
- Caveats they state: the humerus/tibia comparison "is equivocal without a complete humeral cortex and uncrushed preservation of both elements." The tibia stands in for the femur. No megapode histology exists for comparison. A *Megapodius* embryo CT "only somewhat supports this interpretation."
- Plasticity: "morphological and osteohistological maturity are decoupled in Enantiornithes"; "slight variations in ossification patterns cannot be relied upon to reflect interspecific patterns."
- Side claim: the scale bar in Chinsamy & Elzanowski 2001's *Gobipteryx* embryo section is probably wrong, since it implies a cortex ~3x thicker than a same-size Jehol hatchling.

Push-back: the forelimb-more-mature claim rests on one specimen, two crushed fragments, and a tibia-for-femur swap. The authors flag each of these. It is a hypothesis, not a result.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p4)**, V15686 photo over line drawing. Black bone on grey slab, cleanly lit; the drawing is a grey-on-grey silhouette with white labels. Photo half crops well as a "what a hatchling enantiornithine looks like" image. Caption scale bar reads "10 cm" for a 17 mm humerus; must be 1 cm. Don't reuse the bar.
- **Fig 2 (p5)**, V15575 slab and counterslab with drawings. Photo is low-contrast brown; the bones are hard to read without the drawing. Drawing panels (C, D) are busy with label clutter. Weaker than Fig 1 for slides.
- **Fig 3 (p6)**, close-ups of unfused pygostyle (A, B) and sternal anlagen (C, D). A is sharp and readable (separate vertebrae visible in the pygostyle). C and D need the labels to see anything; D especially is a faint smear. A crops alone.
- **Fig 4 (p10)**, V15686 tibia section, stained red, with two enlargements. Good color and clear large vascular canals in B and C. A strip-shaped crushed section; the student will not see "a bone" without being told it is a collapsed tube.
- **Fig 5 (p11)**, V15575 tibia and humerus sections. Same style, six panels; E (polarized) and F (osteocyte lacunae, incipient osteons) are the clearest. Too dense whole; F crops.
- **Fig 6 (p14)**, chart of compound-bone fusion stages (sternum, carpometacarpus, tarsals, synsacrum, pygostyle) across eight specimens 0a-3, with thin-section thumbnails on top. Line drawings, very clean, white background. Best figure in the paper for teaching the fusion sequence; reads left to right. Thumbnails on top row are tiny; crop them off or keep as decoration. Text labels small at slide size; zoom in on 3-4 rows if needed.
- **Fig 7 (p17)**, whole-bone cross sections of four living birds arranged altricial to precocial with an arrow, plus the two enantiornithine sections at the bottom. Visually striking comparison, but the fossil sections are crushed strips beside round modern rings, and K is an "attempted reconstruction ... shape not accurate." Use the left column (A, C, E, G) with the arrow; the fossil rows invite a comparison the data can't fully support.
