# Wang & Zhou 2023, "Low morphological disparity and decelerated rate of limb size evolution close to the origin of birds"

Wang & Zhou. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 7:1257-1266 (2023). doi 10.1038/s41559-023-02091-z.

File: `papers/Low-morphological-disparity-and-decelerated-rate-of-limb-size-evolution-close-to-the-origin.pdf`, 23 pages (with extended data). Read the main text and methods; extended data figures viewed.

## Claim

- Data: lengths of six limb bones (humerus, ulna, metacarpal II, femur, tibia, metatarsal III) in 109 taxa (55 avialans, 17 other paravians, 37 other theropods); specimens without all six complete were left out; subadults or adults only.
- Trees: four informal supertrees moving *Anchiornis* (avialan or troodontid) and scansoriopterygids; "Our main conclusions are not strongly impacted by phylogenetic assumptions."
- Result: "our results show a shift to low disparity and decelerated rate near the origin of avialans that is largely ascribed to the evolutionarily constrained forelimb." Hindlimb shows no shift at Avialae. Fast branches: scansoriopterygids, oviraptorosaurs, later ornithuromorphs.
- Reading: "the bony 'blueprint' of a typical bird wing was formed before the origin of avialans"; the wing proportions needed for flight constrain variation.
- Sampling: longer non-avialan history could inflate their disparity and dilute their rates, but for early avialans the expected bias runs the other way, so the result "cannot be simply ascribed to sampling bias."
- On an earlier limb study (ref. 7, 92 taxa): "the results cannot be directly compared because of different methodologies."

Against Brusatte 2014 (discrete characters, fast rates at birds): a different kind of data on the same transition gives a slowdown, not a burst. Not a contradiction of fact: characters count novelties anywhere in the skeleton; limb lengths measure proportions. Students should ask what each rate measures.

Pairs with [Brusatte et al. 2014](brusatte-2014.md), [Lee et al. 2014](lee-2014.md), [Hartman et al. 2019](hartman-2019.md).
