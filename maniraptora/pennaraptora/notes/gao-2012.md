# Gao et al. 2012, "A second soundly sleeping dragon: new anatomical details of the Chinese troodontid *Mei long* with implications for phylogeny and taphonomy"

Gao, Morschhauser, Varricchio, Liu & Zhao. *PLoS ONE* 7(9):e45203, 27 Sep 2012. doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0045203.

File: `papers/meilong2.pdf`, 17 pages. Read in full; all eight figures viewed. Follows [Xu & Norell 2004](xu-norell-2004.md).

## Claim

- Second *Mei long*, DNHM D2154 (the figure captions say "D2514"; the text uses D2154), Lujiatun beds. ~325 mm long, "not more than 400 mm"; elements average 83% of the holotype. Mass ~420 g.
- Posture: "nearly identical to the avian sleeping posture of the type specimen," mirrored: "in DNHM D2154 the neck arches to the left rather than to the right as in the holotype." Footprint 120 x 83 mm.
- Three specimens, two species: "The presence of three specimens representing two troodontid species in nearly identical positions suggests that the position of these animals is not random." The third is *Sinornithoides*, from aeolian sandstone in the Ordos Basin.
- Maturity: fused neurocentral sutures and sacrum, smooth dense bone. Histology: "two zones of fibrolamellar tissue separated by a band of circumferential and largely avascular bone with a single line of arrested growth (LAG)" plus peripheral lamellar bone read as an external fundamental system. Age "minimally over two and possibly over four years old."
- Juvenile-looking features in an adult (unfused frontals and nasals, free cervical ribs, short steep snout): "One possible conclusion is that Mei long is a paedomorphically small troodontid."
- Growth: "the size reduction of paravians relative to more primitive theropods was apparently not accomplished by a major change in growth. Mei long, despite a body size orders of magnitude smaller, exhibits multi-year growth ... as found in more basal theropods like Megapnosaurus ..., Allosaurus, and tyrannosaurids."
- Taphonomy, new argument: the specimens lack opisthotonic (death-throe) and "pugilistic" (heat) postures. Life posture "contradicts burial in a low energy lahar ... Even a low energy lahar is likely to move a small animal out of a perimortem position." Alternatives: "sheltering within a burrow" or "protective responses to volcanic and eolian events."
- Caveat: "Like many Liaoning specimens, the new specimen also lacks extensive taphonomic and stratigraphic data, making further behavioral inference problematic."
- Phylogeny: basal troodontid, polytomy with *Byronosaurus* and *Talos*. Recoding "do[es] not significantly alter the position of Mei long."

## Figures

All at 200 ppi.

- **Fig 1 (p2)**, line drawing (A) over photo (B), dorsal view. The photo is a pale grey-pink lump; without the drawing the curled pose is hard to see. The drawing is dense with labels. Pair it with the holotype's Fig 1a ([Xu & Norell 2004](xu-norell-2004.md)), which reads far better; this one is the mirror-image second example.
- **Fig 2 (p4)**, photo-drawing pairs: skull (A), sacrum and ilia (B), pelvis (C). Clean drawings with grey fill, colored photos with pink-red tint. B shows the very wide back sacrum. Anatomy slides only.
- **Fig 3 (p5)**, cervical 6 and mid caudals, line drawings. Small; skip.
- **Fig 4 (p7)**, left forelimb and shoulder girdle, greyscale photo, sharp. Skip unless needed.
- **Fig 5 (p9 top)**, right manus and skull, color; the long metacarpal III is hard to see. Skip.
- **Fig 6 (p9 bottom)**, articulated hindlimb and distal tibia, greyscale. Skip.
- **Fig 7 (p11)**, tibia and fibula cross-section under crossed polars: purple-orange-blue, LAG labeled, medullary cavity filled with white spar. The best figure. A (whole section) shows the single LAG ring and the peripheral lamellar band clearly; C is a high-magnification view. Crop A alone for "a half-kilo dinosaur grew for 2-4 years."
- **Fig 8 (p13)**, strict consensus with Bremer values, black italic type. Plain; usable only to locate *Mei*.
