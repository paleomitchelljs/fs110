# Wiemann et al. 2017, "Dinosaur origin of egg color: oviraptors laid blue-green eggs"

Wiemann, Yang, Sander, Schneider, Engeser, Kath-Schorr, Müller & Sander. *PeerJ* 5:e3706, 29 Aug 2017. doi 10.7717/peerj.3706.

File: `papers/peerj-3706.pdf`, 20 pages. Read in full; all five figures viewed.

## Claim

- Bird egg colors come from two pigments: protoporphyrin (PP, reddish-brown) and biliverdin (BV, blue-green). Crocodiles bury eggs and have no pigment. Earlier view: "colored eggs evolved within crown birds."
- Method: liquid chromatography mass spectrometry on three *Macroolithus yaotunensis* eggshells (referred to *Heyuannia huangi*) from Henan, Jiangxi and Guangdong, plus emu eggshell and one sediment sample as controls.
- Result: "the first record of the avian eggshell pigments protoporphyrin and biliverdin in the eggshells of Late Cretaceous oviraptorid dinosaurs." Sediment control showed none.
- Concentrations: BV 1-6 nmol/g, PP 1-2 nmol/g. Emu: 266 nmol/g BV. Plotted raw, the fossils fall in "the visibly olive-green color range" among unspotted bird eggs. A "taphonomic correction" for BV loss then "approximates an intensively blue–greenish oviraptorid egg color."
- Nest: porosity highest mid-egg, lower at the blunt end, so eggs sat "partially stuck in the nesting material, with their blunt ends exposed," a partially open nest. Colored eggs fit open nests needing camouflage, by analogy with emu and cassowary.
- Behaviour: blue-green eggs in birds are linked to "paternal care, communal nesting, and postmating sexual signaling," consistent with paternal brooding proposed for oviraptorids.
- Microbes ruled out by the sediment control and by different BV/PP ratios among samples: "the most parsimonious conclusion is that we have documented oviraptorid eggshell pigments."

## Push-back

- The title color is not what was measured. Raw values plot olive-green; "blue-green" comes from an assumed correction for BV loss. The direction of the correction is reasonable; its size is not measured.
- One sediment sample controls for contamination across three eggs from three basins.
- Fossil BV is ~1/50 to 1/250 of emu levels.
- Homology vs convergence of egg color is left open: "future investigations of pigmentation in other eumaniraptoran eggshells are required."

Later work by the same group extended pigment detection to other dinosaurs; this is the first report and the most cautious about its own correction.

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p5)**, map of China with three provinces in red (A); a green egg silhouette with zonal conductance values (B); top view of a real oviraptorid clutch, a ring of dark paired eggs (C); and a drawing of the reconstructed open nest with teal eggs in a red-brown mound (D). (C) is an excellent clutch photo; (D) is the reconstruction most people will remember. Both crop well.
- **Fig 2 (p6)**, photo of a pair of *Heyuannia* eggs, dark grey with red sediment, beside a diagram of sampling zones with measurements. Sharp; shows real egg size and shape.
- **Fig 3 (p8)**, mass-spec chromatograms (colored traces) for BV and PP in standards, emu, fossils, sediment. Technical.
- **Fig 4 (p11)**, BV vs PP scatter of bird eggs (yellow unspotted, blue spotted clusters) with fossil points in red, and egg color swatches, including "preserved fossil color" vs reconstructed. Shows the gap between measured and reconstructed color if read carefully. Busy.
- **Fig 5 (p12)**, archosaur cladogram (crocodiles to thrushes) with an egg icon (shape and color) and a nest icon (buried vs open) at each tip, branch colors showing pigment. Clean, readable; a good summary slide for "when eggs got color and nests opened up," with the caveat that the oviraptorid color is reconstructed.
