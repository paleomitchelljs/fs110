# Tsuihiji et al. 2014, "An exquisitely preserved troodontid theropod with new information on the palatal structure from the Upper Cretaceous of Mongolia"

Tsuihiji, Barsbold, Watabe, Tsogtbaatar, Chinzorig, Fujiyama & Suzuki. *Naturwissenschaften* 101:131-142 (2014). doi 10.1007/s00114-014-1143-9.

File: `papers/tsuihiji2014.pdf`, 12 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed. Supplementary material not in the folder.

## Claim

- *Gobivenator mongoliensis*, MPC-D 100/86, Djadokhta Fm., Dzamin Khond, Gobi. "the most complete skeleton of a Late Cretaceous troodontid presently known." Missing mid-cervicals, forearms and hands, parts of the hind limbs.
- Troodontid by an anteroventrally inclined quadrate, a tall foramen magnum, grooved distal caudals, and an asymmetrical foot (slender metatarsal II, robust IV). Fully arctometatarsalian. Teeth unserrated.
- Autapomorphies: "a fossa on the surangular in front of the posterior surangular foramen" and "a pointed anterior end of the fused parietal that wedges into a V-shaped notch between the contralateral frontals."
- Palate: first complete troodontid palate. "Overall morphology of the palate in Gobivenator resembles those of dromaeosaurids and Archaeopteryx, showing an apparent trend of elongation of the pterygoid process of the palatine and reduction of the pterygopalatine suture toward the basal Avialae."
- Kinesis: "the skull of Gobivenator would have been akinetic but had already acquired prerequisites for later evolution of cranial kinesis in birds, such as the loss of the epipterygoid and reduction in contact areas among bones."
- Not a straight-line trend: some palatal traits are more bird-like in dromaeosaurids than in troodontids, so "the distribution of derived characteristics of the palate appears to be complex and cannot be explained solely by a simple, progressive evolutionary trend toward Avialae."
- Phylogeny: in a derived clade with *Saurornithoides*, *Zanabazar*, *Troodon*; "support values for each node are rather low."

The *Archaeopteryx* palate in their comparison is the older reconstruction; [O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025-archaeopteryx.md) revise it using the Chicago specimen and cite *Gobivenator* for the troodontid comparison.
