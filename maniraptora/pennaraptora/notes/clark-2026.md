# Clark et al. 2026, "Hyperelongate ornamental tail feathers in a new early Cretaceous enantiornithine bird"

Clark, O'Connor, Xiaoli Wang, Yan Wang, Pruett-Jones, Zhang, Xing Wang, Zheng & Zhou. *PLoS ONE* 21(5):e0347641, 27 May 2026. doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0347641.

File: `papers/journal.pone.0347641.pdf`, 19 pages. Read in full; all seven figures viewed. Supplementary figures not in the folder.

## Claim

- *Plumadraco bankoorum*, STM11-4, Jiufotang Fm., Jianchang County, 121 Ma. Bohaiornithid enantiornithine, 112-144 g, thrush-sized. "the Banko's feather dragon."
- Tail feathers are rachis-dominated feathers (RDFs), an extinct feather type with a "ventrally-open 'C'-shaped cross-section." Here two RDFs 293 mm long on a 149 mm body: "1.97 times that of the body." Previous record *Junornis* ~1.6x; others "1.0–1.6 times."
- Rackets small: "8% the length of the feather compared to 10–54% in other enantiornithines." The rachis fades halfway into the racket, which they call "enfeeblement": a weak tip that would flex and "flicker," as in peacock rump coverts.
- Not aerodynamic: "RDFs do not form a cohesive aerodynamic surface and would incur drag, strongly suggesting that they were either sexually-selected ornaments or for non-sexual conspecific communication."
- Dimorphism evidence from other taxa: "In Early Cretaceous birds for which multiple soft-tissue preserving specimens are known (e.g., Protopteryx, Confuciusornis), RDFs are present in ~40–60% of individuals, a percentage consistent with sexual dimorphism."
- Display by tail pumping: enantiornithine pygostyles show well-developed levator and depressor caudae attachments, reduced side-to-side muscles, so displays were up-and-down "pumping."
- Nesting argument: enantiornithines laid in open ground nests and had precocial young; in living birds with open nests, females tend to be cryptic, and elongate-tailed monomorphic birds are mostly cavity nesters. Hence "STM11−4 represents a male individual whose RDF morphology and length were utilized in courtship displays."
- Caveat: "direct evidence of enantiornithine nesting strategies during the Early Cretaceous and additional information concerning enantiornithine tail musculature are needed." The skeleton itself is "poorly preserved."

The male identification rests on analogy to living birds and on nesting evidence from Late Cretaceous sites 35-50 Myr younger. Plausible, but the specimen itself carries no sex indicator (no medullary bone sampled, no eggs).

Relevance to pennaraptorans: ties to the display-first readings of pennaceous feathers ([Talori et al. 2018](talori-2018.md), [Lü & Brusatte 2015](lu-brusatte-2015.md), [Li et al. 2010](li-2010.md)) and to precocial enantiornithine development ([O'Connor et al. 2025](oconnor-2025.md), [Haase et al. 2026](haase-2026.md)).

## Figures

- **Fig 1 (p4)**, whole specimen (A, a tall narrow panel with the two streamers running down), body close-up (B), line drawing with bones white and soft tissue brown (C). B is a sharp, readable bird with feather halo.
- **Fig 2-4 (pp6-9)**, skull, forelimb/girdle, hindlimb close-ups with grey-fill drawings. Anatomy only.

- **Fig 6 (p11)**, tree (A), close-ups of streamer and racket (B, C), King-of-Saxony bird-of-paradise head plume (D), and Ville Sinkkonen's life restoration (E): green-black bird on a mossy branch with two long racketed streamers. E is the strongest slide image in the paper; crops alone.
- **Fig 7 (p13)**, six feather tips side by side (*Plumadraco*, peacock, racket-tailed parrot, *Orienantius*, motmot, drongo) with arrows at where the rachis ends, grouped under "Distally-enfeebled rachis" and "Continuous rachis" bars. Clear comparison; works as a slide with the two labels.
