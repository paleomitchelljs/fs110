# Zelenitsky & Therrien 2008, "Phylogenetic analysis of reproductive traits of maniraptoran theropods and its implications for egg parataxonomy"

Zelenitsky & Therrien. *Palaeontology* 51(4):807-816 (2008). doi 10.1111/j.1475-4983.2008.00770.x.

File: `papers/Palaeontology - 2008 - ZELENITSKY - PHYLOGENETIC ANALYSIS OF REPRODUCTIVE TRAITS OF MANIRAPTORAN THEROPODS AND ITS.pdf`, 10 pages. Read in full; both text-figures viewed.

## Claim

- Method: a cladistic analysis using only egg and nesting characters, 12 of them (adult contact with eggs, ornamentation, shape, asymmetry, shell layers, number of oviducts, egg orientation, clutch layout, pore shape, squamatic structure). Seven taxa: *Maiasaura* and titanosaurs as outgroup; *Citipati*, *Troodon*, two orphan ootaxa (*Protoceratopsidovum*, *Parvoolithus*) and the guineafowl *Numida*.
- Result: three trees of 18 steps, consistency index 1.0. *Citipati* (oviraptorid) at the base, then *Troodon*, *Protoceratopsidovum*, and *Parvoolithus* + *Numida*. Same order as skeletal phylogenies, "consistent with phylogenies derived from skeletal characters."
- Traits: "several avian reproductive traits, such as adults sitting on eggs, asymmetrical eggs, unornamented eggshell surface, and complex eggshell ultrastructure, were already present in non-avian maniraptorans." Not kept by modern birds: laying "two eggs at a time" and eggs set "subvertically or subhorizontally" in sediment.
- Sitting on eggs: ring and single-layer clutches "permitted adults to sit atop them, a behaviour that would have been difficult to accomplish on randomly distributed, stacked eggs." When heat incubation evolved remains "unknown."
- Ornament and burial: the idea that ornament keeps buried pores clear predicts unornamented eggs in open nests, but buried-bottom *Troodon* eggs are smooth and ornamented oviraptorid eggs may have been in open nests, so the function "requires further investigation."
- Orphan eggs: *Protoceratopsidovum* falls inside Maniraptora, so "Protoceratopsidovum is a misnomer" (not a protoceratopsian); *Parvoolithus* "probably belonged to a Cretaceous bird."
- Parataxonomy: the egg classification's top ranks (basic types, morphotypes) are "unnatural groupings"; "we propose that basic types and morphotypes be abandoned because oofamilies are adequate."
- Coding fights: they correct codings in Grellet-Tinner & Makovicky (2006) (e.g. *Troodon* eggs coded horizontal "should be subvertical") and could not reproduce Garcia et al. (2006).

## Push-back

- Seven taxa and 12 characters; the authors admit egg trees "completely collapse within four additional steps." A consistency index of 1.0 here reflects a tiny matrix, not strong support.
- Adult contact is coded "absent" for hadrosaurs and titanosaurs "based on the assumption that these large dinosaurs did not sit on their eggs," and present for *Troodon* on one adult-near-clutch specimen. The key behavioral character is partly assumed.
- Paired eggs in *Troodon* are coded present despite Grellet-Tinner et al.'s challenge; see the same dispute in [Varricchio & Jackson 2016](varricchio-jackson-2016.md).
- Good for teaching how a "character" is defined and coded, and how two groups can code the same egg differently.
