# Fanti, Currie & Badamgarav 2012, "New specimens of *Nemegtomaia* from the Baruungoyot and Nemegt Formations (Late Cretaceous) of Mongolia"

Fanti, Currie & Badamgarav. *PLoS ONE* 7(2):e31330 (2012). doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0031330. Open access (CC BY).

File: `papers/nemegtonia.pdf`, 16 pages. Read the abstract, posture, taphonomy, and nest sections in full; skull and hand descriptions and stratigraphy skimmed. All figures viewed.

## Claim

- MPC-D 107/15, upper Baruungoyot Fm., Nemegt: "a nest of eggs with the skeleton of the assumed parent of *Nemegtomaia* on top in brooding position." Has a skull, unlike the *Citipati* nesters.
- "*Nemegtomaia* represents the fourth known genus of oviraptorids (*Citipati*, 8; cf. *Machairasaurus*, 5; *Oviraptor*, 1; *Nemegtomaia*, this paper), the first within the Ingeniinae clade, found on nests."
- Contact: skull, neck, left humerus, left femur and both feet "rest directly on or within 5 mm from the surfaces of the eggs." "The direct apposition of the skeleton on the nest ... shows that the nest was not completely covered by sand."
- Posture differs a little: "The forelimbs do not extend laterally as much as observed in other known nesting oviraptorid dinosaurs." Legs "folded up into a crouching position."
- Taphonomy: the body was not buried at once. Five steps, including "partial burial and subsequent deterioration," "gradual decomposition under xeric conditions," "intense scavenging by insect colonies," then "final burial." Dermestid beetle traces on the bones.
- Nest: about 90 x 100 cm collected; "does not preserve a single complete egg, nor have any embryonic skeletal elements been recovered"; seven eggs identified in a lower layer; eggs 14-16 cm long, elongatoolithid. Eggs not in the usual pairs, "likely displaced during early stages of burial."
- On the group: cites earlier proposals that "several individuals gathered eggs into a single nest and arranged them so they could be protected by one individual, possibly a male."

What it adds: a second oviraptorid lineage on nests, from a different formation. What it lacks: embryos, an intact egg, and quick burial; the insect damage means the posture was not sealed instantly, so the brooding reading leans on the resemblance to *Citipati*.

Pairs with [Clark et al. 1999](clark-1999.md) and [Bi et al. 2021](bi-2021.md).
