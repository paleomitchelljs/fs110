# Brusatte et al. 2014, "Gradual assembly of avian body plan culminated in rapid rates of evolution across the dinosaur-bird transition"

Brusatte, Lloyd, Wang & Norell. *Current Biology* 24:2386-2392 (2014). doi 10.1016/j.cub.2014.08.034.

File: `papers/PIIS0960982214010471.pdf`, 7 pages. Read in full; all three figures viewed.

## Claim

- Tree: TWiG matrix expanded to 150 coelurosaurs (152 taxa in the figure) × 853 characters. Strict consensus of 99,999 most parsimonious trees, consistency index 0.322.
- Tree result: "for the first time, a TWiG analysis recovers a polytomy between avialans, dromaeosaurids, and troodontids, meaning that the immediate relative of birds cannot be clearly identified." *Aurornis*, *Xiaotingia* and *Anchiornis* are "basal troodontids, not avialans." *Archaeopteryx* is the "basal-most avialan," against [Xu et al. 2011](xu-2011-xiaotingia.md).
- Rates: maximum-likelihood rates of character change per branch, over 100 dating replicates. "Birds evolved significantly faster than other theropods," and the high rates in birds "persist in all sensitivity analyses." High tyrannosauroid rates are dismissed as "artifactual," caused by "an overinflation of taxa and characters" for well-studied tyrannosaurs.
- Morphospace: "they are indistinguishable from their closest relatives in morphospace." "Birds and dromaeosaurids are less distinct from each other than are therizinosauroids and oviraptorosaurs."
- Conclusion: "there was no great jump between nonbirds and birds in morphospace, but once the avian body plan was gradually assembled, birds experienced an early burst of rapid anatomical evolution." "A Mesozoic naturalist would make no immediate distinction between a Velociraptor-type animal and an Archaeopteryx-type animal."
- Few characters separate birds from their relatives, which "may also help explain why so many alternative phylogenetic analyses have difficulty in placing certain taxa (such as Anchiornis and Archaeopteryx)."

## Push-back and pairing

- The same data series (TWiG), six years apart, gives different answers: here a three-way polytomy with anchiornithines in Troodontidae; in [Pei et al. 2020](pei-2020.md), Deinonychosauria sister to birds and anchiornithines as the first birds. Good evidence that the bird sister group is unsettled.
- The authors explain away high tyrannosauroid rates as sampling but accept high bird rates; they back this with sensitivity tests, though the logic (oversampled groups give high rates) applies to birds too, which are also heavily studied.
- Morphospace axes 1-3 explain only 4.25% of variance. Birds separate on axis 1 only.
- Pairs with [Lee et al. 2014](lee-2014.md): same year, same transition, different data (body size + skeleton, Bayesian) and a different emphasis (a long trend of shrinking starting much earlier).
