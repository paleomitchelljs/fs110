# Balanoff et al. 2013, "Evolutionary origins of the avian brain"

Balanoff, Bever, Rowe & Norell. *Nature* 501:93-96 (2013). doi 10.1038/nature12424.

File: `papers/075_Balanoff_et_al_Brains_2013.pdf`, 5 pages. Read in full; all four figures viewed. Supplementary tables not in the file.

## Claim

- Method: CT-based endocasts (casts of the braincase interior) for crown birds, *Archaeopteryx* (BMNH 37001), and non-avian maniraptorans close to birds: oviraptorosaurs (*Citipati*, *Conchoraptor*, *Khaan*, *Incisivosaurus*), troodontids (*Zanabazar*, IGM 100/1126), the dromaeosaurid *Tsaagan*, the alvarezsaurid *Shuvuuia*, plus tyrannosaurs and *Acrocanthosaurus*. Endocasts split into five regions by bony landmarks: olfactory bulbs, cerebrum, optic lobes, cerebellum, brain stem. Body mass from femur length.
- Result: *Archaeopteryx* is not intermediate between dinosaurs and birds. "Several oviraptorosaurs and the troodontids Zanabazar junior and IGM 100/1126, all have relative endocranial volumes that fall between the values of Archaeopteryx and crown birds." Its volumes "reflect plesiomorphic values expected of a non-avian maniraptoran."
- "bird-like encephalization indices evolved multiple times."
- PCA of region proportions: "complete volumetric separation between Paraves and Oviraptorosauria"; *Zanabazar* plots inside crown birds; *Archaeopteryx* "plots well outside crown birds."
- Not just shrinking bodies: small size was maintained along the line, so "Gross neurological expansion was the primary driver in producing these bird-like indices."
- Flight: "if Archaeopteryx has a 'flight-ready' brain, which is almost certainly the case given its postcranial morphology, then so did other paravians."
- Their own limit: "Endocranial volumes alone, of course, do not tell the whole story." A possible wulst (a bird brain structure for visual and motor processing) imprint in *Archaeopteryx*, not yet seen in deinonychosaurs.
- How many expansions and where depends on whether *Archaeopteryx*'s small cerebrum is ancestral or its own reduction: two scenarios given.

## Push-back

- The flight-ready argument runs from skeleton to brain: *Archaeopteryx* flew (from its skeleton), so its brain was sufficient, so any paravian with a comparable brain had a sufficient brain. That makes brain size a necessary-condition test, not evidence of flight. Good example of how one line borrows its meaning from another.
- One endocast per taxon, body mass from femur length, and an endocast is not the brain (in non-avian dinosaurs the brain may not fill the cavity). *Archaeopteryx* is a flattened specimen.
- Pair with [Bronzati et al. 2025](bronzati-2025.md) (shape of the flocculus and other regions, not just volume) and [Torres et al. 2021](torres-2021.md) (brain and body mass in later birds).
