## Claim

- New endocast of the lagerpetid *Ixalerpeton polesinensis* (ULBRA-PVT059), a non-flying pterosaur relative. 3D geometric morphometrics on 37 endocasts.
- Lagerpetid brain is mostly reptile-like: "an anteroposteriorly elongated brain with a long olfactory tract ... The cerebral hemispheres were poorly expanded—the width of the cerebrum was less than 1.5 times that of the hindbrain, contrasting with the condition in pterosaurs and birds, in which this ratio invariably exceeds two." The one shared step: optic lobe "ventrolaterally displaced and modestly enlarged."
- Pterosaurs sit with non-avian paravians, not with living birds: "Pterosaur cranial endocasts share a region of the morphospace with those of sampled Mesozoic paravians, such as the nonflying troodontid IGM 100/1126, flying paravians such as Archaeopteryx lithographica, and the Cretaceous flying enantiornithine Navaornis hestiae."
- Caution on what the shared shape means: it "may reflect modifications in the occipital region of the skull and/or relative enlargement of the eyeball rather than any direct neurocognitive functional significance related to flight acquisition."
- Size: "pterosaurs had proportionally small brain sizes relative to their body sizes ..., comparable to those of other non-avian archosauromorphs ... proportionally larger brain size was not a prerequisite for flight."
- Timing: pterosaur hemisphere expansion was present "at least 60 million years before similar changes occurred in non-avian paravians." "paravians (including birds) essentially started flying with the same basic brain structure that they inherited from their non-flying maniraptoran ancestors."
- Birds' big brains came later: "a clear increase in brain size relative to body size occurred first in the Paleocene, ca. 80 million years after the origin of flight among paravian dinosaurs." "the size and shape of the brain of living birds is a result of transformations that occurred long after the origin of avian flight."
- Contrast: bird-line brain evolution shows "exaptation"; "pterosaur brain structure was an evolutionary innovation in the Late Triassic that was largely unprecedented by nonvolant pterosauromorphs."
- Flocculus: in pterosaurs "larger than the optic lobe, is unparalleled in vertebrate history," perhaps tied to "sensory input from their membranous wings" rather than flight itself.
- Their limit: much of flight control lies in "central pattern generators in the spinal cord that are largely out of reach for deep-time analyses like this one."

For this unit, the pennaraptoran point is the paravian half: the "bird brain" of a troodontid or *Archaeopteryx* was in place before flight and is not the modern bird brain.

- **Fig 1 (p3)**, endocasts of *Euparkeria*, *Buriolestes*, *Ixalerpeton*, *Allkaruen*, *Tupuxuara* in lateral and dorsal views above a cladogram with silhouettes and colored trait markers, plus a 3D skull of *Ixalerpeton* with the brain inside. Attractive. Pterosaur-focused; crop the *Ixalerpeton* skull-with-brain (L) if a "what is an endocast" image is needed.

(A) carries the paper's claim: the orange and purple hulls overlap, apart from the big blue bird hull. Icons are small and busy; the hulls read at slide size. Crop (A).
- **Fig 3 (p6)**, endocast size vs femur length and residuals by group. Simple scatter; supports the "small brains" point. Clear but secondary.
- **Fig 4 (p7)**, timeline (Triassic-Jurassic-Cretaceous bar) with pterosauromorph endocasts above and dinosaur endocasts below (*Buriolestes*, *Archaeopteryx*, *Navaornis*, albatross), red stars at "powered flight," colored dots for trait origins, orange and blue silhouettes. The best teaching figure: two parallel tracks and when the brain changes vs when flight starts. Readable whole; the bottom row alone works for a paravian-only slide.
