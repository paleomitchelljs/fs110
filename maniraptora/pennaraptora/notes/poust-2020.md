# Poust et al. 2020, "A new microraptorine theropod from the Jehol Biota and growth in early dromaeosaurids"

Poust, Gao, Varricchio, Wu & Zhang. *The Anatomical Record* (2020). doi 10.1002/ar.24343.

File: `papers/poust2020.pdf`, 25 pages. Read in full; all ten figures viewed.

## Claim

- *Wulong bohaiensis*, DNHM D2933, Jiufotang Fm., Shangheshou, Liaoning, ≥120.3 Ma. Complete, articulated, one slab (counterpart not kept). Name: "舞 (wǔ) meaning 'dancing' for the individual's sprightly pose and inferred nimble habits."
- Pose: neck and back hyperextended, "If this is opisthotony ..., then the preservation of this perimortem positioning is evidence for low energy burial and minimal transport." (Compare the life-pose *Mei* specimens, [Gao et al. 2012](gao-2012.md).)
- Preparation problem disclosed: "the distal pedal phalanges of the right Digit II ... have been reconstructed incorrectly" by preparators before the museum got it. Three probable coprolites on the slab.
- Juvenile: porous striated bone texture, unfinished bone ends, tiny unfused sterna. Histology: tibia with no LAGs, no EFS; "it is likely that this animal is less than a year old." A possible faint annulus in the fibula could push it "just over 1 year."
- Comparison with *Sinornithosaurus* "haoiana" D2140: looks adult (fused, smooth), but has one LAG and is "likely still actively growing." Lesson: "markers of maturity, including bone texture, fusion of elements, and sternal ossification, are acquired between the first and second years of life in early dromaeosaurids, well in advance of the completion of growth." "caution should be used in assessing 'adult' status on the basis of macroscopic skeletal features alone."
- Feathers in a yearling: filaments, pennaceous wing feathers ("possibly asymmetric"), pennaceous leg feathers, and two long tail plumes, not a fan. Wing span glenoid to claw "only about 48 cm," aspect ratio ~3.
- The ornament puzzle: in living birds ornamental feathers usually come with sexual maturity; quetzals grow tail plumes "around their third year." "The presence of such elaborate structures in the ... relatively somatically immature Wulong demonstrates that nonavian dinosaurs had a very different strategy of plumage development." Or: "their primary functions were not ornamental or were at least unrelated to mating."
- Phylogeny: sister to *Sinornithosaurus* in Microraptorinae; not a juvenile *Sinornithosaurus* (4-5 Myr apart, different plumage).

Pairs with [Clark et al. 2026](clark-2026.md), where enantiornithine ornamental plumes also "grew ... before both sexual and somatic maturity."
