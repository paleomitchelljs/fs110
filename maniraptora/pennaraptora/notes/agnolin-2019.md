# Agnolín et al. 2019, "Paravian phylogeny and the dinosaur-bird transition: an overview"

Agnolín, Motta, Brissón Egli, Lo Coco & Novas. *Frontiers in Earth Science* 6:252 (2019). doi 10.3389/feart.2018.00252. Open access. Review.

File: `papers/feart-06-00252.pdf`, 28 pages. Read the introduction, "Paravian phylogeny: comments and proposals," the unenlagiid and troodontid sections, and the conclusion; clade-by-clade taxonomy and the anatomy topics skimmed. Figures viewed.

## Claim

- State of the field: "the paleontological community is far from reaching such a phylogenetic consensus, as evidenced by the diverse hypotheses on paravian phylogeny that have been published." Fig 2 lays five side by side.
- The test: they took the TWiG matrix of Gianechini et al. 2017, "chosen ... because it provided the basis for the phylogenetic proposals that were affirmed by a relatively strong consensus," and recoded the unenlagiids (*Unenlagia*, *Austroraptor*, *Buitreraptor* information) from first-hand study.
- Result: "The new codings strongly affected tree topology." The consensus "included a large polytomy at the base of Pennaraptora, encompassing Oviraptorosauria, Unenlagiidae, Microraptoria, Eudromaeosauria, Troodontidae, and Avialae." "Dromaeosauridae sensu lato is not recovered." Their reading: "the traditional concepts of Deinonychosauria and Dromaeosauridae rest on weak evidence and lack strong anatomical support." Abstract: "This indicates that the TWiG phylogenetic scheme needs a deep revision."
- Then they set the tree aside: "Because of the poor resolution ... we opt not to follow these phylogenetic results, and instead present below our own interpretations ... based on a review of the available morphological evidence." Their preferred scheme (from Agnolín & Novas 2013): troodontids, dromaeosaurids, microraptorians, unenlagiids and anchiornithines as successively closer outgroups to Avialae; unenlagiids "the immediate sister-group of Avialae."
- Troodontid traits once said to link them to birds are "also documented in unenlagiid theropods," and some braincase features are shared with oviraptorids and dromaeosaurids.
- Conclusion: "We were not able to find a clear sequence of evolutionary novelties." "The degree of evolutionary experimentation and convergence on 'bird-ness' ... may ensure that a consensus on phylogenetic relationships remains elusive."

Not an independent dataset: it is the same TWiG matrix with one clade rescored, and that alone collapses the base of the tree. That is the use for the deck: it shows the sensitivity Hartman 2019 describes (1-2 steps) as an actual result. The preferred tree after that is argued from selected characters, not from an analysis, which students should notice. The authors are the Argentine group that named most unenlagiids, so they have a stake in where unenlagiids go.
