# Therizinosaur papers: figure notes & key facts

Per paper: key facts first (what the paper reports, not what it concludes), then every figure with what it shows, its main point, its artistic merit, and the claims it asserts or tests.

How I read them. Figures were rendered at 55-70 dpi and looked at directly. Art judgments are from those renders, so a muddy photo may be the PDF's fault. Where a figure is a specimen plate I did not open, the entry says "caption only" and gives the views, specimens and scale bars from the caption (Freimuth Figs 3, 6, 8, 10-24; Tanaka Fig. DR2). "Claims" means what the figure is used to assert. If a figure only documents a specimen, I say none tested.

## 1999 Xu Beipiaosaurus inexpectus
*(was A_therizinosauroid_dinosaur_wi.pdf; Xu, Tang & Wang 1999, Nature 399:350-354)*

### Key facts
- New taxon *Beipiaosaurus inexpectus*, holotype IVPP V11559, lower Yixian Formation, Sihetun near Beipiao, Liaoning. Early Cretaceous.
- Estimated length 2.2 m, largest known theropod from the Yixian. Preserved dentary is 65% of femur length. More than 37 dentary teeth (from alveoli), 3 serrations per mm, replacement teeth in oval resorption pits.
- Anterior dentary downturned, lateral shelf on dentary. Tridactyl pes with splint-like proximal metatarsal I (derived therizinosauroids have the tetradactyl pes). Manus ~10% longer than the femur. Tibia 275 mm vs femur 265 mm. Metatarsus is 39% of tibia length.
- Semilunate carpal is smaller than but otherwise identical to *Deinonychus*; contacts metacarpal II and touches metacarpal I. Manual unguals laterally compressed, strongly curved; ungual II slightly longer than I.
- Ilium is parallelogram-shaped (not the sauropod-like ilium of derived therizinosauroids). Furcula widely arched, no hypocleidium.
- Integumentary filaments over ulna, radius, femur, tibia, pectoral elements. Best preserved near ulna, almost perpendicular to the bone. Mostly ~50 mm long, longest 70 mm. Some with median grooves, possible branched distal ends. Unlike *Sinosauropteryx*, filaments contact the ulna.
- Phylogenetic analysis: 84 characters, PAUP exhaustive search, one most parsimonious tree, length 133, CI 0.707, RI 0.645, RC 0.456. *Beipiaosaurus* is basal within Therizinosauroidea, which nests inside Coelurosauria. Tetradactyl pes evolved independently within the group.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: holotype photo (a) and line drawing (b).** Shows a slab of broken, disarticulated bones, with the hand, ribs, vertebrae, pelvis and hindlimb elements scattered across the plate, and a key drawing that labels each element and marks the integumentary impression near the left forelimb. Main point: documenting that the type is one individual and what is preserved. Art: functional, grainy halftone; the line drawing does all the work and the photo is too low-contrast to read without it. Claims: none tested; asserts the elements are from a single animal (the specimen was bought from a farmer, so that is an assertion backed by "proximity, preservation and proportions").

**Fig. 2: details (a-f).** Six panels: right dentary teeth in medial view with resorption pits, one dentary tooth, left semilunate carpal (photo + drawing), right pes drawing, and the pinched first metatarsal. Main point: the diagnostic details (replacement teeth, carpal, metatarsal I). Art: crowded but labelled consistently; the wrist drawing (d) is the best panel. Claims: carpal like *Deinonychus*, metatarsal I like other theropods (this is the evidence against the "segnosaur = sauropodomorph" reading).

**Fig. 3: forelimb with filaments (a), close-up (b).** Photos of the slab showing fibrous streaks running away from the ulna at a steep angle. Main point: the filaments exist and attach to the bone. Art: the streaks are visible but the photo gives no scale feel; a line tracing would help. Claims: integumentary structures present in a therizinosauroid, comparable to *Sinosauropteryx*.

**Fig. 4: cladogram.** Single tree with *Beipiaosaurus* basal in Therizinosauroidea, with other coelurosaurian groups (Ornithomimidae, Dromaeosauridae, Troodontidae, Oviraptorosauria, Alvarezsauridae, Therizinosauroidea) as the outgroup structure. Main point: therizinosauroids nest in Coelurosauria and the pes/ilium specializations arise independently. Art: plain, text-only tree; fine. Claims: the central phylogenetic claim of the paper; tested by the 84-character parsimony analysis (single tree, no bootstrap or support values reported).

## 2009 Zanno Nothronychus graffami
*(was A_new_North_American_therizinosaurid_and_the_role_.pdf; Zanno, Gillette, Albright & Titus 2009, Proc R Soc B 276:3505-3511)*

### Key facts
- New species *Nothronychus graffami*, holotype UMNH VP 16420, nearly complete postcranial skeleton. Tropic Shale, 65 m above the Dakota Formation, early Turonian (*Mammites nodosoides* ammonoid biozone), Kaiparowits Basin, Kane County, Utah.
- Autapomorphies (4): pubic boot with diminutive caudal process; ventral margin of pubic boot dorsally convex; subtriangular caudal process of ischium <5% of total ischium length; caudal process of ischium proximal to the obturator foramen. Five further features separate it from *N. mckinleyi*.
- Pes functionally tetradactyl, robust metatarsal I, subequal III and IV, pedal unguals transversely thick and moderately recurved.
- Phylogeny: 76 coelurosaurian taxa. Therizinosauria recovered as basalmost maniraptoran lineage. Therizinosaur ingroup relationships differ from earlier studies (positions of *Neimongosaurus*, *Erliansaurus*, *Nothronychus*, *Suzhousaurus*, *N. brevispinus*); Therizinosauridae redefined.
- Concentrated changes tests (CCTs) used to find characters correlated with direct evidence of diet. Parsimony optimization puts at the base of Maniraptora: unserrated premaxillary teeth, loss of ziphodont dentition, low tooth-replacement rate, lanceolate cheek teeth, constriction between crown and root. Basal members of Ornithomimosauria, Therizinosauria and Oviraptorosauria each have at least two CCT-supported herbivory characters.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: skeletal reconstruction with element panels (a-y).** A black-and-white silhouette of a bipedal, pot-bellied, long-necked animal with preserved bones in white, ringed by 25 small labelled drawings and photos: vertebrae, ischium, pubis, sacrum, ilium, furcula, scapulocoracoid, femur, tibia, astragalus, manual unguals, pes, metatarsals. Main point: how complete the specimen is and which parts diagnose it. Art: this is the best figure in the paper, and one of the better skeletal figures in the set; Victor Leshky's silhouette gives you the animal at a glance and the letters radiate around it so you can find an element. Dense, but organized. Claims: none tested; documentation (but the ~70% completeness fraction is what justifies using it for the phylogeny).

**Fig. 2: time-calibrated phylogeny of 76 coelurosaurs.** A tree against a geologic timescale (Middle Jurassic to Maastrichtian), with clades as shaded or black bars, coloured/patterned by diet (omnivory, herbivory and carnivory, predominantly herbivorous, predominantly hypercarnivorous, unknown), with trait boxes at nodes (unserrated premaxillary teeth, loss of ziphodonty, edentulism, etc.) and an asterisk for direct evidence of herbivory. Main point: herbivory-correlated traits cluster at the base of Maniraptora, so hypercarnivory in Paraves is secondary. Art: legible but busy, small type, trait lists squeezed into the margins; the pattern coding (black vs grey vs white bars) is hard to separate at reduced size. Claims: central claim of the paper, tested with CCTs (p < 0.1) and parsimony; note the paper's own hedge: it does not assign a diet to the maniraptoran node, it says "at minimum, an omnivorous common ancestor."

## 2012 Fiorillo therizinosaur track Denali
*(was FiorilloAdams2012-therizinosaurtrackfromDENA.pdf; Fiorillo & Adams 2012, PALAIOS 27:395-400)*

### Key facts
- One pes track (cast DMNH 2010-07-01) from float block of siltstone/fine-grained sandstone, lower Cantwell Formation, Denali National Park, Alaska. Pollen put the unit in the late Campanian or Maastrichtian; a later paleofloristic study (Tomsich et al. 2010, cited in the paper) restricted it to Maastrichtian.
- Four forward-directed digits (I-IV), nearly complete. Total length 23.1 cm, width 20.3 cm. Digit lengths I-IV: 12.2, 17.8, 23.1, 14.8 cm. Divarication angles I-II 36°, II-III 37°, III-IV 45°, I-IV 118°. Digit I is short but large and long compared to a thin, reduced digit.
- Northernmost record of therizinosaurs. Authors argue it is the first support for a Beringia faunal-exchange model between Asia and North America.
- Excluded alternatives: ornithopods, ceratopsians, other theropods (tridactyl); oviraptorosaurs (digit I reduced to a rod); *Macropodosaurus* (heel ~60% of track length); Harebell Formation tracks (very slender digits, ~111°).

### Figures
**Fig. 1: location (A) and outcrop (B).** A polar-ish map of North America with Denali National Park marked as a black square; below, a photo of a grey scree slope with blocks of Cantwell Formation. Main point: where the track came from. Art: the map is fine and small; the slope photo is so low in contrast that nothing in it says "dinosaur." Claims: none.

**Fig. 2: paleogeography at 70 Ma.** A polar projection from the PLATES Project showing land and sea with a bold black arrow going from Asia across the Bering region into North America. Main point: faunal exchange route. Art: the arrow carries it; the base map is muddy. Claims: the arrow asserts the exchange model (it does not test it).

**Fig. 3: track in outcrop (A) and outline (B).** A photo of a four-toed depression with a geology scale bar, over a clean black outline with digits I-IV labelled; the outline shows digit I short and angled out, II and III long and nearly parallel, IV angled opposite. Main point: the identification of the track as four-toed with a reduced digit I. Art: very good; the outline is what you need. Claims: tetradactyl, so not ornithopod/ceratopsian/thyreophoran.

**Fig. 4: laser-scan contour image.** A grey, shaded relief rendering of the cast with contour lines, round and slightly dished, the digits barely distinguishable. Main point: objective record of the track's shape for comparison. Art: attractive but unreadable without Fig. 3 beside it. Claims: none tested.

## 2014 Lautenschlager therizinosaur claws
*(was rspb20140497.pdf; Lautenschlager 2014, Proc R Soc B 281:20140497)*

### Key facts
- Shape analysis (elliptical Fourier transform + PCA, 10 harmonics to eliminate pixel noise, 23 harmonics used) of manual unguals of 65 theropod species and 40 extant and extinct mammal species. Finite-element analysis (FEA) of unguals of 5 therizinosaurs (*Falcarius utahensis*, *Nothronychus graffami*, *Beipiaosaurus inexpectus*, *Alxasaurus elesitaiensis*, *Therizinosaurus cheloniformis* [cast; digital model from photogrammetry]) under three loading scenarios: scratch-digging, hook-and-pull, piercing.
- Shape result: no clear one-to-one relation of ungual shape and function; *Nothronychus* occupies a space shared with extant mammals (all three groups' areas overlap); other therizinosaurs fall in a space distinct from extant or extinct mammals.
- FEA: the short, compact *Alxasaurus* claw shows the lowest stress in all scenarios (consistent with digging). Strongly curved, elongate unguals of the other taxa show highest stress in scratch-digging, so digging is least likely for them. Peak colour scale 150 MPa.
- Keratin: the shape analysis used bone only, because the sheath is rarely preserved. A separate FEA with a simplified, uniform-thickness keratin sheath (E = 1.04 GPa) gave the same pattern, with "largely no effect" for a hollow claw and slightly lower stress for a solid keratin-bearing model; the paper notes sheath thickness and attachment could vary. The sheath was a coat, not an extension past the tip.
- Size: the paper says *Therizinosaurus* manual unguals were "over 50 cm in length". (Qin et al. 2023's abstract says "sometimes over one metre long"; the two papers disagree, and the Hu preprint says "up to 1 m".)
- Neck : forelimb length ratios cited: *Nothronychus* 118%, *Alxasaurus* 105%, *Falcarius* 103%. The author argues pulling vegetation toward the mouth (hook-and-pull) makes sense only if the forelimbs can reach food beyond the neck.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: manual unguals + phylogeny.** A tree of 12 therizinosaur taxa along the bottom with a silhouette of each taxon, and above them the black lateral outlines of each manual ungual, from small and shallow (*Falcarius*, *Jianchangosaurus*) to the huge arc of *Therizinosaurus*. 0.1 m and 1 m scale bars. Main point: ungual shape diversity across the clade. Art: strong. Silhouettes grade from pale grey to black and the claw outlines line up above their animals. Claims: none tested; asserts variability.

**Fig. 2: morphospace (a combined, b theropods only).** Two PCA plots with colour-shaded polygons per clade (Compsognathidae, Dromaeosauridae, Alvarezsauridae, Oviraptorosauria, Ornithomimosauria, Tyrannosauridae, Therizinosauria, basal theropods, plus three mammal groups in (a)), red dashed outline for therizinosaurs, silhouettes of animals labelling each hull, and claw icons marking the extremes of the axes. PC1 27.0%, PC2 18.3% in (a). Main point: therizinosaurs span a wide area, overlapping with fossorial/scansorial/terrestrial mammals only partly. Art: the silhouettes labelling polygons instead of a legend is useful, but the overlapping translucent hulls are muddy and the type is small. Claims: tests whether ungual shape tracks function; result is "no clear correlation."

**Fig. 3: FEA stress contour plots.** A 5 × 3 grid (rows: *Falcarius*, *Beipiaosaurus*, *Alxasaurus*, *Nothronychus*, *Therizinosaurus*; columns: scratch-digging, hook-and-pull, piercing), each a coloured claw from blue (0 MPa) to red (150 MPa), with arrows on top row showing the applied force. Main point: only *Alxasaurus* stays blue everywhere. Art: this is the clearest figure in the paper; same scale, same orientation, instant read. Claims: tests function by stress; result is that *Alxasaurus* could dig and the others could not (but the load magnitudes are the same arbitrary value across taxa, a caveat the paper handles by scaling to equal surface area).

## 2009 Xu Beipiaosaurus feathers
*(was xu-et-al-2009-a-new-feather-type-in-a-nonavian-theropod-and-the-early-evolution-of-feathers.pdf; Xu, Zheng & You 2009, PNAS 106:832-834)*

### Key facts
- Two *Beipiaosaurus* specimens: IVPP V11559 (holotype) and STM31-1 (Shandong Tianyu Museum, Jianchang, Yixian Formation; fully articulated, posterior half missing).
- Broad, unbranched, single filaments called EBFFs. About 100-150 mm long, longest shorter than the skull and about as long as the neck in STM31-1; in the tail of V11559, ~2 mm wide, near-parallel. Filaments in *Sinosauropteryx* are ~0.2 mm wide; ~15% of neck length vs. EBFFs ~100% in STM31-1.
- Present dorsal to the posterior skull and anterior neck, ventral to the neck and dorsal to the trunk at ~45° to the long axis, along the tail. Filaments overlap and form dense patches. Distal slight expansion visible in some.
- Flexural stiffness inferred from bent EBFFs. Dark carbonized impressions.
- Authors say all feather morphotypes predicted by developmental models now documented in the fossil record, and that this stage I morphology (a single filament) matches the earliest predicted stage.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: EBFFs in *Beipiaosaurus* (A-C).** (A) the STM31-1 slab with skull and neck, yellow arrows marking where the broad filaments lie; (B) close-up of the filaments at the base of the neck; (C) tail of the holotype with arrows at separate preserved filaments. Main point: show the filaments exist and are single, broad, not composite. Art: photos are good, the yellow arrows on a tan slab work, and the scale bars are in the caption instead of on the image, which is irritating. Claims: asserts one filament per feather.

**Fig. 2: comparative line drawings (A-F).** Black ink drawings of filaments from *Beipiaosaurus* (neck, skull-neck, tail), *Sinosauropteryx*, *Dilong* and *Sinornithosaurus*, drawn at one scale so the width contrast is visible: thick strokes of *Beipiaosaurus* versus hair-fine filaments of the others. Main point: EBFFs differ in size and form from every other nonavian filament. Art: good, a clean comparative figure; no color needed. Claims: this is the central comparison, tested by direct measurement (width, length vs body parts).

## 2011 Zanno herbivorous ecomorphology
*(was zanno-makovicky-2010-herbivorous-ecomorphology-and-specialization-patterns-in-theropod-dinosaur-evolution.pdf, and its duplicate with "(1)" in the name; Zanno & Makovicky 2011, PNAS 108:232-237)*

### Key facts
- Coelurosaurian taxa with extrinsic fossil evidence of diet (stomach contents, gastric mill etc.): the abstract reports 21 features correlated with extrinsic evidence of herbivory and 6 subclades with morphological evidence of the diet (the taxon counts, 29 of 44, come from a figure legend and I did not recount them from the data table). Phylogenetic character tests find 21 morphological features significantly correlated with extrinsic evidence of herbivory (concentrated changes tests, pairwise comparisons, 2,600 individual trait pairings).
- Herbivory (inferred) widespread: 6 major subclades show morphological evidence (Ornithomimosauria, Therizinosauria, Alvarezsauroidea, Oviraptorosauria, plus the troodontid *Jinfengopteryx* and avialans such as *Sinornis*/*Yanornis*). Hypercarnivory looks rare, potentially secondary.
- Traits significantly correlated include: dentary concavity, conical-subconical teeth, tooth symmetry and loss of ziphodonty (strongest correlation, P values 0.0006-0.01), rostral projection of the dentary symphysis, ventral deflection of the dentary, rostral and gastral bone, etc. (P 0.008-0.05).
- Rank concordance: common order of trait accrual in Ornithomimosauria and Oviraptorosauria (5-8 trait steps, significant at P 0.05-0.005 in some tests); no significant concordance for Therizinosauria or when all three are combined in some analyses.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: cranial traits in four theropods (A-E).** Five skull images in lateral view (A *Opisthocomus hoazin*, the hoatzin, extant; B *Ornithomimus edmontonicus*; C *Shenzhousaurus orientalis*; D *Incisivosaurus gauthieri*; E the lower jaw of *Caenagnathus collinsi*), each overlaid with numbered callouts and circles at the beak, teeth, mandible and symphysis where significant traits sit. Main point: the traits are anatomical, in the face. Art: dense and clinical; the callout numbers are tiny and need the key. Claims: marks which traits are correlated with herbivory.

**Fig. 2: coelurosaur phylogeny, correlation and reconstruction (A-D).** A very tall tree of ~100 taxa from *Limusaurus* to *Dromaeosaurus* with colored box columns on the right: green for herbivory, red for carnivory, black stripe for ambiguous, with left strips showing extrinsic evidence, columns of coloured trait-presence boxes, and a lower key. Main point: herbivorous ecomorphology appears at the base of most of the clades, hypercarnivory only in Tyrannosauridae, Compsognathidae, Dromaeosauridae. Art: ambitious, unreadable at the size reproduced; the colour logic is good, the type is not. Claims: central claim, tested by ancestral-state reconstruction under several optimizations.

## 2008 Kundrat therizinosauroid embryos
*(was Acta Zoologica - 2008 - Kundrát - Embryos of therizinosauroid theropods... .pdf; Kundrát, Cruickshank, Manning & Nudds 2008, Acta Zoologica 89:231-251)*

### Key facts
- In ovo embryos from the Nanchao Formation (Santonian-Campanian, ~75-85 Ma), Nanyang Valley, Henan Province, China. Freed from eggs by acid etching. Eggs about 70 × 90 mm; one clutch of seven eggs (CAGS-01-IG-13); embryo catalogue numbers CAGS-01-IG-1 onward. The eggs reached the authors by way of a museum in China and the collection history is partly reported secondhand.
- Therizinosauroid identity argued from six features: edentulous premaxilla with sharp downturned edge; dentary with lateral shelf; fan-shaped teeth with a few marginal cusps; humerus with massive deltopectoral crest and pointed proximomedial tuberosity; ilium with expanded hooked preacetabular process; strongly curved hypertrophied manual unguals.
- Closest to *Neimongosaurus yangi* and *Erliansaurus bellamanus*. Several traits (elongate narial opening, reduced basipterygoid process, low cervical neural spines, narrow pubic apron, pubic foot expanded anteriorly) are listed as synapomorphies uniting Therizinosauroidea and Oviraptorosauria.
- Ossification patterns: embryos died in the final third of development. Fused cervical and caudal neural arches/centra, completely ossified thoracic ribs and ilium, possible tibia-fibula co-ossification, fused pubes, complete metapodials, only small unossified epiphyses. Authors read this as "advanced precociality."
- Four preservational/developmental stages (A-D) from egg-bottom bone distribution; stage D is the most articulated. Compared with *Alligator mississippiensis* at 45, 50, 64 days and post-hatching; the stage D embryo (CAGS-01-IG-5) is more advanced than a 64-day *Alligator* embryo.
- Parabasisphenoid has a massive cultriform process, pneumatic recesses (infraotic, infrahypophyseal, and communications to the tympanic recess).

### Figures
**Fig. 1: embryo in ovo (A) and reconstruction (B).** (A) top-down photo of an egg in section: a round bed of pale matrix with a brown jumble of bone, scale bar 1 cm. (B) A color painting (Pavel Riha) of a curled hatchling-like animal inside the egg, long claws on the forelimb, head tucked, with an egg shell wall around it. Main point: what the fossil is and what it would have looked like. Art: the painting is the best image in the paper and does what the photo can't; the photo alone is unreadable. Claims: none (illustrative; the reconstruction asserts posture).

**Fig. 2: craniofacial bones (A-C).** (A) lateral view of the embryo skull, bones labelled and crowded with abbreviations (pmx, mx, n, la, ju, qj, po, orbit, antorbital fenestra, hypertrophied hand claws in the upper right); (B) magnified upper and lower jaw with tooth rows; (C) one erupted tooth, 0.5 mm. Main point: the diagnostic jaw/skull features. Art: the labels are tiny and many, with the photo's red/brown matrix; works for specialists. Claims: edentulous premaxilla, lateral shelf, fan-shaped teeth (the identification claim).

**Fig. 3: basicranium (A-D).** (A, B) the bone assemblage seen in ventral view, with the parabasisphenoid boxed and magnified; (C, D) dorsal view. Dense labels of nerve foramina (fnab, fnoc, fsa) and artery grooves. Main point: braincase morphology and the pneumatic parabasisphenoid. Art: close-up, high detail, scale bar 5 mm; unreadable without the abbreviation key. Claims: description.

**Fig. 4: parabasisphenoid (A-C).** Left lateral (A), right lateral (B), posterior (C) photos with labels; shows the cultriform process, internal carotid foramen, infrahypophyseal recess. Main point: inner pneumatic structure and cavernous bone. Art: same as Fig. 3; informative but only if you read the figure legend. Claims: homology to *Gallimimus*, troodontids, oviraptorosaurs.

**Fig. 5: postcranium (A-C).** (A) full-scale photograph of axial and appendicular bones (humerus, ulna, radius, scapula, ribs, tibia, femur, ischium) scattered in orange-brown matrix with a 1 cm scale; (B) magnified view of the first four cervical vertebrae; (C) magnified view of the pubic bone. Main point: how complete the postcranium is. Art: busy but readable at (A); (B, C) crop in tightly. Claims: description.

**Fig. 6: postcranium, humerus and ilium (A, B).** (A) axial and appendicular bones including a humerus with a deltopectoral crest and an ilium with hooked preacetabular process; (B) appendicular bones (femur, fibula, tibia, ulna, humerus). Two black circles mark where a bone overlaps another. Main point: diagnostic postcranial elements. Art: same as 5. Claims: diagnostic features of the ilium and humerus.

**Fig. 7: stage comparison with *Alligator*.** A 4-row grid: left column egg photos for stages A-D (from a flat mat of crushed bone to a more articulated skeleton, 1 cm scale), a magnified vertebral sample in the middle column (CAGS-01-IG-7, -11, -6, -5), and on the right the *Alligator* embryo in cleared-and-stained colour at 45, 50 and 64 days and "posthatching," each paired with a small photo of the egg. Main point: the therizinosauroid embryos' ossification state is comparable to a late-stage alligator embryo. Art: strong, the cleared-and-stained alligators (pink/green/purple bone) are legible at a glance and make the case on their own. Claims: stage matching (ossification-based inference that the embryos were in the last third of incubation).

**Fig. 8: ossification of postcranial structures (A, B).** (A) the most advanced therizinosauroid embryo (stage D, CAGS-01-IG-5) with coloured arrows marking cervical spinal process, neural arch, distal part of scapula, proximal epiphysis of humerus, distal epiphysis, ventral part of anterior thoracic rib, tibia; (B) the almost-hatched *Alligator* embryo cleared and stained with the same arrows in the same colours. Main point: which structures are ossified in the fossil vs the alligator. Art: the colour-matched arrows are a good device; the fossil photo is cluttered. Claims: the therizinosauroid is more ossified than the alligator at hatching, which authors take as precociality.

## 2018 Fiorillo hadrosaur-therizinosaur tracks
*(was An_unusual_association_of_hadrosaur_and_therizinos.pdf; Fiorillo, McCarthy, Kobayashi, Tomsich, Tykoski, Lee, Tanaka & Noto 2018, Scientific Reports 8:11706)*

### Key facts
- Lower Cantwell Formation, Denali National Park, Alaska. Zircon U-Pb ages from bentonite at Sable Mountain 71.5 ± 0.9 Ma and 71.0 ± 1.1 Ma (the paper also cites 69.5 ± 0.7 Ma from another outcrop); palaeolatitude ~71° N.
- Climate (CLAMP, Sable Mountain): mean annual temperature 7.4 ± 2.4 °C, warmest month 17.1 ± 3.2 °C, coldest month −2.3 ± 3.8 °C, growing-season precipitation 229.4 ± 671.8 mm. Mean annual precipitation from carbon isotopes: 353-1050 mm/yr before the Middle-Maastrichtian Event (~69 Ma), 168-470 mm/yr during it, 475-1451 mm/yr after.
- Large float block with hadrosaur and therizinosaur tracks together (Fig. 2). Hadrosaur tracks: *Hadrosauropodus* isp., tridactyl, wider than long, short blunt digits; a size range from small to large indicating multi-generational group.
- Therizinosaur tracks: at least 31 tetradactyl tracks at Big Creek, averaging 21.4 cm long; at least three of them appear to form a trackway made by a single individual on the same bedding plane as hadrosaur tracks made by at least three individuals (the "at least three individuals" is the hadrosaurs, not the therizinosaur). Hip height estimate ~85.6 cm from the usual four-times-track-length rule. Attributed to therizinosaurids rather than oviraptorosaurs or other theropods on the basis of digit I weight-bearing, wide divarication, comparison with *Erlikosaurus* and *Khaan* feet.
- First North American co-occurrence of hadrosaur and therizinosaur tracks. Discussion offers scenarios for coexistence (separate in time; co-occurring and tolerating each other).

**Fig. 1: regional map.** An Alaska inset and a geological map of Denali with the upper and lower Cantwell formations in two shades of green/tan, fault types as black lines, a red star at the track locality, and a 10 km scale bar. Main point: where. Art: fine; simplified map in Illustrator. Claims: none.

**Fig. 2: block with co-occurring tracks (a) and line drawing (b).** (a) a photo of a steeply tilted grey block with blue circles around hadrosaur tracks and yellow circles around therizinosaur tracks; (b) overhead line drawing with hadrosaur tracks as blue blobs labelled H and therizinosaur tracks as yellow footprints labelled T, 1 m scale. Main point: the two animals walked the same surface. Art: the colour coding (blue vs yellow) is the right move; the photo is hard to read in any case. Claims: co-occurrence on a single bedding plane (the paper notes obliquity of the photo, no orthogonal view is possible).

## 2013 Lautenschlager Erlikosaurus beaks
*(was lautenschlager-et-al-2013-edentulism-beaks-and-biomechanical-innovations-in-the-evolution-of-theropod-dinosaurs.pdf; Lautenschlager, Witmer, Altangerel & Rayfield 2013, PNAS 110:20657-20662)*

### Key facts
- Digital model from CT of the skull and lower jaw of *Erlikosaurus andrewsi* (IGM 100/111, Upper Cretaceous, Mongolia), slice thickness 145 µm, 1,998 slices; 3D finite element models, with reconstructed bone for breaks.
- Four skull configurations: restored (edentulous with a rhamphotheca implied), small rhamphotheca, large rhamphotheca, dentigerous (teeth in the premaxilla and extra teeth). Keratin layer ~3 mm thick. Bite simulations at the snout tip, rostral tooth position, caudal tooth position; also neck muscle loading.
- Stress and strain highest at the premaxilla/rostrum; rhamphothecae reduce Von Mises stress, strain and displacement in the rostral skull; dentigerous models give higher stress/strain than restored. Keratin sheath itself takes strain.
- Conclusion: edentulism plus keratinous beaks improved cranial stability, so mass-saving for flight is not the (only) explanation for the trend in maniraptoriforms.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: reconstructed cranial morphology (A-C).** (A) photo of the original specimen, a yellow-brown skull, lateral view; (B) digital render in bone-white and grey; (C) restored skull model in light-grey with mesh used for the FEA. Main point: the model is based on a real fossil. Art: a strong opening figure, the colour progression is clear. Claims: none.

**Fig. 2: Von Mises stress and displacement under bites (A-X).** A 6 × 4 grid of lateral skull views: columns are restored, small rhamphotheca, large rhamphotheca, dentigerous; rows alternate stress (rainbow 3-9 MPa) and displacement (0.05-0.25 mm) for different bite positions (red arrows). Main point: the rostral stress hot spot (red) shrinks when a beak is added. Art: heavy; 24 panels, tiny labelling, but the identical views make it scannable. Claims: central comparison; the keratin models consistently show lower stress in the bone.

**Fig. 3: skull stress/strain at the premaxilla tip (A-L).** Three rows (Von Mises stress; maximum principal strain; minimum principal strain) × four columns (same four configurations), including coronal cross-sections through the premaxilla showing the keratin layer. Main point: the keratin takes the strain and the bone below it is protected. Art: good, cross-sections are a clever device. Claims: tests whether rhamphotheca dissipates strain: result yes.

**Fig. 4: mandible (A-P).** Lower jaw models, same grid logic, bite at three positions with restored and rhamphotheca-bearing mandibles. Main point: mandible stress concentrated at the angular and articular regions, less affected by the rhamphotheca at the tip. Art: same as above. Claims: the lower jaw is more sensitive to bite position than to the beak.

**Fig. 5: neck muscles (A-H).** Skulls with adductor + neck muscles vs neck muscles only, restored vs large rhamphotheca. Main point: neck-driven biting loads the rear skull wall. Art: adequate, smaller. Claims: a neck-muscle-driven "pulling" feeding mode would not increase stress dramatically.

- Digital mandible models for six therizinosaurians: *Falcarius utahensis*, *Jianchangosaurus yixianensis*, *Beipiaosaurus inexpectus*, *Alxasaurus elesitaiensis*, *Erlikosaurus andrewsi*, *Segnosaurus galbinensis*. Postdentary elements are scaled from *Erlikosaurus* for taxa where they are unknown (*Falcarius*, *Alxasaurus*), so only the dentary is real in those.

## 2020 Button herbivory dinosaurs
*(was PIIS0960982219313909.pdf; Button & Zanno 2020, Current Biology 30:158-169)*

### Key facts
- 34 biomechanical characters of the cranium and mandible, taxa with at least 25% of characters scorable, matrix 76% complete, non-avian dinosaur sample including sauropodomorphs, ornithischians and theropods (avians excluded).

## 2010 Zanno Therizinosauria
*(was Zanno2010Therizinosaurphylotax.pdf; Zanno 2010, Journal of Systematic Palaeontology 8:503-543)*

### Key facts
- Phylogenetic analysis of Coelurosauria with a character set built to resolve Therizinosauria. Strict consensus of 756 most parsimonious trees, tree length 1225. Bremer, symmetric-resampling GC and bootstrap values reported.
- Therizinosauria, Therizinosauroidea and Therizinosauridae are all kept; Therizinosauria is redefined and *Falcarius* is excluded from Therizinosauroidea.
- *Beipiaosaurus*, *Enigmosaurus*, *Suzhousaurus*, *Segnosaurus* and *Therizinosaurus* are rediagnosed and photodocumented.
- Ingroup topology: *Neimongosaurus*, *Erliansaurus* and *Enigmosaurus* are intermediate (therizinosauroid) on the basis of relatively primitive pelvic morphology, despite derived forelimbs in the first two. *Nothronychus* and *Nanshiungosaurus brevispinus* are therizinosaurids. *Erlikosaurus*, *Segnosaurus* and *Therizinosaurus* are labile. *Nanshiungosaurus bohlini* is excluded from analysis (97% missing data).
- Pruning *Therizinosaurus* from the matrix cuts the number of most parsimonious trees from 756 to 108; another pruning step reduces it from 756 to 378.
- Distribution: no compelling evidence for therizinosaurians outside Asia and North America, nor for referral of Kazakh material to cf. *Neimongosaurus*. Time calibration needs a pre-Turonian dispersal event for therizinosaurids in North America and Asia, consistent with an Albian Laurasian interchange.

### Figures
**Fig. 1: previously hypothesized outgroup relationships (A-G).** Seven small trees, each a column of taxon names with the therizinosaur node shaded grey (Barsbold & Maryanska 1990; Russell & Dong 1993; Makovicky & Sues 1998; Sues 1997; Holtz 1998; Rauhut 2003; plus a summary of OTUs combined in the TWiG analyses). ## 2019 Tanaka Javkhlant nesting site (supplement)
*(was 2019307.pdf; GSA Data Repository 2019307, supplement to Tanaka, Kobayashi, Zelenitsky, Therrien, Lee, Barsbold, Kubota, Lee, Chinzorig & Idersaikhan 2019, Geology. ### Key facts
- Javkhlant nesting site, eastern Gobi, Mongolia, excavated every summer 2012-2015 and 2018. ?Santonian-Campanian.
- 15 clutches from a single horizon over 136 m². Two partial clutches collected: MPC-D 100/1030 (30 eggs, 20 collected) and MPC-D 100/1031 (6 eggs). Clusters with at least three eggs counted as in situ.

## 2025 Freimuth Falcarius skull
*(was The Anatomical Record - 2025 - Freimuth - New craniodental materials of Falcarius utahensis... .pdf; Freimuth & Zanno 2025, Anatomical Record 309:1995-2048)*

### Key facts
- New cranial bones of *Falcarius utahensis*, Cedar Mountain Formation, Utah (Early Cretaceous), from the Crystal Geyser Quarry (CGQ), which has produced over 4,500 specimens, 99% initially identified as *Falcarius* in the field. The assemblage is near-monospecific, and the authors cite taphonomic evidence for a social aggregation and a population-level mass mortality (or few events).
- New elements: maxilla, jugal, parietals, squamosal, laterosphenoids, pterygoid, plus additional frontals, postorbitals, quadrates, braincase elements, dentaries, a surangular, and teeth.
- CT data separate the incisiform rostral dentary dentition from possible premaxillary teeth (NCSM 39122), the first in a therizinosaurian.
- Counts: complete dentary tooth row of 28 alveoli (UMNH VP 14529; UMNH VP 14528 also appears to have 28), more than twice *Ornitholestes* (11). Maxilla with ~23 caudal-most alveoli preserved; ascending ramus inclined 21-22° relative to tooth row in NCSM 39118, compared to 23° in *Erlikosaurus* and 32-34° in *Fukuivenator*.
- Primitive jugal and frontoparietal complex shared with tyrannosauroids, *Incisivosaurus*, *Ornitholestes*, *Fukuivenator*; large maxillary fenestra convergent with troodontids.
- Variable traits between specimens: prominence of ridges and contours (muscle-related) and proportions of pneumatic features. Invariant: topology of bony contacts and major cranial nerves.
- Author's inference: early integrated evolution of rostrum and adductor complex in early therizinosaurians, later modified with reduced paranasal complexity in therizinosaurids.

### Figures
Twenty-five figures. Twenty-three are specimen plates; one is a composite reconstruction (Fig. 1); one is an evolutionary summary (Fig. 25). I viewed pages with Figs 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9 and 25 directly; the plate layout of the remaining figures is read from captions (which describe views, specimen numbers and scale bars) and I say so below.

Main point: how much of the skull is now known and from which specimens. Art: this is the clearest figure in the paper, the colour-per-bone key is excellent; the mirrored and rescaled elements give a patchwork feel. Claims: asserts the composite is a good fit (some elements are mirrored and rescaled "for the best fit").

## 2007 Suarez Crystal Geyser Quarry
*(was palo.2006.p06-014r.pdf; Suarez, Suarez, Kirkland, González, Grandstaff et al. 2007, PALAIOS 22:513-527, "Sedimentology, stratigraphy, and depositional environment of the Crystal Geyser dinosaur quarry, east-central Utah")*

### Key facts
- Quarry at the base of the Yellow Cat Member, Cedar Mountain Formation (Barremian), near Green River, Utah. Nearly monospecific for *Falcarius utahensis*.
- Sediments and cements (pisolites, travertine fragments, tufa clasts, radial-fibrous calcite) point to nearby springs, with bones buried by spring and overbank flood deposits. Evaporative component in the cements.
- At least two episodes of bone accumulation from stratigraphy and lithology: one at the level of the basal carbonate and at least one above. A companion study (Suarez et al. 2007, geochemistry) suggests three.
- Time 1 bones are partly rain-washed colluvium from topographic lows: east-west orientation, small parallel scratch marks, short transport by flowing water, some pre-fossilization spiral fractures. Later bones include many fragments and flakes (short transport) and some good-condition partial skeletons that need in-situ or nearby burial.
- At the top of the bone-bearing section, many isolated limb bones are near vertical, read as trampling of carcasses; small pneumatic vertebrae in good condition could not have been trampled.
- The authors' conclusion: "either one episode of bone accumulation that has been reworked or two or more episodes… Regardless of the number of events, such a large, monospecific accumulation suggests that Falcarius utahensis was gregarious and may have been attracted to the area due to local springs."
- Comparison: Cleveland-Lloyd (*Allosaurus*) is read as drought-induced, not mired (Gates 2005); Asian therizinosauroids are isolated individuals, not bone beds.
- Number of animals: "probably >100 individuals", the most prolific therizinosauroid locality in the world. (The 4,500-specimen and 99% figures are in Freimuth & Zanno 2025, not in this paper.)
- Three more bone beds found nearby since the study began.

### Figures used in the deck
**Fig. 4A (p. 6): quarry wall, Profile 2.** Photo of a pale-grey wall with yellow dashed lines at the caprock, an interbedded mudstone-and-limestone unit, an erosion surface, the bone-bearing unit and a pisolite horizon, 10 cm-interval scale pole. Main point: the layers around the bones. Art: clear, labelled on the photo. Claims: none tested.

**Fig. 10 (p. 13): five time steps.** Five stacked cross-sections, Time 1 (1st bone accumulation, basal carbonate) at the bottom to Time 5 (caprock deposited in a pond) at the top, with bones as black shapes in a depression on the Morrison surface (Jm), a small theropod at Time 2, roots at Time 3 and trench labels T1-T5, Q1-Q2 along the top. Main point: the quarry formed in stages, not in one catastrophe. Art: good schematic, grey on white, small type. Claims: the stage model, tested against stratigraphy and lithology (the paper gives "at least two periods").
