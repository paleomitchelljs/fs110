## 2. How you actually use the framework

You use claim, evidence, alternative, test as a way of talking, not as a visible structure. The moves are all in the transcript.

- **Name the evidence's status.** "That's kind of a weak basis for figuring out whether… a particular trait… actually is associated with" diet. "Is that very good evidence? It's pretty weak, right?"
- **Give the weak version, then the test.** Stories about beaks and chins first; then "What you'd like ideally is something better" and the 27-trait paper.
- **State what a result does and does not mean.** "The other 6… doesn't mean they weren't. It just means there wasn't enough statistical power." This is where you call back to last class's dice.
- **Scale evidence to its assumptions.** "The strength of that evidence is conditional on how red it gets, and the assumptions of the model you put in."
- **Name the fallacy.** Four ideas proposed, three rejected, the fourth never tested: "This is a bad argument… failing to find evidence for the other three inherently supports another one is tricky."
- **Show new evidence moving an old claim.** *Duonychus*: "it does change the balance of evidence against those other features."
- **Give the alternative a fair run.** "Display is always a good hypothesis for a weird thing in an extinct animal. It's just a hard one because you can't test it directly."
- **Two lines of evidence that agree.** Jaw shape and pot belly, "both pointing in the same direction".

None of these need on-screen words. They need a slide that holds still long enough, and a figure with the right part lit.

## 3. What you did that the slides did not anticipate

- **Analogies.** Steak knives versus kitchen shears (teeth, chin); Legos and blocks (long claw as lever); a 6-inch finger bone on a 5-foot person (claw proportion); coyotes, foxes, wolves and lions (solitary versus social); cats' claws held to the light (keratin sheath). Slides carry none of these. Leave empty space around the figure so a drawn sketch can go beside it.
- **Hand drawing.** You drew a tooth and the feathers ("artistic masterpiece"). The deck has no whiteboard or "blank" cue beyond key `B`; that is fine, but plan a drawn moment for any new tooth, claw, or bone shape.
- **Callbacks.** To the dice class (statistical power), to *Cytachosaurus*/heterodontosaur quills, to "the let them fight paper" (the FEA colour code), to *Jurassic Park* and 1999 feathers. A deck for week N can assume week N-1 and name it.
- **Cold questions.** "Why might you have a toothless beak?" got an answer from the room. These are a few seconds each and cost nothing on a slide.
- **A concept slide you did not have.** The convergence-versus-inheritance explanation (289 words) is the hinge of the "what kind of dinosaur" argument and had no slide. A new deck should have one picture for it.
- **Numbers read aloud that differ from the deck.** Claw: you said about a metre; the deck says 50 cm+ (Lautenschlager 2014: "over 50 cm"; Qin et al. 2023 abstract: "over one metre"). *Display* claim date: you said "2017"; the claw stress papers are 2014 (hooking, digging) and 2023 (the display argument). Nest density: you said about 10 per 100 m²; the slide computes 11. Decide which number you want and make the slide match.

## 5. How the deck is built

You can skip this once you have done it twice. It is here so a new deck starts from a recipe and not from reading code.

**Folder layout.** `index.html` loads `css/deck.css` and the scripts in `js/`. Everything on screen comes from `js/content.js`; nothing else needs editing for a new lecture. The engine (`js/deck.js`) and the parts (`js/parts/*.js`) are reused as is. Copy the folder (`cp -R therizinos newname`), delete `papers/`, `img/`, `transcript/`, and rewrite `js/content.js`.

**The stage.** 1600 × 900, scaled to the window. Nothing smaller than about 19 px. Prompt line is gone; the five icons at top left (claim, evidence, else, test, sure) light when a build sets `rail`.

**Segments, builds, levels.** A segment is one slide. A build is one key press. A segment has `id`, `title`, `min` (clock minutes), `tier` (1, 2 or 3), `builds` (an array, one object per press, optional `rail`), and `items` (parts placed on the stage by `box: [x, y, w, h]`). Press `L` for level; `#L1`, `#L2`, `#L3` set it on load. The clock packs sessions at 50 minutes.

**The parts you reuse.**

| Part (`kind`) | What it does | Builds |
|---|---|---|
| `cover` | picture and title, nothing else | 1 |
| `title` | part number and 2–3 words | 1 |
| `fig` | one figure, large; pills on the right, one per press; a pill can light a region (`rect` as fractions of the figure), swap the figure (`src`, `w`, `h`), and carry a `role` icon | 1 + number of pills |
| `solo` | a drawn picture at full size (`viz`: `predict`, `bones`, `body`, `lever`, `density`) | as many as you give it |
| `traitgrid` | trait table you drag across columns, with photos on some rows (`imgAt` sets when) | as many as columns plus photos |
| `sort` | cards dealt into bins; the last build shows the answer (`finalAt`) | 1 + cards + 1 |
| `guess` | timeline, one pill per press, scrub with the playhead | one per event |
| `quarry` | five stacked panels revealed bottom to top, then side stats | 5 + 1 |
| `wall` | one picture per claim, with the room's call | 2 |
| `fact` | a picture and a few big numbers | one per number |
| `sure` | thin "How sure?" bar, keys 1–5 | 1 |

**Which to reach for.** A figure from a paper: `fig`. A quantity with a choice (how many hold up, where does the line go): `solo` with a drag. A sequence of events: `guess` or `quarry`. A sort of clues: `sort`. A table that lights column by column: `traitgrid`.

**Figures.**
1. Put the PDFs in `papers/` named `year-author-species.pdf`.
2. Add a box per figure to `tools/figs_table.py`: `name: (paper key, page, (left, top, right, bottom))`, fractions of the page.
3. Draw the box with the grid trick. Render the page with `pdftoppm -r 80`, overlay lines every 10% with Pillow, view it, read off the fractions. Do this once per figure; guessing costs three tries.
4. `python3 tools/crop_figs.py name`. Open the JPEG. Check the edges (several first crops cut tops and sides).
5. Zone `rect`s on the figure are also fractions, also read off a gridded copy. Check them by looking at the rendered slide; by-eye zones were off on four of six scenes.

**Test it.** `JSDOM=/path/to/node_modules/jsdom node tests/deck.smoke.js` walks every build at all three levels and checks the parts. Headless Chrome screenshots (`--headless=new --screenshot`, then `#L3/17.3` for segment 17 build 3) are the only way to see layout. Run them in parallel; each takes about 20 seconds.

## 6. A recipe for the next deck

1. **Read the papers first, write `paper_summaries.md`.** Figure by figure: what it shows, the point, merit, the claim. This is where the facts come from. Without it the slide text gets invented.
2. **Write the spoken arc before any slides.** Four parts, each a question ("What is it?", "What did it eat?"). For each, the claim you want students to assess, the weak evidence, the stronger test, the alternative, and one number.
3. **One picture per beat.** If you would say it in a sentence and it is a thing, find the image. The most useful slides here were the figure with a lit region (the skeleton zoom) and the object you could drag (the trait grid, the stress colours).
4. **Zones in the order you speak.** The `meet` and `falcarius` slides matched your speech because the pills were in the order of the features. Do that on purpose.
5. **Put the claim in your head, not on the slide.** Pills with a role icon are enough; the five icons at top left tell students which move you are making.
6. **Add one concept slide per part** for any idea you will explain at length with nothing to look at (convergence versus inheritance this time).
7. **Check what you will say aloud against the slide.** Numbers (claw length, date, density) differed three times this lecture.
8. **Build the whole thing, then cut.** You covered about the level 1 set. Treat levels 2 and 3 as a back pocket.
9. **Push early.** The version with the redraft pushed at 10:59 had not built by class. GitHub Pages builds can take a few minutes or longer. Push the night before, open the live URL once, and keep a local copy that opens from `index.html`.
