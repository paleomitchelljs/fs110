# Sales & Schultz 2017, "Spinosaur taxonomy and evolution of craniodental features: Evidence from Brazil"

*PLoS ONE* 12(11):e0187070. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0187070. File: `spinos/papers/salesschultz.pdf`, 30 pages, 57 MB (full-res images), CC-BY. PDF page = printed page. Figs 2, 4, 11 viewed. Figs 1, 3, 5-10 known from captions. Fig pages: 1 p3, 2 p6, 3 p7, 4 p9, 5 p11, 6 p14, 7 p16, 8 p17, 9 p18, 10 p19, 11 p20.

Written before Ibrahim 2020 and the 2022 debate. Mostly about *Irritator*, *Angaturama*, *Oxalaia*. Does not take on swimming or tails.

## Claim

Reinterpreting the Brazilian skulls changes the spinosaur picture. Both *Irritator* and *Angaturama* holotypes preserve the same tooth, so they can't be one animal. *Irritator* has nostrils in a baryonychine-like position. The Brazilian forms are intermediates, and Baryonychinae may not be a real group.

## Facts and quotes

Specimens:
- *Irritator challengeri* SMNS 58022, Romualdo Fm, Araripe Basin, Albian. "the spinosaurid specimen with the most complete preserved skull." Likely from Buxexé near Santana do Cariri, Ceará. Geographic and stratigraphic data "lack accurate." Came in a concretion.
- *Angaturama limai* USP GP/2T-5, same formation, front of the snout. Same provenance problem.
- *Oxalaia quilombensis* MN 6117-V, premaxillae, Alcântara Fm, Laje do Coringa, Cajual Island, Cenomanian. Fragmentary referred maxilla MN 6119-V. "likely reaching c. 12-14 m in length."
- Spinosaurus holotype history: "In 1912, when the fossil collector Richard Markgraf unearthed a partial skeleton with neural spines up to 165 cm in height from the Bahariya Oasis, western Egypt." "destroyed by a British air raid on Munich during World War II."

Irritator vs Angaturama:
- Previous idea: they were the same specimen, broken apart. "Sereno et al. (1998) even suggested the holotype of the former may even represent part of the same specimen as the latter," per [Hone & Holtz 2017](hone-holtz-2017.md).
- New: "Each holotype preserves tooth Lm3, which indicates that the specimens overlap minimally and cannot be considered at the very least the same individual."
- Careful: "the holotypes of Irritator and Angaturama likely belonged to different individuals, but the hypothesis of considering them as synonyms requires additional overlapping cranial remains." So different individuals, possibly same species. Abstract: "they could have been the same taxon."
- Argument is a nice example of reasoning: one tooth position, counted in both. Good to show.
- Size: *Angaturama* premaxillae are like *Baryonyx* in size, so its skull "might have been larger than that of Irritator."
- [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) and [Buffetaut 2004](buffetaut-2004.md) treat them as one taxon. [Sereno 2026](sereno-2026.md) Fig 5 shows only *Irritator*.

Nostrils:
- "Contrary to previous assumptions, within Spinosauridae, a more anterior location of the nares is shared by baryonychines and the spinosaurine Irritator." *Baryonyx* nares begin between m2 and m3, *Suchomimus* m3 and m4, *Irritator* m3 and m4, "the external nares of MSNM V4047 start only at the level of m9."
- Bones around the naris differ by taxon. *Irritator*: premaxillae, maxillae, nasals. MSNM V4047: "only surrounded by maxillae and nasals."
- So nostril retraction isn't a shared spinosaurine feature. It's strongest in the African form.
- Size: largest in baryonychines, smallest in MSNM V4047.

Teeth: "the number of maxillary teeth of baryonychine spinosaurs is almost twice as much as that of spinosaurines." *Suchomimus* 22, MSNM V4047 at least 12, *Irritator* inferred 11. Suggests intercalated loss behind m4, plus loss of the last alveoli.

Phylogeny, 3 runs:
- All OTUs: 145 MPTs, 1069 steps, CI 0.409, RI 0.666. Spinosaurids in a polytomy.
- Excluding *Irritator*: 150 MPTs, 1051 steps, CI 0.415.
- Excluding *Cristatusaurus*, *Angaturama*, *Oxalaia*: 148 MPTs, 1056 steps, CI 0.413.
- "typically baryonychine taxa do not form a monophyletic clade" in the second and third. Baryonychines "may be shown to correspond to successive outgroups of Spinosaurinae." "Baryonychinae would be paraphyletic." Compare [Sereno 2026](sereno-2026.md) and [Arden 2019](arden-2019.md), both of which keep the split, though Arden says "we agree with Sales and Schultz that the monophyly of this group is unclear."

Serrations as a sequence: "theropods initially had coarsely serrated teeth, which might have then become finely serrated, as in baryonychines, and finally unserrated in spinosaurines."

Paleobiology, a different angle on ecology:
- Small retracted nostril = less olfaction: "Proportionally smaller external nares suggest reduction of the relative importance of the olfaction." "MSNM V4047 might have relied more on mechanoreception than on olfaction, whereas the latter sense might have been more important for the predatory habits of Irritator and baryonychines."
- Prediction: "MSNM V4047 may have mainly fed on aquatic items such as fish, relying possibly on vision and mechanoreception," while baryonychines and *Irritator* took terrestrial prey too.
- Isotopic: "some spinosaurids might have been more terrestrial than others; this could have been an important niche partitioning."
- Tip dipped: "at least its anterior portion might have been submerged while the animal foraged in water," nares kept above water. Compatible with wading or floating, and they don't choose.
- Evidence weakness stated: *Onchopristis* vertebra with MSNM V4047 "might be regarded at best as a dubious evidence of piscivory."
- "One could argue that a smaller space could be compensated by a greater concentration of olfactory sensory cells, but such a hypothesis is untestable."
- "the proposed neotype" is their wording, so they hedge on Ibrahim's designation. Sees Ibrahim's reconstruction as supporting "semiaquatic habits."

**Fig 4 (PDF p9): SCHEMATIC SKULLS, FOUR NAMED SOURCES, ALIGNED.** Colored: premaxilla dark teal, maxilla light teal, nasal pale, secondary palate gray, others black. Scale 10 cm. All aligned at m4 by a vertical dotted line. Star at the last maxillary tooth.
- A: *Baryonyx walkeri* premaxillae and maxillae, modified from Charig & Milner.
- B: MNN GDF501, partial rostrum "referred to Suchomimus tenerensis," modified from Sereno 1998.
- C: MSNM V4047 (the giant snout), modified from Dal Sasso 2005. Labelled African spinosaurine in the paper, *Spinosaurus* by others.
- D: "Reconstructed skull of Irritator challengeri," modified from Sues 2002, showing the whole skull.
- Direct, and clear about specimens. The nostril shifts backward from A to C. D is small and shows that *Irritator* has few teeth and forward nostrils. Same scale, so the size jump in C is immediate.
