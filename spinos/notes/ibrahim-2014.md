# Ibrahim et al. 2014, "Semiaquatic adaptations in a giant predatory dinosaur"

*Science* 345:1613-1616. DOI 10.1126/science.1258750. Figures viewed on the rendered pages. Fig S3 is in the folder as `spinos/papers/ibrahim2014-figS3.png`. The supplement's text, tables and other S-figures are not, so they're known only from citations.

Authors: Nizar Ibrahim, Paul C. Sereno, Cristiano Dal Sasso, Simone Maganuco, Matteo Fabbri, David M. Martill, Samir Zouhri, Nathan Myhrvold, Dawid A. Iurino. Ibrahim and Sereno share a University of Chicago department here. By 2022, Sereno and Myhrvold lead the critics ([Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md), [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md)) and Fabbri leads the bone density paper on the other side ([Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md)).

## Claim

A partial subadult skeleton from Morocco, made the neotype, plus referred bones, shows *Spinosaurus* was a semiaquatic foot-paddling piscivore and "an obligate quadruped on land."

## Facts and quotes

- Original Egyptian bones "were destroyed in World War II."
- Neotype: "The subadult skeleton, here designated the neotype of S. aegyptiacus (8), preserves portions of the skull, axial column, pelvic girdle, and limbs." Number FSAC-KK 11888. Locality 18 km northeast of Erfoud, southeastern Morocco, base of the upper unit of the Kem Kem beds, "probably Cenomanian in age (~97 million years ago)."
- *Sigilmassasaurus brevicollis* and *S. maroccanus* are lumped into *S. aegyptiacus*: "We regard two additional Kem Kem theropods... to be referable to S. aegyptiacus."
- How the model was built: "The neotype skeleton and isolated bones referable to S. aegyptiacus were scanned with computed tomography, size-adjusted, and combined with a digital recreation of the original Egyptian fossils (Fig. 2A, red). Missing bones were extrapolated between known bones or estimated from those of other spinosaurids."
- Length: "The digital model of the adult skeleton of Spinosaurus (Fig. 2A), when printed and mounted, measures over 15 m in length, longer than Tyrannosaurus specimens (~12.5 m)." The model is an adult, the neotype is a subadult.
- Snout: foramina "appear similar to foramina in crocodilians that house pressure receptors that detect water movement." Fleshy nostril fossa "small and, unlike any other nonavian dinosaur, is retracted to a posterior position to inhibit the intake of water."
- Neck: broad anterior dorsals make a "horizontal cervicodorsal hinge" that "would facilitate dorsoventral excursion of the neck and skull in the pursuit of prey underwater."
- Pelvis: iliac blade area "approximately one-half that in most other theropods."
- Hindlimb: "just over 25% of body length (table S1)" in the text. The 19% figure in Fig 3 is a different measure, a percentage of forelimb + hindlimb + body. Don't mix the two. Fig 3 range for hindlimb: 34% *Allosaurus* to 19% *Spinosaurus*, with *Suchomimus* at 25%. Fig 3 caption cites "Table 1," and no Table 1 appears in the article pages.
- Femur "substantially shorter than the tibia," with the caudofemoral attachment "along nearly one-third of the femoral shaft." Compared with early cetaceans and semiaquatic mammals "that use their hindlimbs in foot-propelled paddling."
- Pes: first phalanx of digit I is the longest nonungual phalanx. Unguals "long, low, and flat-bottomed." "suggest that the foot may have been adapted to traversing soft substrates or webbed for paddling."
- Bone: long bones have solid shafts "with no development of the open medullary cavity," and density "is 30 to 40% greater in Spinosaurus than in other theropods."
- Center of mass: "in front of both the hip and knee joints at a distance greater than femur length (fig. S3), suggesting that forelimb support was required during terrestrial locomotion."
- The person who calculated that number later called it an error. [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md): "That calculation (made by PCS, one of the current authors) has since been recognized to have erroneously shifted the center of mass forward from the hip by the addition, rather than the subtraction, of the estimated volume of internal air space."
- Quadruped: "Spinosaurus must have been an obligate quadruped on land, the first discovered among theropod dinosaurs."
- Stromer cross-check: reduced pelvis, short hindlimb, short femur, flat unguals "can be verified in the second partial skeleton described by Stromer as 'Spinosaurus B'."
- Tail, as of 2014: "Spinosaurus used lateral undulation of the tail," with "flexibility of the tail and the form of the neural spines" supporting "tail-assisted swimming." Propulsion is credited to the feet. Ibrahim 2020 changes this.
- Sail: "the tallest axial structure documented among dinosaurs, has been argued to be a thermoregulatory surface, a muscle- or fat-lined hump (30), or a display structure." Compared with the crested chameleon *Trioceros cristatus*, following Stromer. "The striated surface, sharp edges, and dense, poorly vascularized internal bone of the spines suggest that they were wrapped snugly in skin and functioned as a display structure that would have remained visible while swimming." Supplement: spine height "up to ten times greater than centrum height."
- Diet: "primarily a piscivore, subsisting on sharks, sawfish, coelacanths, lungfish, and actinopterygians."

## Figures

**Fig 1 (PDF p2).** Map of Morocco with an X northeast of Erfoud (A). Stratigraphic column of the Kem Kem beds with a *Spinosaurus* silhouette at the boundary between lower and upper units, *Carcharodontosaurus* above, *Deltadromeus* and *Rebbachisaurus* below (B).

**Fig 2A (PDF p2): SKELETON, COMPOSITE.** Left lateral, swimming pose, limbs hanging, tail extended. Red = known bones. Gray = not known.
- Specimens pooled in the red: neotype FSAC-KK 11888 (subadult), "referred specimens," and "drawings of original bones" from Stromer 1915 (the destroyed holotype). All are size-adjusted to one adult. Caption wording: "known bones (red) based on size-adjusted, computed tomographic scans of the neotype (FSAC-KK 11888), referred specimens, and drawings of original bones."
- The red is one color. You cannot read off the figure which red bone comes from which animal, or how much each was scaled. Arden et al. 2019 state the objection: "Reconstructions are based on specimens from different localities (Ibrahim et al., 2014) and possibly different species (Evers et al., 2015)."
- The specimen-coded version is Fig S3, below. Henderson 2018: "The colour codings of the vertebrae used in the reconstruction (Ibrahim et al., 2014, Fig. S3) clearly show that the majority of the vertebrae come from other animals and locations."
- Gray includes other spinosaurids as sources ("estimated from those of other spinosaurids," refs 6, 13, 14 = Dal Sasso 2005, Sereno 1998, Charig & Milner 1997).
- Looking at the crop: snout and lower jaw front are red, the back of the skull is gray. Neck is mostly red with gray. Most dorsal spines are red, with one gray spine just in front of D13. Sacral spines are gray. Ilium, femur, tibia and much of the feet are red. Most of the tail is gray with spaced red vertebrae. Gastralia are gray.
- The tail here is the pre-2020 tail: thin, tapering, low spines. Compare with Ibrahim 2020.

**Fig S3 (supplement, `spinos/papers/ibrahim2014-figS3.png`, 1374 x 513, your screenshot): SKELETON, COMPOSITE, COLOR-CODED BY SOURCE.** Left lateral, on all fours, jaws open, gray flesh outline, low pre-2020 tail. This is the drawing [Henderson 2018](henderson-2018.md) modeled and [Hartman 2014](hartman-2014-2020.md) rescaled.
- Color key, from the supplement's caption ([supplement note](ibrahim-2014-supplement.md)): "red, neotype (FSAC-KK 11888); orange, Stromer's bones; yellow, isolated bones from the Kem Kem; green, surrogate bones modeled or taken from the spinosaurids Suchomimus, Baryonyx, Irritator or Ichthyovenator; blue, inferred bones from adjacent bones."
- So: bright red = neotype FSAC-KK 11888. Rust-orange = Stromer's Egyptian specimens. Yellow = isolated Moroccan bones. Green = other genera. Blue = restored.
- Hartman 2020: "the use of highly saturated red and orange can make it somewhat difficult to tell the FSAC material from the type material." True of this screenshot, so red vs orange below is tentative.
- Read from zoomed crops. Upper jaw yellow (MSNM V4047 by Fig 2B, my inference). Lower jaw front rust-orange (Stromer's dentary), rear green. Back of skull green with small red patches. Neck mixes yellow, red, rust and one blue spine, with green ribs. Dorsal spines mostly rust-orange, one with a yellow section, one blue mid-series, and the last ones before the hip blue. Ilium, pelvis, femur, tibia bright red. Feet mostly red with some green and blue. Scapula, humerus and forearm yellow, hand green, red and yellow. Tail mostly blue with scattered yellow, green and red, all low-spined.
- So in this one skeleton, the legs and hips are one Moroccan subadult, most of the sail is a destroyed Egyptian animal, the snout is an isolated Moroccan adult, and the arm is isolated bones. All scaled to one size.
- Red dot: "shows the approximate position of the center of mass." This is the number [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md) calls an error.
