## Claim

Spinosaurid snouts carried a dense nerve and vessel network, so the snout was highly sensitive, but that doesn't mean aquatic. "This sensitivity of the snout depends neither on the environment (because it is found in aquatic, semi-aquatic, terrestrial and even underground taxa), nor on the animal's diet." And Pittet's reading of ecology: "when these animals fished, they must have done so from the bank, at least for large prey, with the rosette of their muzzle in the water."

This cuts two ways. It supports the sensory organ idea from Ibrahim 2014 and undercuts that organ as evidence of water life.

## Facts and quotes

Specimens, all in MNHN Paris, all CT scanned:
- MNHN.F.GDF365, *Cristatusaurus lapparenti*, adult ("mature"), two premaxillae. Tegama Fm, Aptian, Gadoufaoua, Niger.
- MNHN.F.GDF366, *C. lapparenti*, juvenile, "two unfused premaxillae and a fragment of the right maxilla." Same place.
- MNHN.F.SAM124, "Spinosaurus maroccanus," two premaxillae and two incomplete maxillae. Albian, Gara Samani, Algeria.
- Crocodilians CT scanned for comparison: *Gavialis* MHNG S14.25, *Crocodylus moreletii* TMM M-4980, *Tomistoma*, *C. niloticus*.

Taxonomy conflicts:
- *Cristatusaurus*: Pittet treats it as valid. [Sales & Schultz 2017](sales-schultz-2017.md) and [Hone & Holtz 2017](hone-holtz-2017.md): possibly a nomen dubium or a synonym of *Suchomimus*. Pittet's own Fig 20B: "almost perfect overlap between Cristatusaurus and Suchomimus" in the premaxilla. So results may effectively apply to *Suchomimus*.
- *S. maroccanus*: treated as valid. [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md): "reduced to a junior synonym of S. aegyptiacus," specific status "in doubt," MNHN SAM 124 as "Spinosaurus sp." and as an inland record. [Sales & Schultz 2017](sales-schultz-2017.md): invalid, may share a taxon with MSNM V4047.

Counts:
- GDF365 (adult): 49 foramina right, 42 left, 1 to 8 mm, average 3 mm.
- GDF366 (juvenile): 50 right, 54 left, < 1 to 4 mm, average 1 mm.
- SAM124 premaxillae: 28 left, 36 right, < 1 to 9 mm, most 4 to 6 mm. Maxillae: 17 right, 23 left.
- Network volume / bone volume in the premaxilla: GDF366 7.43 / 79.17 cm3 = 9.39%. GDF365 33.96 / 339.90 cm3 = 9.99%.
- *Neovenator* (Barker 2017): 7.3% premaxilla, 4.1% maxilla. "about a third more than observed in a 'classic' terrestrial theropod as Neovenator." Only that one comparison. "Other comparative studies between theropods are, however, necessary."

Inference chain:
- "the diameter of the nerves is proportional to the diameter of the bone foramina" (crocodilians, George & Holliday 2013; primates, DeGusta 1999). Transferred to dinosaurs.
- Branch is "probably" the maxillary branch of the trigeminal nerve V2, and "the fact that the muzzle of these animals (and the rosette in particular) was extremely sensitive is beyond doubt." The certainty goes well beyond "probably." Soft tissue isn't preserved.
- ISO warning: "should not, however, lead us to conclude that they also have ISOs sensu stricto." Compare [Ibrahim 2014](ibrahim-2014.md): "appear similar to foramina in crocodilians that house pressure receptors."
- Stated purpose: the sensitivity "reflects the compensation of another sense otherwise diminished for environmental or morphological reason rather than to an aquatic or semi-aquatic lifestyle/diet, as commonly suggested."
- Terrestrial comparisons: echidna, moles, kiwi, ibis, and *Tyrannosaurus* itself: "It is not necessary to be long nosed to be 'sensitive of the muzzle.'"
- "Paraphyletic" is used for what's convergence or polyphyly: "This feature is so totally paraphyletic." Don't copy this word onto a slide.
- Long snout: "The longirostral character has more of a hydrodynamic utility and for the mode of nutrition... rather than an obligatory sign of sensitivity."

Fishing:
- "Unlike the crocodilians, the Spinosauridae probably hunted on the bank for large preys (and not in the water), but with the same system of detecting waves in an aqueous environment, with the front of their snout submerged in water."
- Three clues: long snout for extraction (Cuff & Rayfield), crest as water-cutter (Gimsa & Gimsa 2021), *Irritator* ear canals at 45 degrees (Schade 2020).
- On swimming: "Perhaps small prey could have been captured underwater by active swimming... although this has not been demonstrated so far (Myhrvold et al. 2024)." Neck and tail evidence is left open, and "this does not, however, discredit the theory of fishing from the bank."
- Note the point isn't a test. Those three clues come from other papers.

Tooth replacement:
- "The presence of three generations of teeth in each alveolus of the younger Cristatusaurus specimen is evidence of intense and uninterrupted dental development." "fewer replacement teeth in the more mature individual." "the rate of tooth replacement decreases as animals grow."
- That's generation count per alveolus, N = 2 individuals, and not a measured rate. Relevant to [Beevor 2021](beevor-2021.md), which assumed that rate was not unusual.
- Juvenile GDF366: "a total of 35 main and replacement teeth on both premaxillae: 18 on the right and 17 on the left."

Juveniles: young spinosaurids "already highly innervated." Lakin & Longrich 2019 juveniles cited, "in the same environments."

**Fig 14 (PDF p21).** SAM124 *S. maroccanus* premaxillae in transparency with teal network and gray teeth. A: right labial. B: ventral, the two front teeth with concentric generations. Scale 10 cm. The spinosaurine counterpart to Fig 4.

**Figs 16, 17 (PDF p23-24).** SAM124 maxilla grooves and teeth, 3 generations at the 6th alveolus.

**Fig 18 (PDF p26): SKULL PHOTO + OUTLINES, *T. rex*.** A: photo of FMNH PR2081 "Sue," upper skull in right lateral (from Brochu 2003). B: "Sue" outline with foramina marked, circumfenestral row in red. C: BHI 3033 "Stan" dentary in anterior view. Two named individuals. Good surprise: same foramina on a land predator.

**Fig 19 (PDF p28).** *Crocodylus moreletii* TMM M-4980 (A-C) and *Gavialis* MHNG S14.25 (D-F), whole skulls with blue networks. Visual match with Fig 4.

**Fig 20 (PDF p32).** Left premaxillae "reduced to the same size," overlaid. A: GDF365 green vs GDF366 orange. B: *Suchomimus* MNN GDF501 blue (from Hendrickx 2016) vs GDF365. C: *Baryonyx* BMNH R9951 red vs GDF365. "Not to scale." Fig 20B is the near-perfect overlap with *Suchomimus*.

**Fig 21 (PDF p35): LIFE ART, not a skeleton.** Black-and-white drawing by Alain Bénéteau. Two adult *C. lapparenti* standing on a bank, one lifting a coelacanth from the water, "fishing from the bank and using their snout sensitive receptors." No sail like *Spinosaurus*. Illustrates the model, doesn't test it. Good contrast to a swimming-pose reconstruction.
