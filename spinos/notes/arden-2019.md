# Arden et al. 2019, "Aquatic adaptation in the skull of carnivorous dinosaurs (Theropoda: Spinosauridae) and the evolution of aquatic habits in spinosaurids"

*Cretaceous Research* 93:275-284. DOI 10.1016/j.cretres.2018.06.013. File: `spinos/papers/1-s2.0-S0195667117303427-main.pdf`. PDF page = journal page minus 274. Figs 2, 4, 5, 6 viewed on the page. Figs 1, 3 known from captions and text.

## Claim

Raised orbits and arched frontals put the eyes on top of the skull. The authors read that as aquatic adaptation, and read two frontal shapes as two spinosaurine taxa in the Kem Kem.

## Facts and quotes

- Abstract: "Elevated orbits and bending of the frontals placed the eyes atop the skull, as in semiaquatic animals such as crocodiles and hippos."
- Morph A (= ? cf. *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus*): FSAC-KK-3209 (left frontal + partial right), Ifezouane Fm, Begaa, 114 mm across, width/length 0.42. FSAC-KK-3210 (paired frontals and parietals), Aoufous Fm, north of Erfoud, 113 mm across. Fused, "near or at somatic maturity."
- Morph B (= ? *Sigilmassasaurus brevicollis*): fused frontals, 149 mm across, Ifezouane Fm. The paper writes the number two ways: FSAC-KK-7715 (material list, Fig 3 caption) and FSAC-KK-7115 (description, discussion). Check before citing.
- Maturity is the argument against a growth series: the small bone is "dense and avascular," the big bone "highly vascularized." So "the smaller specimens are more mature than the larger one," and "the differences in size and shape therefore appear to represent species-level differences."
- Frontal arch: "deflected by about 30 [degrees] with respect to one another."
- Taxon assignment is soft, and they say so: "These assignments cannot be definitively proven at present, given the absence of overlapping material."
- On Ibrahim's composite: "the composite reconstruction of Ibrahim et al. (2014) may well incorporate more than one species, but it probably provides a reasonably accurate picture of the body plan of a spinosaurin."
- On the neotype: "The relationship between the Spinosaurus neotype... and the holotype, is unclear." Holotype spines "are much more expanded than those of the neotype." Could be ontogeny, dimorphism, or taxonomy, "in which case the neotype might conceivably represent a species other than the S. aegyptiacus holotype."
- Not unique to water: "This morphology is not exclusively associated with aquatic habits - notably, the warthog, a highly terrestrial animal, has elevated orbits." Their inference leans on the other evidence: "in the context of other evidence... the most likely explanation for this unusual morphology is aquatic adaptation."
- Modern comparisons: otters, polar bears, crocodilians, *Hippopotamus*, and early whales (*Pakicetus*, *Ichthyolestes*, *Ambulocetus*).
- Olfactory bulbs reduced: "Olfactory cues would have been of little use to an animal that primarily hunted underwater."
- [Cau 2026](cau-2026.md) reads the same kind of frontal fossae as salt gland sites, and cites Arden's specimens for the orbital notch and sulcus. The two readings don't exclude each other.
- A published reply exists: Hone & Holtz 2019, "Comment on: Aquatic adaptation in the skull," *Cretaceous Research*. See [Hone & Holtz 2019](hone-holtz-2019.md).
- Gradient: baryonychines lack pachyostosis, so "they may have waded into lakes and rivers to fish, like herons and Kodiak bears." Spinosaurines are "highly specialized semiaquatic predators." Compared to whales, a radiation that "varied in their degree of specialization."
- Ecology: giant pholidosaurs like *Sarcosuchus* are absent from the Cenomanian of North Africa. Largest croc *Elosuchus*, skull ~100 cm. Spinosaurids either took vacant niches or drove them out "via competitive displacement." No test is offered between those.
- Phylogeny: 12 shortest trees, length 795.56, CI .5780. Stratigraphy used as a tie-breaker (.01 step per stage), and continent as a binary character. Tree therefore carries time and place as data. Neotype excluded "pending a full description."

## Figures

**Fig 2 (PDF p3).** Photos, tan on black. A-D: Morph A frontal FSAC-KK-3209 in dorsal, ventral, right lateral, anterior. E-H: Morph A frontoparietal FSAC-KK-3210 in the same views. (Caption says "E-F" for the second specimen, but the views run E-H.) Scale 50 mm.

**Fig 3 (PDF p4, not viewed).** Morph B frontals FSAC-KK-7715, four views. Scale 100 mm.

**Fig 4 (PDF p6): tree plus drawn elements.** Time-scaled tree, Oxfordian to Maastrichtian, with colored ovals by continent. Numbered drawings show the steps to aquatic habits:
1. an elongate caudal spine, caption says "early spinosaurids," no specimen named;
2. pelvic elements, *Ichthyovenator* by the caption;
3-4. skull with nostril and orbit arrows, same drawing as Fig 6A, so also a composite;
5. a silhouette with short limbs, tall sail, no specimen named.
Reads as a sequence, but the order comes from their tree, which is weakly supported by their own account.

**Fig 5 (PDF p7).** White outlines of frontals on gray, same postorbital width. A: *Carcharodontosaurus saharicus* (after Sereno 1996). B: Morph B. C: Morph A, with the long narrow nasal process. Clean visual of the difference.

**Fig 6A (PDF p8): SKULL, COMPOSITE OF FOUR SOURCES.** Caption: "Spinosaurin skull composite reconstruction, incorporating the frontoparietals of FSAC-KK-3210, the rostrum of MSNM V4047 (dal Sasso et al., 2005) the mandible of the Spinosaurus aegyptiacus holotype (Stromer, 1915a), and the skull of Irritator (Sues et al., 2002)." Lateral view, sitting in a gray body wash at a waterline, eye above.
- Sources: a Morph A skull roof (FSAC-KK-3210), the giant Milan snout (MSNM V4047), the destroyed holotype jaw (from Stromer's drawing), and a different genus from Brazil for the back of the skull.
- The paper assigns Morph A to cf. *Spinosaurus* and assigns the dal Sasso rostrum to *Sigilmassasaurus*: "Morph B to Sigilmassasaurus along with the larger and more robust rostrum (dal Sasso et al., 2005)." So by its own text this skull joins two of its taxa, plus *Irritator*. The caption calls it "Spinosaurin" for that reason.

**Fig 6B (PDF p8): SILHOUETTE, not a skeleton.** Whole body silhouette in "surface swimming posture" with only the Fig 6A skull drawn in. Waterline at the skull. No specimen named for the body. Body plan looks like Ibrahim 2014 (short legs, feet hanging), but the caption doesn't say so.
