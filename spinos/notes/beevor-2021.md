# Beevor et al. 2021 (online 2020), "Taphonomic evidence supports an aquatic lifestyle for Spinosaurus"

*Cretaceous Research* 117:104627, DOI 10.1016/j.cretres.2020.104627 (citation from the Hone & Holtz 2021 reference list, since the PDF doesn't print it). File: `spinos/papers/Taphonomic_evidence_supports_an_aquatic.pdf`, 31 pages, line-numbered manuscript. Captions on PDF p17-18. Figs 1-7 on PDF p19-25 in order. Tables 1-3 on PDF p26-31. Figs 4 and 6 viewed. Others known from captions.

Authors overlap with the aquatic camp: Ibrahim, Zouhri and Martill are also on Ibrahim 2020.

## Claim

*Spinosaurus* teeth dominate two river bone beds at Tarda, Morocco, far above any other dinosaur. "it is highly likely that this animal was living mostly within the river rather than along its banks."

## Facts and quotes

Sites and how the samples were collected:
- Site 1: channel-lag bone bed in lower Ifezouane Fm, n = 926 vertebrate fossils, 317 teeth. Collected by the team from abandoned diggings and spoil dumps.
- Site 2: single event horizon in upper Ifezouane Fm, n = 1261, 928 teeth. "One of us (DMM) purchased all of the fossils that a digger had obtained and put into a large sack." Authors acknowledge "some larger bones and more valuable larger teeth" may have been removed. "likely... only a small number (pers. obs.)."
- Sites 1.5 to 2 km apart (methods say "1.5 kilometres," Fig 3 caption says "just shy of 2 km"). Neither sample is a controlled excavation, so size bias and selection are possible at both.

Counts:
- Site 1 teeth: *Onchopristis* rostral "teeth" 159 (50.2%), *Spinosaurus* 152 (47.9%), *Lepidotes* 3, *Carcharodontosaurus* 1, lungfish 1, croc? 1.
- Site 2 teeth: *Spinosaurus* 407 (43.9%), *Onchopristis* 375 (40.4%), Pholidosauridae 73, *Carcharodontosaurus* 25, Theropoda indet. 19, Abelisauroidea 6, Titanosauria 3, others.
- Non-spinosaurid dinosaurs: < 1% of teeth at Site 1, 5.6% at Site 2.
- Conclusions text: "outnumber terrestrial dinosaur teeth by a factor of ~150 to 1" (Site 1), "~8 to 1" (Site 2).
- Not all *Spinosaurus* teeth could be typed: 225 of 407 went into Richter 2013 morphotypes.
- Table 2B prints the pholidosaurid percent as "73" in the % column, a misprint. Should be ~7.9.
- *Onchopristis* vertebrae: 381 (41%) at Site 1, none at Site 2. "not easy to explain." The digger may have left them, or it may be density sorting. So sampling or sorting effects at this scale are admitted, while the *Spinosaurus* vs other-dinosaur gap is taken as biological.
- "We know of no other dinosaur-bearing bone bed where such an abundance of dinosaur teeth occurs." "the first reported occurrence of a bone-bed dominated by dinosaur teeth."
- Elsewhere: Béchar, Algeria, *Spinosaurus* 94% of the theropod dental assemblage. Here 99% and 89%. Southern Tafilalt: theropods only ~6% of the assemblage, though *Spinosaurus* outnumbers other dinosaurs at most sites.

Logic:
- "An animal living much of its life in water is much more likely to contribute teeth to the river deposit than those dinosaurs that perhaps only visit the river for drinking."
- Tooth rate assumption: "there is little reason to suspect that tooth shedding rates would be very different between Spinosaurus and Carcharodontosaurus (although of course they may have been)." They estimate a "56 to 777 day range." [Hone & Holtz 2021](hone-holtz-2021.md) cites Heckeberg & Rauhut 2020: spinosaurids replace faster, so overrepresentation is expected. This is the weak link.
- Tooth count: ~68-72 for *Spinosaurus* vs 60-80 in allosauroids.
- Tooth loss site: teeth drop where the animal feeds. Feeding in water from a bank or wading also sheds teeth in the river. They accept this: "Of course, it is possible that the abundance of Spinosaurus remains in a fluvial deposit are the result of a wading heron-like ecology." Their rebuttal is anatomical, not taphonomic: wading birds have long legs.
- Wading rebuttal: "hindlimbs of Spinosaurus are greatly shortened relative to body size... inconsistent with those of a wading animal," "more poorly adapted to a wading mode of life than any other non-avian theropod." "consistent with only one form of avian locomotion: active swimming." Hindlimb proportion here depends on Ibrahim's composite. Compare the opposite use of leg length in [Sereno 2026](sereno-2026.md) and [Hone & Holtz 2021](hone-holtz-2021.md) (absolute length, so deep wading is fine).
- Table 1 tags *Spinosaurus* as "Semi-aquatic to aquatic" and the abstract calls it "the aquatic theropod dinosaur Spinosaurus" in advance of the argument.
- Teeth are IDed to *Spinosaurus* sp., and the worn crown issue is noted in [Hone & Holtz 2017](hone-holtz-2017.md): "worn spinosaur teeth may be hard to separate from those of sympatric crocodilians."
- Assemblages are "semi-autochthonous." Transport hasn't been quantified. Sereno 2022 argues that most deposits here are delta/transported.
