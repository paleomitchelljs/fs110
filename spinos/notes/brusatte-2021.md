# Brusatte 2021, "Quick guide: Spinosaurus"

Stephen L. Brusatte. *Current Biology* 31, magazine section, 25 Oct 2021, pages R1369 to about R1371. File: `spinos/papers/PIIS0960982221012124.pdf`, 3 pages, 300 dpi images. PDF p1 = R1369 and starts with the end of an unrelated primatology piece. Read in full. Figs 1, 2 viewed.

Written before [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md) and [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md). A short outside overview, single author, no original data. Useful as plain framing for students.

## Claim

*Spinosaurus* wasn't a normal land theropod, and wasn't a whale. The real question: "was Spinosaurus more like a grizzly bear or more like a crocodile?"

## Facts and quotes

Why it's famous:
- "It used to be that whenever I visited schools and asked children to name their favorite dinosaur, I would hear a chorus of Tyrannosaurus and Triceratops. Now, what I hear most is: Spinosaurus!"
- "adults were around 15 or 16 meters long, a good three meters longer than your average T. rex." Compare [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md), under 14 m. This figure is a composite estimate, stated flat.

History:
- "Its first fossils were discovered in 1912 in Egypt, in 95-to-100-million-year-old rocks."
- "the German aristocrat and paleontologist Ernst Stromer von Reichenbach." Name means "the 'Egyptian spine lizard'."
- "the Paläontologische Staatssammlung in Munich, which in April 1944 found itself in the crosshairs of a World War II British bombing raid." This corrects the "1945" in [Hone & Holtz 2017](hone-holtz-2017.md) Fig 2. No source given here, so Smith et al. 2006 is still the one to cite.
- "All that remained were Stromer's drawings and notes, and some ghostly photographs."
- "For decades afterwards, only a handful of new -- and quite incomplete and random -- Spinosaurus bones emerged."
- "teams led by Nizar Ibrahim, Paul Sereno and colleagues finally reported substantially more complete skeletal remains from Morocco." "announced to global fanfare, in a 2014 paper in Science and 2020 paper in Nature, and backed by the marketing machine of National Geographic, with the attendant television documentaries, magazine covers and speaking tours." Sereno isn't an author on the 2020 Nature paper, so this runs the two together.

Habitat: "its home was among the tidal flats and mangrove forests." Compare inland *S. mirabilis*, [Sereno 2026](sereno-2026.md).

Diet: "There is little doubt that Spinosaurus was piscivorous, at least as part of its dietary repertoire, but there are many ways one can catch a fish."

Limits both ways:
- "Spinosaurus clearly was not a traditional land-living theropod like Tyrannosaurus, as its stubby hindlimbs were not built for speed."
- "it was not a fully aquatic animal like a whale or dolphin, as it lacked the flippers, fluked tails and streamlined bodies." Flipper absence is an inference, since soft tissue isn't preserved. Compare [Gordon 2025](gordon-2025.md).

The two camps, by name:
- "Nizar Ibrahim and colleagues favor the crocodile model, positing Spinosaurus as an active aquatic predator."
- "David Hone, Thomas Holtz and Donald Henderson, however, argue for the grizzly-bear model."

What he asked for:
- "they are not complete skeletons, so we still lack a full understanding of body size and proportions."
- "The real game changer will be biomechanical analyses."
- "Now, what is needed are whole-body, three-dimensional digital studies testing how an entire animal... could move, hunt, and bite in the water." [Henderson 2018](henderson-2018.md) had already modeled flotation, and he cites it. [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) arrived the next year.

## Figures

**Fig 1 (R1369, PDF p1): LIFE ART, no specimen.** "Spinosaurus paddling through the shallow subtropical mangroves of Cretaceous Africa on the hunt for fish." Art by Davide Bonadonna. Half underwater, sail and head up, deep tail, sawfish below. Bonadonna is a coauthor on [Ibrahim 2020](ibrahim-2020.md), where "D.B. sculpted the life reconstruction." So it pictures that side's model.

**Fig 2 (R1370, PDF p2): SKELETON, TWO POSES, no specimen numbers.** "Two visions of Spinosaurus." Top: "akin to a grizzly bear." Bottom: "akin to a crocodile." "Modified from Hone and Holtz (2021), based on a skeletal reconstruction by Genya Masukawa."
- Same drawing as [Hone & Holtz 2021](hone-holtz-2021.md) Fig 1, without the evidence arrows. White bones on black. No known-vs-inferred coding.
- Hone & Holtz say it is "scaled to the size of the neotype." So the size is FSAC-KK 11888, not the 15 or 16 m in this article's text.
