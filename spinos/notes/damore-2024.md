# D'Amore, Johnson-Ransom, Snively & Hone 2025 (online 2024), "Prey size and ecological separation in spinosaurid theropods based on heterodonty and rostrum shape"

*The Anatomical Record* 308:1331-1348. DOI 10.1002/ar.25563. Published online in 2024, in the 2025 volume. File: `spinos/papers/The Anatomical Record - 2024 - D'Amore - Prey size and ecological separation in spinosaurid theropods based on heterodonty.pdf`, 18 pages. PDF page = journal page minus 1330. Figs 1, 4, 5, 6 viewed. Figs 2, 3 known from captions and text. Appendices S1, S2 not in folder.

## Claim

Spinosaurid jaws were built for quick strikes and deep punctures, with no sign they were limited to fish. "Overall, there is no indication that spinosaurids were restricted to fish or small aquatic prey." Spinosaurines could handle larger prey than baryonychines.

They don't take a side in the aquatic debate: "Our detailing of the spinosaurid feeding apparatus does not provide strong validation for either subaqueous pursuit predator or the wading feeding hypotheses." "our data does not strongly favor one hypothesis over the other."

## Facts and quotes

Sample:
- Alveoli: 18 spinosaurid specimens from 10 species ("several designed as 'indeterminate'"), 16 crocodylian specimens, 10 species. 1346 alveoli.
- Crowns: 732 tooth crowns with 56 semilandmarks + 3 landmarks. Megalosaurids added for comparison.
- Jaws: 6 rostra and 6 dentaries for spinosaurids, each with 16 crocodylians. "Only Baryonyx walkerii and Suchomimus tenerensis had both their rostra and dentary preserved."
- Fossil data "measured directly by the authors from museum collections where possible, as well as from the figures of published manuscripts where necessary."
- Sizes: head width only from reconstructions of *Irritator* and *Suchomimus*. Rosette width otherwise.

Crocodylian categories (Drumheller & Wilberg 2020), a useful frame:
1. "slender longirostrine," prey smaller than themselves
2. "generalists," prey around their own body mass
3. "macro-generalists," prey larger than themselves

Where spinosaurids fall:
- "tooth crowns similar to certain slender-longirostrine crocodylians in shape but heterodonty and rostral morphology more similar to generalists/macro-generalists."
- "Spinosaurids overall had relatively large alveoli."
- So not simply gharial-like. "This calls into question the idea that they were crocodile 'mimics.'"
- *Gavialis* analogy rejected for 5 reasons (thinner teeth, smaller alveoli, less heterodont, weaker skull, *Baryonyx* stomach contents).

Baryonychines vs spinosaurines:
- *Suchomimus* maxilla has eight more positions than spinosaurines (Sales & Schultz count 22 vs 11 or 12, so a gap of about 10). *Baryonyx* has "twice as many overall positions" in the dentary.
- Spinosaurines: "another region of alveolar enlargement along the caudal dentary." Baryonychines don't have this.
- Baryonychines "probably did little oral processing," spinosaurines "could have processed relatively large vertebrates." Well suited for "prey that could have been relatively large, but not relatively hard, such as gars and Onchopristis (Beevor et al., 2021)."
- Compare to [Hone & Holtz 2021](hone-holtz-2021.md): they suggest hard, slow prey (Massare Grasp/Smash or Crunch guilds). This paper, sharing Hone as a coauthor, says "not cutting flesh or durophagy."

Functional reading:
- "strongly suggests spinosaurids acquired their prey with quick strikes."
- Why uneven tooth size: stress concentrates at one apex, so a few enlarged teeth puncture deep. Similar-sized teeth would share the load.
- Straight, thick crowns gain puncture and lateral strength "at the expense of cutting through substrate."
- Rapid replacement: "A fast replacement rate kept the tooth row functional" (Heckeberg & Rauhut 2020). Relevant to [Beevor 2021](beevor-2021.md).
- Forelimbs: "the claws assisted in manipulating the food, breaking up large prey that could neither be easily swallowed whole." Speculative, stated as "the possibility." "highly unlikely that spinosaurids rolled."
- Developmental constraint: jaw margin undulations track tooth size because long roots need bone depth. So the "festoon" may not be an adaptation in itself. "may indicate a developmental constraint."
- Lateral compression "may have been an adaptation to reduce drag when striking prey within water."
- Scavenging: "may have made them less effective at scavenging from large carcasses."
- Limit: "As no crocodylian has been observed feeding by bringing its jaw down into the water, they are overall not a very good model organism for the wading or shoreline feeding hypotheses."
- Orcas eat moose. Pike and catfish eat birds. Brown trout eat rodents. "Even animals that are very aquatic can take primarily terrestrial animals." So diet doesn't settle habitat.

Young: *Scipionyx* noted as a possible immature spinosaurid (Cau 2024), and its gut held both land prey and fish. "This tentatively suggests that young spinosaurids shared feeding habitats with adults."

## Figures

No skeletal diagrams and no whole-animal reconstructions. Silhouettes and jaw outlines only.

**Fig 1 (PDF p5): JAWS, NAMED SPECIMENS.** "Host bones and alveoli for sample members of Spinosaurinae (left) and Baryonychinae (right)." Scale 50 mm.
- a: premaxilla and maxilla of MSNM V4047 (*Spinosaurus*, photo by Dal Sasso)
- b: partial dentary of NHM R16421 (spinosaurine)
- c: premaxilla and maxilla of cast of MNN GDF501 (*Suchomimus*, per Sereno 1998, a cast)
- d: dentary of NHM R9951 (*Baryonyx*)
- Below each bone, alveoli are drawn as colored ovals. Top: orange *Spinosaurus* and green baryonychine silhouettes "modified with permission from Scott Hartman." Silhouettes are symbols, not those specimens. Clear comparison of large vs small tooth sockets.

**Fig 5 (PDF p9): SKULL DIAGRAMS, not specimens.** "Diagrams of a baryonychine (a) and spinosaurine (b)." White skull outlines with colored teeth and numbered features. Scale 50 cm. No specimen named in the caption. "Crown size is based on alveolar lengths. Crown shape is identical across the arcade, representing the average tooth shape for each taxon." So the teeth are averaged and generic. Skull a resembles *Suchomimus* or *Baryonyx*, b *Spinosaurus*, but these are composites by construction. Green marks the deep caudal dentary. Good labelled diagram for the differences. Fits direct-figures preference, with the caveat said aloud.

**Fig 6 (PDF p10): CARTOONS, no specimen.** Green baryonychine and orange spinosaurine heads, each attacking a blue prey silhouette before (a), during (b), after (c) a strike. Prey: a small dinosaur for the baryonychine, a long fish for the spinosaurine. Starred = deep punctures. Shows the prey size claim as the authors picture it.
