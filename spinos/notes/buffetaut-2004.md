# Buffetaut, Martill & Escuillie 2004, "Pterosaurs as part of a spinosaur diet"

*Nature* 430:33. File: `spinos/papers/BuffetautMartillEscuillie-Pterosaursaspartofasp.pdf`, 1 page, Brief Communication. Fig 1 viewed.

## Claim

A broken spinosaurid tooth stuck in a pterosaur neck shows pterosaurs were on the menu, by predation or scavenging. The authors favor scavenging.

## Facts and quotes

- Specimen: WDC-SFB-001a,b,c, Wyoming Dinosaur Center. Three articulated cervicals (numbers 4 to 6) of an ornithocheirid, in a nodule from the Romualdo Member, Santana Formation, northeastern Brazil, Early Cretaceous. Wingspan "about 3.30 metres."
- The tooth was found "during the course of acid preparation of the nodule."
- Path of the tooth: enters the right side of the centrum, "crosses the neural canal and damages the base of the neural arch, with its apex embedded in the left prezygapophysis."
- Why spinosaurid: "The thin, smooth enamel, straight crown, slightly compressed cross-section and lack of serrations on the lateral carinae." No taxon assigned to the tooth, but *Irritator challengeri* and *Angaturama limai* are the Santana candidates, the latter "generally agreed" to be a junior synonym.
- Fresh when bitten: "the neck must have been fresh and articulated when it was bitten; it was not swallowed and digested, because the vertebrae remain in articulation and lack evidence of etching by gastric juices."
- Predation vs scavenging is not resolved by the fossil. Caught "on the wing or on the ground" are called "relatively unlikely," and "the scavenging of a pterosaur carcass on the shore of the lagoon... may be a more plausible explanation." The journal subtitle ("a mortal tussle with a hungry predator") says more than the authors do. Label from the authors' text, not the subtitle.
- Conclusion: "this rare evidence of predation or scavenging shows that pterosaurs were part of the diet of spinosaurs, which we conclude were not strictly piscivorous."
- Also cited: *Baryonyx* type specimen with "*Lepidotes* scales etched by gastric juices" and bones of a juvenile *Iguanodon*.
