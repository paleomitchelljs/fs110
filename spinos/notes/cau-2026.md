# Cau et al. 2026, "Avian-like salt glands in Spinosauridae"

Cau, Gostling, Lacerda, Falasca & Paterna 2026. *Historical Biology* 1-13, DOI 10.1080/08912963.2026.2669954 (citation from [Cooper 2026](cooper-2026.md), since the manuscript doesn't print it). File: `spinos/papers/Cau_et_al_2026_accepted_ms.pdf`, 22 pages, line-numbered accepted manuscript.

**The file has no figures.** `[Insert Figure 1 here]` placeholders and four captions at the end. Everything below on figures comes from captions. Nothing was viewed. Supplementary information is not in the folder.

## Claim

Moroccan spinosaurine skull roofs carry the bone traces of salt glands above the eyes, as in marine birds and marine iguanas. They evolved "independently from birds in the ecologically specialized spinosaurine lineage which exploited brackish conditions."

The authors don't claim this settles the aquatic debate: "the presence of the SSGs in Spinosaurinae is compatible with either ecological model discussed for that clade (Hone & Holtz, 2021)." And: "the more haline preference inferred from the SSG... does not necessarily equate to the acquisition of a fully-aquatic lifestyle."

## Facts and quotes

Specimens:
- Moroccan spinosaurine skull roofs NHMUK PV R 16423 and OPH 2103. "The two specimens are of comparable size and broadly overlap in the orbital region of the frontal."
- "We examined spinosaurid cranial material housed in the IWCMS, NHMUK and OPH collections." Plus CT and photos of MSNM V4047 and *Irritator* SMNS 58022, and photos from Arden 2019.
- Comparison material: *Ceratosuchops* IWCMS 2014.95.1, *Baryonyx* NHMUK PV R 9951, nasal NHMUK PV R 16426, snout NHMUK PV R 16420.
- Name hedge: "the large-bodied spinosaurine from the Cenomanian of Morocco (Spinosaurus sensu Ibrahim et al., 2020a; see also Hone & Holtz, 2021; Lacerda at al., 2024; Cau & Paterna, 2025)." They avoid saying the Moroccan frontals are *S. aegyptiacus*, since Cau's own 2025 paper argues over lumping and splitting.

The evidence is bone, not gland:
- "each frontal is distinctly excavated by a pair of dorsal fossae." The smaller lateral one is "semilunate in dorsal view," "a configuration recalling the shape of the salt glands in several birds."
- Large vascular foramina, a "round notch in the middle of the orbital rim," and a ventral sulcus "distinctly larger than the impressions of the vascular canals."
- "We consider the lateral component of the supraorbital frontal fossae in the Moroccan spinosaurine, the associated large vascular foramina, the round orbital notch and the ventral periorbital sulcus as the osteological correlates of a cephalic osmoregulatory organ analogous to the SSGs of birds."
- Alternatives excluded: temporal muscle attachment (a transverse ridge separates the fossae from the temporal region) and pneumaticity ("no source for pneumatization was identified").
- In a reconstruction, "the salt gland is assumed to occupy the drop-shaped floor of the lateral supraorbital fossa."

Distribution:
- Baryonychines: "there are no paired depressions on the skull roof." They keep the ordinary nasal gland, in grooves under the nasals, as in *Triceratops*.
- *Irritator*: a large foramen in the orbital margin, with "the dorsal surface... eroded." So its evidence is partial.
- Three-step assembly along the tree, *Baryonyx* to *Irritator* to Moroccan forms (Fig 4).

Evolution:
- Exaptation: nostrils moved back for feeding in fresh water, and this later cleared the way for a gland to migrate. "Features originally acquired by the spinosaurid last common ancestor as semi-aquatic or fish-eating adaptations in freshwater conditions... were subsequently co-opted."
- Same pattern in marine iguanas and euornithine birds: reduced orbito-narial distance.
- Why dinosaurs stayed off the sea: "constraints in their craniofacial architecture which limited the evolutionary plasticity of the osmoregulatory glands." Hedged: "could have been."
- Close: spinosaurines "had realized a first, yet only incipient step along the evolution of haline adaptations."

Tree and habitat analysis:
- BEAST tip-dated tree plus RASP habitat reconstruction, "non-haline" vs "haline."
- Support: "Nodal support for several spinosaurid nodes is relatively weak (posterior probability <0.5)." "Angaturama, Eocarcharia and Sigilmassasaurus" pruned to improve it.
- Placement differs from most trees here: "Wiehenvenator as sister taxon of Spinosauridae, and the latter clade as sister taxon of the megalosauroid-allosauroid clade." Useful disagreement for the opening tree.
- Habitat scored "according to the palaeoenvironmental conditions inferred from the depositional settings." So the habitat result rests on where fossils were buried, which is what taphonomy arguments (Beevor, Sereno 2022) dispute. Possibly transported taxa were scored "unknown."
- Result: "a flexible osmotic regime for the Cenomanian giant spinosaurine(s), able to exploit different degrees of water salinity," in agreement with "both freshwater and brackish depositional settings (Ibrahim et al., 2020a; Sereno et al., 2026)." So they read inland *S. mirabilis* as compatible.

Snout cavities: paired fusiform cavities in MSNM V4047, called a "nasal sinus" by Dal Sasso 2014, are reinterpreted as blood vessel passages for cooling the head. "we consider more likely that the fusiform cavities in spinosaurines housed enlarged blood vessels." Compare [Pittet 2026](pittet-2026.md), who treats snout networks as sensory, in a different set of canals.

## Connections

- [Arden 2019](arden-2019.md): same kind of frontal, different function. Arden: the concavity raises the eyes. Cau: it holds a gland. Cau cites Arden's specimens for the notch and sulcus: "These features are visible in other Moroccan specimens (Arden et al., 2019)." The two readings don't exclude each other.
- [Cooper 2026](cooper-2026.md) wording is stronger than this paper: "just like today's marine or semi-aquatic birds." Cau says "analogous."
- Salt tolerance bears on salinity and not on swimming. A brackish-water wader would need it as much as a diver.
