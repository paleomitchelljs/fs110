# Charig & Milner 1997, "Baryonyx walkeri, a fish-eating dinosaur from the Wealden of Surrey"

*Bulletin of the Natural History Museum, London (Geology)* 53(1):11-70. File: `spinos/papers/part75343.pdf`, 61 pages, page images with a rough OCR layer, so check any quote against the page. Read: discovery, diagnosis, life habits, taphonomy. Figures viewed: frontispiece, Figs 1, 47, 48.

## Claim

*Baryonyx* was mainly a fish-eater working from banks or shallows, and an ordinary land animal in build.

## Facts and quotes

- Found "on 7 January 1983 by Mr William J. Walker at the Ockley Brick Company's clay pit near Ockley, Surrey," and "a week later he found the missing tip of the claw."
- "MATERIAL. The holotype alone." BMNH R9951, so every *Baryonyx* skeleton here is one individual.
- Stomach, inside the rib cage: "Acid-etched scales and teeth of the common Mesozoic fish Lepidotes (Fig. 47). These are of especially great significance." And "the disarticulated skeletal remains of a young Iguanodon."
- "On balance, we still envisage Baryonyx as mainly a fish-eater. It probably crouched on the banks of lakes, creeks and rivers or waded in the shallows (Frontispiece), and it secured its prey by direct seizure with the jaws and perhaps also by 'gaffing'."
- "The enlarged claws could also have been used for 'gaffing', i.e. hooking or flipping fishes out of the water as is done today by grizzly bears." The grizzly comparison in [Brusatte 2021](brusatte-2021.md) has a long history.
- Against aquatic life: "its anatomy gives no indication of any modifications towards that mode of life. For example, it certainly had no flipper-like modifications of the limbs and it lacked the dorso-ventrally flattened skull with dorsally situated external nares typical of crocodiles."
- "the position of the nostrils on the side of the skull would be disadvantageous to an animal spending much of its time in the water." Later authors read retracted nostrils the opposite way.
- "Baryonyx could probably swim, as can most land vertebrates."
- "a large terrestrial piscivore." Stance: bipedal with a possible quadrupedal posture when fishing from a bank.

## Figures

**Frontispiece (PDF p2): LIFE ART.** "restoration of a living animal in the Wealden landscape," John Sibbick. Based on the holotype. Pictures the bank-fishing idea. Decoration and hypothesis, not data.

**Fig 1 (PDF p4): SKULL, ONE SPECIMEN, RESTORED.** BMNH R9951 "reconstruction of skull." Solid and dashed lines, dashed is restored. Large part of the skull is dashed.

**Fig 47 (PDF p52): SEM, TWO SCALES.** A: *Lepidotes* scale from Block 44, rib-cage region of the skeleton, etched. B: same horizon and locality, not associated with the skeleton, smooth. A controlled comparison, which is why it works.

**Fig 48 (PDF p53).** Plan of the excavation at Smokejack's Brickworks, May-June 1983, with numbered blocks and skeletal elements. Not used on a slide.
