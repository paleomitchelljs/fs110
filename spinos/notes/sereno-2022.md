# Sereno et al. 2022, "Spinosaurus is not an aquatic dinosaur"

*eLife* 11:e80092. DOI 10.7554/eLife.80092. File: `spinos/papers/elife-80092-v1.pdf`, 44 pages, CC-BY. PDF page = eLife page. Figs 1-9 viewed. Figs 10, 11 known from captions. Appendices sampled, not read in full.

## Claim

A CT-based skeleton and posable flesh model make *Spinosaurus* a biped on land and an unstable, slow surface swimmer in deep water, too buoyant to dive. "best understood as a semiaquatic bipedal ambush piscivore that frequented the margins of coastal and inland waterways."

Sereno argues against "aquatic," not against "semiaquatic." The title is easy to misread as "no water at all."

## Facts and quotes

What they say they are testing, the "aquatic hypothesis":
1. "reverted to a quadrupedal stance on land"
2. "functioned in water as a capable, diving pursuit predator using an expanded tail"
3. "fossils would be found exclusively in coastal or deep-water marine habitats"

Point 3 is Sereno's framing, not Ibrahim's text. Ibrahim 2020 puts the animal in "a major fluvio-deltaic system" with "a rich freshwater fauna" and never predicts marine-only fossils. So the inland Niger argument hits an inference Sereno draws from large marine reptiles and mammals, and doesn't refute a stated prediction. See [Ibrahim 2020](ibrahim-2020.md).

Terminology:
- "aquatic" = "adapted for life primarily, or solely, in water with severely reduced functional capacity on land."
- "semiaquatic" = "less profound adaptations," "no matter the proportion of aquatic foodstuffs in its diet, the proportion of time spent in water, or the proficiency of swimming or diving."
- Complaint about [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md): "That broadened usage of 'aquatic lifestyle,' however, blurs the long-standing use."
- Part of this dispute is about the definition of a word. Good teaching point.

Skeleton:
- "just under 14 m long," "more than 1 m shorter than previously reported."
- Against Ibrahim 2020's silhouette: "The length of the presacral column, depth of the ribcage, and length of the forelimb in that reconstruction were overestimated by ~10, 25, and 30%."
- Cause of the disputed quadruped: "That additional front-loading appears to be the main factor generating their mid trunk location for CM."
- A different cause appears in 2024. [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md), with Sereno as co-lead, says of the 2014 calculation, "made by PCS," that it "erroneously shifted the center of mass forward from the hip by the addition, rather than the subtraction, of the estimated volume of internal air space." This eLife paper doesn't mention that error.
- Trunk length: "unnatural ventroflexion of the dorsal column that also spread further the neural spines."
- Only 3 associated specimens: "These are the only associated specimens known for S. aegyptiacus." Of those: "only the bones of the neotype are preserved, all of which have been CT-scanned except for recently discovered bones of the tail."
- So "CT-based" has limits. The tail and the holotype are not from CT. Holotype and Spinosaurus B come from "reconstructions (based on lithographic plates and photographs)."

Table 1, specimens used and how they are scaled. "All are scaled to the size of the adult snout":
- BSPG 1912 VIII 19, holotype (destroyed), subadult, 76%, upsized x1.32. Dentaries, presacral, sacral and caudal vertebrae, dorsal sail.
- FSAC-KK 11888, neotype, subadult, 76%, x1.32. Skull bones, partial limbs, dorsal sail, most of the tail.
- BSPG 1922 X45, "Spinosaurus B" (destroyed), subadult, 66%, x1.51. Five partial dorsals, seven partial caudals, both tibiae.
- MSNM V4047, adult, 100%. Isolated snout. (Table spells it "MSMN.")
- UCRC PV8, adult, 80%, x1.25. Manual phalanx I-1, 28.0 cm.
- UCRC PV24, adult, 75%, x1.33. Cervical ~C9.
- Methods misprints the holotype as "BSP 9012 VIII 19." Use BSPG 1912 VIII 19.

Bone:
- Infilling is "fibrolamellar and cancellous," similar to large terrestrial dinosaurs and mammals.
- Variable: "narrow medullary cavity in a femur of another individual slightly larger than the neotype (Russell, 1996; NMC 41869)." Same femur as CMN 41869 in [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md).
- Offset by air and marrow elsewhere. "better explained as compensation for the reduced size of the hind limb long bones." "Bending strength increases by as much as 35%."

Flesh model and physics:
- Body mass 7390 kg, density 833 kg/m3 with avian-like air. Freshwater 1000, saltwater 1026, crocodylians 1080.
- CM "over the ground contact of symmetrically positioned hind feet," "No matter the included volume of internal air space."
- Flotation at ~2.6 m water depth.
- Speed: "~0.8 m/s" surface, "~1.4 m/s" submerged. The text also gives "maximum velocity is only 1.2 m/s," the abstract "<1 m/s," conclusion 8 "~1 m/s." Quote the specific number you mean. Pursuit predators "10-33 m/s."
- Tail: "820 Watts (683 N or 154 lbs)," assuming an Alligator-like tail.
- Sail adds 33.4% drag.
- Dive: "~17,000 N," "~25 times the maximum force output of the tail." "Even with lizard-like internal air space, diving still requires ~15 times."
- Stability: stable equilibrium "when floating on its side." "Righting requires substantial torque (~5000 Nm)."
- Tail: "functioned more as a pliant billboard than flexible fluke."
- Foot: "for which there is no hard evidence to establish as likely." Crocodylian webbing adds "<20%" area.
- Lizards: spine-supported sails "have evolved multiple times for intraspecific display rather than aquatic propulsion."

Inland fossils (Appendix 4), "pertaining to Spinosaurus," genus level:
- MNBH EGA1, both maxillae + dentary edge, Égaro North, Chad Basin, Niger.
- MNBH IGU11, ~D1 centrum, In Abangharit, Niger, "differences suggest it may pertain to a distinct species."
- MNHN SAM 124, 62 cm snout, Gara Samani, Algeria.
- Stated strongly: "The inland location of these fossils completely undermines the interpretation." Evidence strength is genus-level identifications of jaws and a centrum. Ask how far these bones could travel.
- Size rule: "All large-bodied secondarily aquatic vertebrates are marine." Transition to aquatic life "has only occurred at relatively small body size (<3 m)."

Evolution: stage 1 (~130 Ma, all spinosaurids): snout rosette, retracted nares, nasal crest, sail, cervicalized anterior dorsals. Stage 2 (spinosaurines): spaced smooth teeth, more retracted nares, ventral muscle attachments, scythe manual unguals, taller crest, torso-to-caudal sail.

Final position: "bipedal, semiaquatic dinosaur using ambush predation of large fish while wading into shallow coastal and riverine waters."

Tone is part of the record: "failed spectacularly," "completely undermines," "is unlikely to survive."

## Figures

**Fig 1A (PDF p5): SKELETON, COMPOSITE, COLOR-CODED BY SPECIMEN.** *S. aegyptiacus*, left lateral, bipedal stance, adult just under 14 m, with 1.8 m human skeleton and 1 m scale. This is the clearest specimen key in the folder.
- Red: holotype BSPG 1912 VIII 19.
- Blue: neotype FSAC-KK 11888.
- Yellow: referred specimens. The caption doesn't name them one by one. Table 1 and methods name the main ones (MSNM V4047, UCRC PV8, UCRC PV24, BSPG 1922 X45).
- Gray: "Bones without representation among specimens attributed to S. aegyptiacus."
- Overlap priority: "neotype, holotype, referred bones." So a bone colored blue may also be known from the holotype. Red and yellow understate those specimens.
- All upsized to the MSNM V4047 snout by the Table 1 factors. No bone in the drawing is at its real size unless it's the snout.
- Red cross: center of mass of the flesh model, near the hip.
- Read from a 300 dpi crop: Upper jaw yellow. Lower jaw front red, back gray. Skull middle partly blue and gray. Neck red, then yellow, with some blue. Dorsal spines alternate red and blue, D13 red, two spines before it gray, sacral spines gray. Ilium, femur, tibia, fibula, foot blue. Humerus and forearm yellow, scapula and coracoid gray, hand yellow with some blue. Tail mostly blue, gray patches, almost to CA40.
- Forelimb sources from the text: neotype has only "partial manual digit II." "Our reconstruction of the manus is based on a recently described forelimb of the close relative Irritator." "The proportions of more proximal forelimb segments and the pectoral girdle are based on the holotypic specimens of Baryonyx and Suchomimus." So the forelimb length, one of the disputed numbers, relies on other genera. [Gordon 2025](gordon-2025.md) uses this forelimb, and it is the reconstruction that comes out "highly/fully aquatic."
