# Hone & Holtz 2021, "Evaluating the ecology of Spinosaurus: Shoreline generalist or aquatic pursuit specialist?"

*Palaeontologia Electronica* 24(1):a03. DOI 10.26879/1110. File: `spinos/papers/honeholtz21.pdf`, 28 pages, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. PDF page = printed page. Figs 1, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 viewed. Figs 2, 3, 7 known from captions and text. Submitted July 2020, so it answers Ibrahim 2020 but predates Fabbri 2022 and Sereno 2022.

## Claim

"the pursuit predation hypothesis for Spinosaurus as a 'highly specialized aquatic predator' is not supported. In contrast, a 'wading' model for an animal that predominantly fished from shorelines or within shallow waters is not contradicted by any line of evidence and is well supported." And: "Spinosaurus almost certainly fed primarily from the water and may have swum, but there is no evidence that it was a specialised aquatic pursuit predator."

The disagreement is narrow. Both sides have it eating from water. "Pursuit predator" is what they reject. "Being a good swimmer is not mutually exclusive to foraging inshore."

## Facts and quotes

Two models:
- Wading: "these animals acted like large herons or storks," taking fish "from the edges of water or in shallow water, but also foraging for terrestrial prey and scavenging on occasion."
- Pursuit predator: quoted from Ibrahim 2020, "an active and highly specialized aquatic predator that pursued and caught its prey in the water column."

Taxonomy assumption: "Although we do not agree with this designation, through the following manuscript we do work under the assumption that all the new material... does relate to S. aegyptiacus." They grant the composite for argument's sake.

Skull (original data):
- PCA on 5 skull measures scaled to length, PC1 83.5%, PC2 13.7%. "this analysis fails to clearly discriminate between terrestrial, aquatic and semi-aquatic taxa." So their own PCA can't separate the ecological groups. All three spinosaurid skulls are incomplete.
- Nares "retracted posteriorly but not dorsally." "a substantial portion of the head would need to be above the surface to keep the nares clear of the water."
- Eyes: "the eyes are not dorsally positioned in Spinosaurus compared to many aquatic and semi-aquatic animals." DIRECT CONFLICT with [Arden 2019](arden-2019.md). Arden uses frontals. Hone & Holtz use a whole-skull reconstruction, with its own composite.
- Nares in storks: a stork's retracted naris lets it feed with the bill in water. *Irritator* is said to have a habitual head-down pose (Schade 2020), and that is inferred from *Irritator*, not *Spinosaurus*.
- Skull compared to *Baryonyx*: "The two are very similar... which contradicts the idea that one is an aquatic specialist."
- Baryonychines "arguably more specialist piscivores with around twice as many teeth as spinosaurines."
- Snout foramina: found in *Neovenator*, *Daspletosaurus*, so "no particular reason to think that the snout foramina in Spinosaurus correlates with aquatic foraging." Compare [Pittet 2026](pittet-2026.md).

Claws (original data):
- *Spinosaurus* unguals ~165 degrees, flat, but "not unique." *Majungasaurus*, *Sinraptor*, *Gallimimus* similar or higher. Slope 0.044 with length, p = 0.4. R2 printed as 0.0319.
- Also from birds: "no clear or strong correlation between flattened unguals and aquatic locomotion."

Neck: long necks are unlike aquatic predators, whose necks are often half the skull length or less. *Sigilmassasaurus* facets indicate a sigmoidal neck, "unlike the near straight one illustrated by Ibrahim et al. (2020a)." Ventriflexion supports vertical lunging.

Tail:
- Spines slender, "lack strongly rugose regions, suggesting far less development of propulsive muscles."
- Efficiency in Ibrahim: "crocodiles had an efficiency 1.5 times greater," and crocs are themselves slow, so the result "stands in contrast to the suggestion that it is an aquatic specialised for pursuit predation."
- Comparison shape *Triturus*: "the fin is not a swimming aid but a sexually selected display feature... absent in females." So the best-thrust comparison is a display organ.
- Flexibility: fast tail-swimmers have a stiffened distal unit, and the *Spinosaurus* distal tail doesn't.

Drag and depth:
- "the dorsal sail in particular would offer enormous pressure drag."
- To avoid wave drag: 2.5 x 1.4 m torso = 3.5 m, plus 2.6 m body and sail, "an absolute minimum depth of water of at least 6.1 m."
- Hyposphene-hypantra: "it is not overly restricted with for example hyposphene-hypantra articulations." [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) says "trunk and sacral vertebrae are immobilized by interlocking articulations (hyposphene-hypantrum)." Direct conflict.

Sail: signaling. Extant comparisons *Hydrosaurus* and *Basiliscus*, which swim, but "the extra tail flukes are not for improved swimming as these are typically kept clear of the water." "Thermoregulation, however, cannot be ruled out." Display and propulsion are "not mutually exclusive."
- Heat (PDF p19): "a prominent broad surface would almost certainly change the environmental thermal exchange of an animal," but "specific modelling... would be needed." Sails "are not restricted to the tropics of the Cenomanian-Turonian 'supergreenhouse'": *Hypselospinus*, *Ouranosaurus*, *Concavenator*, *Ichthyovenator*.
- Shape as evidence: "it is notable how different the form of the dorsal spines are in Ichthyovenator (Allain et al., 2012) compared to Spinosaurus suggesting that at least one of these was not under strong selection based on their mechanical performance."
- Shape and drag (PDF p15): "the 'M' shaped arrangement of the sail with a distinctive notch in the middle (as favoured by Ibrahim et al., 2014a, 2020a) would generate further drag with two tips than would a more typical D-shaped arrangement."

Diet: Hassler 2018 calcium isotopes: some "possibly even exclusively piscivorous," others with dinosaurs as "a notable part of their diet." Direct evidence listed: fish, dinosaurs, pterosaurs. See [Buffetaut 2004](buffetaut-2004.md).

Kem Kem and teeth: answers Beevor 2020 by saying abundance "might instead reflect the spinosaurid obtaining food within the river." Fast replacement overrepresents spinosaurid teeth. See [Beevor 2021](beevor-2021.md).

Conclusion: "best interpreted as shoreline generalist." "The pursuit predator model is strongly contradicted by numerous lines of evidence but by contrast the wading model is entirely consistent with the available data." Note that consistency is a weak standard: this model allows swimming, wading, fishing and land prey. Ask what evidence *would* contradict it.

## Figures

**Fig 1 (PDF p4): SKELETON, TWO POSES, single-color drawing, evidence arrows.** Top: standing, bent neck, dip fishing (wading model). Bottom: swimming, straight neck, "based on Ibrahim et al., 2020a" (pursuit model). Black body with white bones. Scale 1 m.
- Specimens: caption says "Skeleton modified from the original by Genya Masukawa (used with permission) and scaled to the size of the neotype."
- It's an artist's skeletal, modified. No specimen numbers, and no coding of known vs inferred bones. "scaled to the size of the neotype" gives the size of FSAC-KK 11888 only. Doesn't mean the bones drawn all come from the neotype.
- Arrows A-W: white supports, gray ambiguous, black contradicts. Scored by the authors, and the arrows on the top skeleton read white or gray, matching their claim that nothing contradicts wading.
- Both models side by side, so students could score it themselves. [Brusatte 2021](brusatte-2021.md) Fig 2 is the same drawing without arrows.

**Fig 2 (PDF p5).** PCA of skull shape with hulls and PhyloPic silhouettes.

**Fig 3 (PDF p7).** Regressions of skull measures by ecotype. *Spinosaurus* in red.

**Fig 4 (PDF p8).** Ungual curvature vs length, with measuring diagram inset. Labels by genus. Sp and SB (*Spinosaurus B*) in red. Sd (yellow) "a further specimen attributed to a spinosaur." Ma and others above or near them.

**Fig 5 (PDF p8): SKULLS, black and white.** *Spinosaurus* (top) and *Baryonyx* (bottom), "scaled to the same size." "Not to scale." No specimens named. The *Spinosaurus* drawing has to be composite, since no one specimen preserves the whole skull. Text says all spinosaurid skulls are incomplete and reconstructed.

**Fig 6 (PDF p9): SKULLS at waterline.** A: snout dipped at 45 degrees (angle "based on Schade et al. 2020 for Irritator"). B: floating, with naris and orbit clear (per Arden 2019). C: tilted steeply back to expose nares. Same drawing as Fig 5, unspecified composite, angle borrowed from another genus.

**Fig 7 (PDF p10, not viewed).** Stork skull and living saddlebill feeding; crocodile skull and resting croc.

**Fig 8 (PDF p16).** Animal outline "modified from Ibrahim et al. (2020a)" under a wavy surface, showing ~6 m depth. Scale 1 m. Size is therefore that of Ibrahim's 2020 outline, and no specimen is named.

**Fig 9 (PDF p18).** Line drawings of mid-caudals. A *Spinosaurus* (Ibrahim 2020a, vertebra number not given). Top row, signaling: B *Bagaceratops*, C *Hydrosaurus*, D *Basiliscus*, E *Drepanosaurus*, F *Trioceros*. Bottom row, aquatic: G *Tomistoma*, H *Mystriosuchus*, I *Mosasaurus*, J *Pelamis*, K *Hovasaurus*. Strong sorting image: *Spinosaurus* looks like the top row. Authors chose the taxa, though. Scale bars differ widely, from 2 mm to 200 mm.
