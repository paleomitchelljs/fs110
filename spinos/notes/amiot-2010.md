# Amiot et al. 2010, "Oxygen isotope evidence for semi-aquatic habits among spinosaurid theropods"

*Geology* 38(2):139-142. DOI 10.1130/G30402.1. File: `spinos/papers/Amiotetal.20101.pdf`. PDF page = journal page minus 138. Figs 1-3 viewed (all on PDF p2). Table 1 on PDF p3. Written before the 2014 skeleton.

## Claim

Spinosaur tooth enamel has lower oxygen isotope values than coexisting theropods and matches crocodilians and turtles. The authors read that as time spent in water: "they spent a large part of their daily time in water, like extant crocodilians or hippopotamuses."

## Facts and quotes

- Data: 109 new + 24 published δ18Op values, 12 fluvial or fluvio-deltaic localities, Hauterivian-Barremian to early Cenomanian, all continents with spinosaurids. Enamel for teeth, shell bone for turtles. Teeth "of similar sizes" were chosen to limit body-size effects.
- Result: spinosaurs "1.3‰ lower than δ18Op values of other coexisting theropods (Wilcoxon signed rank, n = 9, p = 0.02), but not significantly different from δ18Op values of coexisting crocodilians (Wilcoxon signed rank, n = 9, p = 0.515) and turtles (Wilcoxon signed rank, n = 6, p = 0.345)."
- Modern analogy (Fig 1): hippos have lower values than coexisting zebra, buffalo, elephant, rhino at Tsavo and Amboseli, Kenya.
- Mechanism: water cuts "daily aerial evapotranspiration," a process that enriches body water in 18O. Also "elevated water turnovers" in semiaquatic animals.
- Why not diet: "A diet-related difference... is highly unlikely, because there is direct fossil evidence indicating an opportunistic feeding behavior among spinosaurs." Cites [Buffetaut 2004](buffetaut-2004.md).
- Why not diagenesis: terrestrial theropods stay offset from crocs and turtles "whatever their age and geographical location," and diagenesis would have homogenized them. Their wording is "at least partial preservation."
- Pre-2014 anatomy view: "their postcranial anatomy differs relatively little from that of usual large, bipedal theropods, and is not particularly suggestive of aquatic habits." And "apparent lack of anatomical adaptation."
- THE SPINOSAURUS EXCEPTION. "This semiaquatic oxygen isotope signature is not clearly observed for Spinosaurus from Tunisia and Morocco." Their reading: competition "may have forced some African spinosaurs to have a more opportunistic habitat use by alternating aquatic and terrestrial life." Results text: at some Moroccan and Tunisian localities spinosaur values "are either comparable to those of terrestrial theropods (location 12; Table 1) or extend from crocodilian and turtle values to terrestrial theropod values (locations 6, 8, and 11; Table 1)."
- Table 1, North African sites, mean δ18Op, spinosaurs vs other theropods vs crocodilians:
  - Loc 12 Bou Laalou, Morocco: 19.7 / 18.8 / 17.5. Spinosaurs are HIGHER than theropods here.
  - Loc 11 Khetitila Srhira, Morocco: 18.8 / 19.4 / 17.7.
  - Loc 10 Chaaft, Morocco: 18.4 / 21.3 / 19.6.
  - Loc 9 Takemout, Morocco: 18.7 / 20.0 / 19.9.
  - Loc 8 Jebel al Qabla, Morocco: 19.3 / 19.8 / 19.0.
  - Loc 6 Bateun El Hmaima, Tunisia: 19.5 (st. dev. 2.4, n = 3) / 20.5 / 18.3.
  - For contrast, Loc 1 Phu Wiang, Thailand: 13.5 / 25.6 (theropods far above). Loc 2 Khok Kong, Thailand: 16.1 (n = 1) / 21.1 / 15.8.
- So the largest gaps are at old Asian sites, likely not *Spinosaurus*. 3 of the 5 Moroccan sites (12, 11, 8) have gaps under 1‰ or reversed. Small samples per site: spinosaur N runs from 1 to 8.
- Close: "Stable oxygen isotopes unambiguously show for the first time that some dinosaurs... used freshwater environments more as a living habitat than just as temporary hunting (or fishing) grounds." "Unambiguously" is theirs. Compare the Moroccan rows above.

## Figures

**Fig 1 (PDF p2).** Plot of δ18O phosphate vs δ18O water. Tsavo and Amboseli mammals, with hippo (open circle) below the zebra, rhino, buffalo and elephant symbols. Fractionation lines for mammals, crocodilians and turtles.

**Fig 2 (PDF p2).** World map with numbered localities. Inset is a black silhouette of a bipedal *Spinosaurus*, "artist view of Spinosaurus (modified from Bogdanov's artwork)." NOT a specimen diagram. Pre-2014 body plan, with long legs.

**Fig 3 (PDF p2).** Dot plot. δ18O on x, localities (1)-(12) on y sorted by age. Diamonds = theropods, triangles = crocs, circles = turtles, open diamonds = spinosaurids. The open and filled diamonds separate strongly at the old Asian sites at the bottom and much less at the Moroccan sites at the top. The whole argument is visible here.

**Table 1 (PDF p3).** Locality means and N.

No skeletal diagrams in this paper.

## Ideal images

1. Fig 3: best single image. The separation changes by locality. Good prompt: "Where is *Spinosaurus* on this plot?"
2. Fig 1: hippo vs zebra, the modern control that makes Fig 3 interpretable.
3. Table 1 North Africa rows, if a number is wanted on a slide.
4. Fig 2: map only. The inset silhouette is useful as a before-2014 picture of the animal.
