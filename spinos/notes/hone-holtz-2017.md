# Hone & Holtz 2017, "A Century of Spinosaurs - A Review and Revision of the Spinosauridae with Comments on Their Ecology"

*Acta Geologica Sinica (English Edition)* 91(3):1120-1132. File: `spinos/papers/A_Century_of_Spinosaurs_-_A_Review_and_Revision_of.pdf`, 13 pages. PDF page = journal page minus 1119.

The PDF is an image scan with no text layer. I ran OCR at 200 dpi. Quotes below come from the OCR, so verify against the page before putting any on a slide. Figs 1, 2, 5, 6 viewed. Figs 3, 4 known from captions. Print is black and white in this scan apart from Fig 6.

## Claim

Spinosaurs are a poorly known clade, "known primarily from isolated teeth," and many claims outrun the material. Written after Ibrahim 2014 and before Ibrahim 2020.

"The evidence that at least some spinosaurs spent considerable time in and around water is highly convincing." But: "the idea that spinosaurs generally were fully adapted to an aquatic lifestyle... is limited."

## Facts and quotes

Limits of the record:
- "No spinosaur has yet been found with a complete skull or a complete limb, and there are only few complete series for a single part of the axial column."
- Most complete: "the Baryonyx holotype, is known from a partial skull, some elements of the cervical, dorsal, and caudal series, cervical and dorsal ribs, the pectoral girdle and much of the forelimbs and partial hindlimbs... but no one unit (e.g. the cervical series, the pelvis) is complete."
- "reconstructions of species are often based on multiple specimens or taxa. Claims of extraordinary sizes should be tempered without complete or near-complete specimens."

History:
- Stromer 1915. Holotype loss "as a result of an allied bomb during World War II." Fig 2 caption: "before its loss in 1945." [Brusatte 2021](brusatte-2021.md) says "April 1944," so the caption looks wrong. Smith et al. 2006 should settle it.
- Owen, early 1840s, described spinosaur teeth from Mantell as crocodilian. Fun: first spinosaur fossils were called crocodile.
- Buffetaut 1992 first linked *Baryonyx* to *Spinosaurus*.
- Taxonomy unsettled: "This taxonomic quandary would seem to be presently unresolvable." Ibrahim 2014 lumps. Evers 2015 splits *Sigilmassasaurus* from *Spinosaurus*.

Taxonomy used here:
- Baryonychinae: *Baryonyx*, *Suchomimus*, *Ichthyovenator*. Note [Arden 2019](arden-2019.md) and [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) put *Ichthyovenator* in Spinosaurinae.
- Spinosaurinae: *Oxalaia*, *Irritator*, *Spinosaurus*, *Sigilmassasaurus*.

Anatomy:
- Spines: *Baryonyx* and *Suchomimus* "neural spine much over twice the height of the centrum" at most. *Ichthyovenator* > 3 times. Ibrahim diagnoses *Spinosaurus* as "dorsal neural spine height up to ten times greater than centrum height."
- Skull: premaxillary rosette, sinuous tooth row, secondary palate. Six to seven premaxillary teeth versus four in most Tetanurae.
- Forelimb: "radius is robust and typically close to only half the length of the humerus," enlarged olecranon.
- Holotype diagnosis in Evers 2015: big front teeth, minute middle teeth, large posterior teeth in the dentary; "strong, rounded expansion at the base of the neural spines."

Forelimb function:
- Against bear-style fishing: "fishing with arms held under the head that could not be seen by eyes would seem, at best, a sub-optimal strategy."
- Their own suggestion: digging. "much in common with animals that engage in scratch-digging and hook-and-pull digging." Nests, water, prey. "Digging is not the only hypothesis that would fit."
- On quadrupedality: "hampered by the lack of definitive (and in particular, associated) forelimb material for the genus."

Nares, 2 statements that disagree:
- Anatomy section: "somewhat dorsally positioned (Sues et al., 2002)."
- Ecology section: "primarily laterally positioned and not dorsally located." Their later papers keep the second.
- "A partially or fully submerged animal would be obliged to raise much of the head above the water to breathe, and so perhaps these animals were typically more terrestrial and instead waded and dipped into the water to feed... as with many modern shore-birds."

Diet: "remarkably varied." Fish, juvenile iguanodontid, pterosaur. "Spinosaurs have also been suggested to be specialist scavengers" (Kitchener), rejected because viscera go first and because of high local abundance.

Sail: Stromer called the fatty hump "extremely improbable" and favored a *Dimetrodon*-like sail. Socio-sexual signal favored. Gimsa 2016 (cited as 2015) dismissed: "based on a number of unverifiable assumptions about the anatomy and locomotion of these animals and so is not here considered a viable hypothesis." Compare [Gimsa 2016](gimsa-2016.md).

Distribution: Tithonian (c. 147 Ma) to Late Santonian (c. 85 Ma), both by teeth. Europe, Africa, Brazil, Asia. "Bizarrely... they are currently unknown from any of the considerable number of well-studied North American Cretaceous localities."

Teeth caution: "worn spinosaur teeth may be hard to separate from those of sympatric crocodilians." Relevant to [Beevor 2021](beevor-2021.md) and [Amiot 2010](amiot-2010.md).

## Figures

**Fig 1 (PDF p2): SKELETON, BARYONYX, NOT SPINOSAURUS.** "Bauplan of a typical spinosaur - the British baryonychine Baronyx [sic] walkeri." "Skeletal reconstruction by Scott Hartman, used with permission." White bones on black silhouette, left lateral, bipedal, walking. Scale 1 m.
- No specimen number in the caption. The holotype named in the text is BMNH R9951, and it's the obvious basis, but the caption doesn't say that this drawing shows only that animal. Text also says no one unit of the holotype is complete, so the drawing must include inferred bones.
- No known/inferred coding in this version. Bones all white. Body shape is long-legged, low-sailed, unlike any of the *Spinosaurus* reconstructions.

**Fig 2 (PDF p2): PHOTO, THE HOLOTYPE ITSELF.** "Photograph of the holotype material of Spinosaurus on display in Munich before its loss in 1945." BSPG 1912 VIII 19 per other papers. Black and white. Vertebrae and tall neural spines mounted in a row on a wall, 7 tall spines with their centra, a few shorter pieces at either end, a board of teeth at the right, the lower jaw below it. Nothing is reconstructed: it's the actual bones. The only direct photo of the holotype in the folder. Spine bases show the strong expansion mentioned in the diagnosis. Reads very well, even in scan.

**Fig 3 (PDF p4, not viewed).** Simple tree after Allain 2012.

**Fig 4 (PDF p4, not viewed).** Tooth CMP 3760, with close-up of enamel fluting.

**Fig 5 (PDF p7): PHOTOS, three specimens.** a: rostrum of *Spinosaurus* MSNM V4047, in dorsal, lateral and ventral, with terminal rosette and alveoli. b: dorsal neural spine of *Ichthyovenator* MDS BK10-01 to 15, with vertebra. c: *Baryonyx* humerus, left, lateral view, BMNH R9951. d: first manual ungual of *Baryonyx*, BMNH R9951 (caption lists it under c, panel is labelled d). All scales 100 mm.

**Fig 6 (PDF p10).** Life art by Matt van Rooijen, a spinosaurid in "a speculative threat display posture." No specimen, no taxon named. Decorative.

## Ideal images

1. Fig 2: the lost holotype on the Munich wall. Strong opener for "what happened to the type specimen."
2. Fig 5a: MSNM V4047, the giant snout, 3 views. Best clean shot of the rostrum here.
3. Fig 1: *Baryonyx* skeletal, as the baseline spinosaur to set against *Spinosaurus*.
4. Fig 5c and d: humerus and claw, for forelimb function.
