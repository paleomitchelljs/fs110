# Fabbri et al. 2022, "Subaqueous foraging among carnivorous dinosaurs"

*Nature* 603:852-857, DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-04528-0 (citation from the Sereno 2022 reference list, since the PDF doesn't print it). File: `spinos/papers/Fabbri_et_al_2022_subaqueous_foraging_among.pdf`, 39 pages, line-numbered author manuscript. PDF page = printed page number. Figures on PDF p27-29, Extended Data Figs 1-10 on p30-39. Figs 1, 2 and Ext Data Fig 10 viewed. Fig 3 known from caption. Supplement not in folder.

Fig 1 embedded image is 1178 px wide, about 220 dpi. Usable, though the published version would be sharper.

## Claim

Across amniotes, high bone density goes with frequent underwater foraging. So *Spinosaurus* and *Baryonyx* foraged underwater, *Suchomimus* did not, and spinosaurids were "aquatic specialists with surprising ecological disparity."

## Facts and quotes

Data: femora and dorsal ribs across amniotes, total 380 observations, 174 taxa for ribs, 83 overlapping. Femur n in Fig 2 = 206 observations, 200 taxa. Text drops the femur taxon count ("of and 174") in this manuscript, a typesetting gap. New non-avian dinosaur data: 36 femora and 12 ribs.

Scoring: subaqueous foraging 0 unable, 1 able but infrequent, 2 frequent. Flight scored separately.

Best model: "bone compactness ~ subaqueous foraging" (AICc weight 0.673 femur, 0.638 rib). p < 0.001. Text gives median λ 0.97 and 0.969. Table 1 prints λ 0.919 for the femur model and 0.969 for rib. R2 in Table 1: 0.172 femur and 0.108 rib, so foraging class accounts for 17% and 11% of the variance in the best model.

Classification: correct 84-85% femora, 83-84% ribs. "This increases to 90%" without graviportal and deep diving taxa.

Spinosaurid results, median probability of subaqueous foraging:
- *Spinosaurus*: 100% femur, 95% rib. Femur bd=0.968, rib bd=0.931.
- *Baryonyx*: 98% femur, 96% rib. Femur bd=0.876, rib bd=0.921.
- *Suchomimus*: 31% femur. Femur bd=0.682. No rib. [Gordon 2025](gordon-2025.md) gets the opposite for its forelimb, highly aquatic, and Fabbri's own *Spinosaurus* forelimb is the one that falls short there.

Sample underlying that:
- "the holotype of Baryonyx (NHM R 9951), two individuals of Suchomimus (G51, G94), and the neotype of Spinosaurus (FSAC-KK 11888)."
- So the ecological claim for *Spinosaurus* and *Baryonyx* rests on one individual each. The "disparity" between *Baryonyx* and its sister *Suchomimus* rests on one vs two. Sereno 2022 notes another *Spinosaurus* femur with a narrow cavity (NMC 41869).
- Ages differ: "skeletally mature specimen of Suchomimus (G51) and the neotypic skeletally immature individual of Spinosaurus."

What bone density can't separate. Their words, important:
- "High bone density is therefore an excellent indicator for the initial stages of aquatic adaptation, but poorly distinguishes between wading, deep diving, and terrestrial habits."
- "Infrequent subaqueous foraging and wading behavior are not significantly associated with variation in bone density." "wading birds that feed in water but rarely submerge... have similar compactness to non-aquatic taxa."
- So on this method, a wader looks like a land animal. Dense bone argues against a pure wader, which is what makes *Spinosaurus* the test case. But the *Suchomimus* "wading predator" suggestion cannot be tested by the same method.
- Graviportal confound: "Large-bodied terrestrial amniotes have relatively high femoral compactness related to graviportality." Their answer: "graviportality does not affect rib compactness," and columnar limbs differ. That's why the rib matters. Compare Sereno 2022 on infilling for weight support.

Other inferences:
- Osteosclerosis across elements "rendering previous biomechanical models inaccurate" (cites Henderson). Strong claim, no recalculation given in the manuscript. Henderson 2018 had asked what denser skeleton would do, and found it still floats. See [Henderson 2018](henderson-2018.md).
- "tentatively suggests that subaqueous foraging, is ancestral for Spinosauridae... the absence of osteosclerosis in Suchomimus results from secondary loss."
- "Because of their unique anatomy, spinosaurids may have had ecologies with no modern equivalent, limiting direct autecological interpretations based on modern taxa."
- Scope: "Spinosauridae is the only clade of non-avian dinosaurs with unambiguous evidence of subaqueous foraging." "the invasion of freshwater ecosystems." Other proposed aquatic dinosaurs (ornithomimosaurs, halszkaraptorines, ornithopods) "show open medullary cavities."

Terminology: opens with "Secondary adaptations to aquatic lifestyles, such as wading behavior... subaqueous foraging... and deep diving." So wading counts as an aquatic lifestyle here, which is what [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) objects to. Fabbri calls *Suchomimus* a possible wader and calls Spinosauridae "ecomorphologically adapted to life in water."

## Replies

- April 2022, preprint: "Spinosaurids as 'subaqueous foragers' undermined by selective sampling and problematic statistical inference," bioRxiv 10.1101/2022.04.13.487781. In the folder as `myhrvold-2022-subaqueous-critique-preprint.pdf`.
- May 2022, Fabbri et al. reply: "Sinking a giant: quantitative macroevolutionary comparative methods debunk qualitative assumptions," bioRxiv 10.1101/2022.05.05.490811. See [Fabbri reply 2022](fabbri-reply-2022.md).
- 2024, [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md): "allows us to confidently dismiss those findings."

## Figures

**Fig 1a (PDF p27).** Black-and-white cross sections with bd values. Femora: *Aramus* 0.323, *Fratercula* 0.702, *Troodon* 0.665, *Tyrannosaurus* 0.656, *Antetonitrus* 0.702, *Caiman* 0.929, *Ichthyosaurus* 0.659. Ribs: *Aramus* 0.728, *Gavia* 0.87, *Lourinhanosaurus* 0.631, carcharodontosaurid 0.805, *Alamosaurus* 0.523, *Nothosaurus* 0.949, *Mollesaurus* 735 (sic, printed without decimal). Direct visual, and it's a real-data panel. Note the deep divers (*Ichthyosaurus* 0.659) score like a *T. rex*.

**Fig 1b (PDF p27): SKELETONS, SINGLE-INDIVIDUAL, ORANGE = PRESERVED.** Tree with three skeletals and sections. Caption: "Skeletal reconstructions are based on single individuals (holotype of Baryonyx and neotype of Spinosaurus), exception made for Suchomimus (see Supplementary Information for further details); preserved bones are highlighted in orange."
- *Baryonyx walkeri* NHM R 9951: holotype only. White on black, orange on skull fragments, some neck and trunk, hip, hindlimb parts, forelimb. Scale 1 m.
- *Suchomimus tenerensis* G51, G70, G94: three specimens pooled, orange across pelvis, back, proximal limbs. Scale 1 m. [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md) says G51 and G94 are field numbers for holotype MNBH GAD500 and subadult MNBH GAD70, that "Neither of these specimens were sectioned," and that "We do not believe these thin sections pertain to Suchomimus."
- *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus* FSAC-KK 11888: neotype only. This is the same drawing as [Ibrahim 2020](ibrahim-2020.md) Fig 1g, now with what the neotype really preserves in orange. Read at 300 dpi: most of the tail (centra and part of the spines), hip region, femur, tibia, fibula, feet. Some dorsal spines. Patches on the skull and upper neck. Neck mostly white. Forelimb nearly all white. Snout white apart from a small orange section.
- So white here means not preserved in this individual. It may be known from other specimens. Compare [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) Fig 1A (color by specimen) and [Ibrahim 2014](ibrahim-2014.md) Fig 2A (red pools specimens).
- Sections beside each: *Baryonyx* femur and rib. *Suchomimus* femur only. *Spinosaurus* femur and rib. *Spinosaurus* femur looks nearly solid. *Suchomimus* has a large open white center.

**Fig 2 (PDF p28).** a: PGLS bone density vs log femur diameter. b: violin plots by lifestyle with three spinosaurid values marked. c: time-scaled archosaur tree with density color and probabilities. Silhouettes are symbols. The violins (2b) read at slide size, the tree (2c) does not.

**Fig 3 (PDF p29, not viewed).** Rib equivalent of Fig 2.

**Table 1 (PDF p26).** Models, AICc, weights, R2, lambda.

**Ext Data Figs 1-7 (PDF p30-36).** Arrays of femoral and rib sections with bd. Not viewed one by one.

**Ext Data Figs 8, 9 (PDF p37-38).** Density on consensus tree.

**Ext Data Fig 10 (PDF p39).** Side-by-side sections: scapula *Baryonyx* 0.954 vs *Rahonavis* 0.602; rib *Baryonyx* 0.921 vs *Saltriovenator* 0.704; dorsal neural spine *Spinosaurus* 0.929 vs *Ouranosaurus* 0.58; tibia *Spinosaurus* 0.946 vs *Tyrannosaurus* 0.692.
