# Ibrahim et al. 2020, "Tail-propelled aquatic locomotion in a theropod dinosaur"

*Nature* 581:67-70, DOI 10.1038/s41586-020-2190-3 (citation taken from the Sereno 2022 reference list, since the PDF doesn't print it). File: `spinos/papers/Tail_propelled_aquatic_locomotion_in_a_t.pdf`, 22 pages, author manuscript, unformatted. Figs 1, 2, 3 on PDF p13, 14, 16.

Limits of this file:
- Embedded images are low resolution: 685 px wide, about 105 dpi. Fine for reading, soft on a slide. The published Nature PDF would project better.
- Extended Data Figs 1-8 and the Supplementary Information are cited but are not in the file. That includes Ext Data Fig 3 (site and tail in juxtaposition), Fig 6 (compare with Stromer's drawings), Fig 7 (life reconstruction), Fig 8 (center of mass vs prior analyses).

## Claim

The neotype's tail is a tall, flexible fin. A plastic model of its shape in a flume makes more thrust than terrestrial theropod tail shapes, so *Spinosaurus* swam with its tail and was "an active, highly specialised aquatic predator that pursued and caught its prey in the water column."

## Facts and quotes

- Stated strength: "the first unambiguous evidence for an aquatic propulsive structure in a dinosaur."
- Specimen: "a nearly complete, partially articulated tail of a subadult individual of Spinosaurus aegyptiacus (FSAC-KK 11888)." Zrigat hill, Kem Kem beds, southeastern Morocco.
- Tail: "More than 30 near-sequential caudal vertebrae (located within caudal positions 1-41)," "approximately 80% of original tail length." Over 90% of the new material came from "field excavations in late 2018."
- Association: tail "was found in direct juxtaposition to the remainder of the skeleton." Histology on 5 kinds of element (right femur, left fibula, rib, gastralium, two neural spines) was run to test for "a chimeric association." Result is in the Supplement, not in this file. [Hartman 2020](hartman-2014-2020.md) still questions the first-collected presacrals.
- History: "accidental discovery and partial excavation by a local collector in 2008." Expeditions 2015-2019, four joint trips.
- Spines: "spines of proximal caudals are about three times taller than their centra"; distal spines "well over seven times the height of the centrum." Mid-distal spines are "proximo-distally rather than transversely flattened."
- Chevrons: "distal chevrons are as elongate as the proximal chevrons."
- Flexibility: zygapophyses "not only do not overlap but almost disappear... allowing the caudal region considerable flexibility, especially with regard to lateral movements." Compare with Sereno's "pliant billboard" and segment-crossing spines.
- Experiment: 2D shapes cut from 0.93 mm plastic of one stiffness, all scaled to 20 cm, driven by a robotic flapper in a flume at 10 cm/s, 1 Hz. *Spinosaurus* tested N = 10, others N = 5. Comparison shapes: *Coelophysis*, *Allosaurus*, *Crocodylus*, *Triturus*, and a rectangle of equal area (63 cm2).
- Result: "more than eight times the thrust of the other theropod tail shapes and achieved 2.6 times the efficiency." "greatest thrust was achieved by the crested newt tail shape (1.8 times Spinosaurus; 14.8 times Coelophysis)." Crocodile had the best efficiency among animal shapes, "1.5 times Spinosaurus."
- Control: "The lower efficiency recovered in this experiment for Spinosaurus (compared to the control with the same surface area)... indicates an effect of tail shape."
- Habitat: "a major fluvio-deltaic system" with "a rich freshwater fauna." They do not predict marine-only fossils. See [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md).
- Kem Kem paradox: "several large-bodied predators, but few terrestrial herbivores, is partially explained by the largely aquatic, likely piscivorous, lifestyle of Spinosaurus."
- Rolling: "may have also increased lateral stability of the body in the water, reducing the tendency to roll while floating," a reply to [Henderson 2018](henderson-2018.md). Stated as "may," no measurement in this file.
- Scope: "a significant invasion of aquatic environments by dinosaurs," "a persistent and widespread invasion of aquatic habitats." Other spinosaurids are "partially aquatic" and "The exact extent... remains to be established."
- Pose against Henderson: "Contrary to recent suggestions that Spinosaurus was confined to wading."
- Depth: [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md) argues the flume tail ran "submerged to a depth equal to or greater than half the length of the tail," so at full size "at least 5 m below the surface." "Although they never used the words 'dive' or 'diving' in the paper, there is little other means to fully submerge."
- Coauthor Davide Bonadonna "sculpted the life reconstruction." His painting is Fig 1 of [Brusatte 2021](brusatte-2021.md).

## Reading Fig 3 against the text

Read off the plot by eye, so these are approximate:
- Thrust (N): control ~0.028, *Coelophysis* ~0.003, *Allosaurus* ~0.008, *Spinosaurus* ~0.025, *Crocodylus* ~0.040, *Triturus* ~0.045.
- Efficiency: control ~0.195, *Coelophysis* ~0.05, *Allosaurus* ~0.05, *Spinosaurus* ~0.125, *Crocodylus* ~0.19, *Triturus* ~0.155.
- The 8.1x bracket on the plot runs from *Coelophysis* to *Spinosaurus*. Against *Allosaurus*, the large theropod, the ratio is closer to 3x. The text says "more than eight times... the other theropod tail shapes." Plural. The plot doesn't support that for *Allosaurus*.
- A plain rectangle of the same area beats the *Spinosaurus* shape in both thrust and efficiency. The test compares shapes, and it largely rewards area. Tails only, with no body attached, so no drag from the front half or sail. That is the complaint in [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md): "failed to take account of the bizarre anterior half of the animal."
- Fair to the authors: they ran the control and printed it.

## Figures

**Fig 1a-b (PDF p13): TAIL OF ONE SPECIMEN.** FSAC-KK 11888 only. "preserved parts in colour." Orange/brown = found, white outline = reconstructed. a: dorsal. b: left lateral. Ca4, Ca15, Ca31 labelled. Scale 50 cm. The gaps are visible directly, which Fig 1g doesn't allow. Spine tops in the distal half are largely white outline, so height there is partly inferred.

**Fig 1c-e.** Reconstructed cross sections with muscles at Ca4, Ca15, Ca31 (spinalis, longissimus, caudofemoralis, ilio-ischiocaudalis). Inferred soft tissue.

**Fig 1f.** Neural spine of Ca23, sections.

**Fig 1g (PDF p13): SKELETON, whole animal, ONE COLOR.** White bones on a black body silhouette, left lateral, limbs trailing, tail extended. Scale 1 m. Caption: "Reconstructed skeleton and caudal series of Spinosaurus FSAC-KK 11888. ...g, skeletal reconstruction."
- What it shows: the caption names only FSAC-KK 11888, but the neotype doesn't preserve all of this. No distinction between known and inferred bones in this panel. Sources for the rest aren't stated in the caption. [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md) Fig 1b reprints the same drawing with the neotype's preserved bones in orange, which shows how much of it is that one animal. Use that alongside this.
- Sereno 2022 describes this drawing as "based primarily on holotypic and neotypic specimens" and reprints a colored version in its Fig 9G. The coloring isn't in this file, and its key isn't given in Sereno's caption.
- Size: on the printed bar, the animal measures about 10.5 m (my measurement, roughly 1050 px at 100 px per m). That fits a subadult at individual size and doesn't fit the >15 m adult of 2014. Sereno's 76% of ~14 m gives ~10.6 m. So don't compare it to 2014's Fig 2A by size without saying so.
- Tail, compared with [Ibrahim 2014](ibrahim-2014.md) Fig 2A: the 2014 tail tapers with low spines. Here it's a deep paddle along its whole length, with the skin outline drawn well beyond the bones.
- Pose: forelimbs and hindlimbs trailing, swim posture. No air or water surface drawn.

**Fig 2 (PDF p14).** Photos of FSAC-KK 11888 caudals and chevrons. a: Ca4. b-c: Chv7. d: Ca31, with its long inclined spine. e: Ca12. f-g: Chv27. h: Ca16. Heavily labelled. All one specimen. Scale 5-10 cm.

**Fig 3 (PDF p16).** a: orange plastic *Spinosaurus* tail shape on the drive shaft, 20 cm, flow arrows. b: thrust lollipop chart with silhouettes, 8.1x bracket. c: efficiency lollipop chart, 2.6x bracket. Silhouettes are outlines, not specimens.
