## Short version

Hartman withdrew this correction himself in 2020. The 27% came from a measurement mismatch: the 2014 table measured vertebrae "rim to rim," and he had assumed full centrum length. His objection then moved to a different one: FSAC-KK 11888's leg proportions don't match Stromer's Spinosaurus B once the same landmarks are used. So the picture is not a live alternative skeleton. It's a dated episode in the argument.

## Sequence

**12 Sep 2014, Hartman posts the image, a day or so after publication.**
- "the ilium in the reconstruction from the paper is much too small relative to the vertebrae from the same specimen. In fact it needs to be increased about 27% (e.g. x 1.27) to match the published length."
- "the rest of the pelvis and the entire hindlimb is off, and they are off by about the same amount."
- "At the very it least it calls into question the idea that Spinosaurus was an obligate quadruped on land." (typo his)
- Credit to the authors in the same post: "they are to be commended for the supplemental figure, which so clearly labels which bones are based on which specimens."

**18 Sep 2014, the reply, signed "Nizar Ibrahim, Simone Maganuco, Tyler Keillor, Matteo Fabbri."** Printed on Hartman's own site.
- "This detail was omitted from the table legend - we are measuring from rim to rim, not including the anterior condyle."
- "your 'corrected' skeleton, assembled within a day or so of our publication, has suggested to some that the actual reconstruction, based on the fossil material, was 'fishy'. That is not the case."
- "The proportions of the digital skeleton are correct, according to our identifications."
- On one animal or several: "The new remains come from a site with just one individual."
- "We are not saying that our reconstruction is 100% perfect."
- "We also know from several lines of evidence that the vertebrae of 'Sigilmassasaurus' belong to Spinosaurus."

**22 Sep 2014, Witton.** Per the search summary, he replicated Hartman's numbers, and "the legs should... be c. 25-27% larger." Hartman 2020 says the same: "Mark Witton replicated my measurements and found the same thing."

**Nov 2020, Hartman withdraws.**
- "Despite near-identical results we were both unwittingly in error, because the authors of the 2014 paper reached out to us to clarify that their vertebral measurements table was not reporting the entire length of the vertebral centrum as we had assumed, but were instead reporting 'rim to rim' measurements that did not include the condyle (the anterior ball) in the length."
- "the full centrum length in this case is 33% longer than the rim to rim measurement."
- "when Mark and I checked the scaling based on rim to rim measurements the 2014 model checked out."

**Nov 2020, the new objection.** The same landmark problem, turned on the comparison with Stromer. His tables give "größte Länge" (greatest length), while the FSAC dorsals are rim to rim.
- Dorsal centrum to tibia ratio, higher means shorter legs. Cau's blog: 25% FSAC, 23% Spinosaurus B. Evers 2015 p72: 26.9% and 23.3%.
- Hartman, after correcting FSAC dorsals upward by 26.3%: "I get a dorsal to tibia ratio of 23.96% for Spinosaurus B... But I get a ginormous 32.76% for FSAC-KK 11888."
- "the proposed leg proportions of FSAC-KK 11888 simply do not come close to matching those Stromer recorded for Spinosaurus B."
- "Does that mean the FSAC specimen is a chimera, at least as regards some of the presacral material that was privately collected under uncontrolled circumstances? It certainly raises suspicions, but in the end I can't say for certain."
- "if the FSAC proportions are valid they do not match Spinosaurus B, and it would call into serious question whether FSAC-KK 11888 really is Spinosaurus."
- His own reconstruction: "I went with Spino B proportions to guide my skeletal reconstruction."
- Limits: this relies on correction factors estimated from photos ("If you are understandably concerned about the use of photos this way"), and he says, "I will need to someday recheck these numbers when better data becomes available."

Against the chimera idea, [Ibrahim 2020](ibrahim-2020.md) ran histology on the neotype to test for "a chimeric association," with the tail "in direct juxtaposition" to the rest of the skeleton. The results are in its Supplement, which isn't in the folder. Those excavations were systematic, while Hartman's doubt is aimed at the first-collected presacrals.

## Figures

**`hartman-proportions-correction.png`: TWO SKELETONS, SAME COMPOSITE.** Caption on his post: "Original at the top, corrected at the bottom."
- Both panels are [Ibrahim 2014](ibrahim-2014.md) Fig S3, so both show the same specimens and the same colors (neotype FSAC-KK 11888, Stromer's Egyptian specimens, isolated Moroccan bones, other genera, restored bones).
- The only thing changed in the bottom panel is the ilium, rest of the pelvis and hindlimb, all bright red, all FSAC-KK 11888, enlarged about x1.27 against the rest of the animal.
- Neck, sail, forelimb and tail are untouched. The bottom panel has a small H-shaped bar on a mid dorsal, which I take to be his D8 reference length (my inference).
- It doesn't add a specimen, and it doesn't resize the animal as a whole.

**S2 rim-to-rim image.** Road III shows a vertebra, "modified from Ibrahim, et al. (2014) supplemental figure S2," with the two ways of measuring it. Not in the folder. It explains the whole dispute in one picture, so it's worth getting from the post.

**Hartman's 2020 skeletal.** The series ends in his own reconstruction using Spinosaurus B proportions. Not in the folder and not viewed. This is the current version of his view, if you want one.
