# Myhrvold et al. 2024, "Diving dinosaurs? Caveats on the use of bone compactness and pFDA for inferring lifestyle"

Myhrvold, Baumgart, Vidal, Fish, Henderson, Saitta & Sereno 2024. *PLoS ONE* 19(3):e0298957. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0298957. File: `spinos/papers/journal.pone.0298957.pdf`, 79 pages, CC-BY, 300 dpi images. PDF page = printed page. Read: history, bone ballast, "unsupported inferences," spinosaurid measurements, conclusions. Statistics sections sampled, and S1 Appendix not in the folder. Figs 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9 viewed. Others from captions.

Myhrvold and Sereno "contributed equally." Both were authors on [Ibrahim 2014](ibrahim-2014.md). A full reply to [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md), whose lead author was also on Ibrahim 2014.

## Claim

Bone compactness and pFDA, as Fabbri used them, can't tell you whether a dinosaur foraged underwater. "These findings not only invalidate the conclusions of the particular analysis of Fabbri et al. but also have important implications for future quantitative uses of bone compactness and discriminant analysis in paleontology."

## The center of mass admission

This matters beyond bone density. On the Ibrahim 2014 quadruped:

"That calculation (made by PCS, one of the current authors) has since been recognized to have erroneously shifted the center of mass forward from the hip by the addition, rather than the subtraction, of the estimated volume of internal air space."

PCS is Paul C. Sereno. So the 2014 mid-trunk center of mass was a sign error, by Sereno's own account in a paper he co-led. [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md) instead attributes the forward position to the skeleton's front-loading. [Henderson 2018](henderson-2018.md) couldn't reproduce the 2014 result from the same proportions. This sentence explains why, and it's a good moment for the personal side of the debate.

## Facts and quotes

Taxa that don't fit the "subaqueous forager" class:
- Fabbri's class includes the "common hippo," "pygmy hippo," "common tapir," "Malayan tapir," "beaver," "European water vole," which "forage substantially--in some cases exclusively--on land and above water." Mink and desman eat land prey too. Alligator and Nile crocodile "lunges above the water to capture prey are clearly not 'fully submerged behavior'."
- Gharial, a better underwater feeder, "not included."
- Fabbri's reply preprint [16] redefined the shared property as "habitual subaqueous submersion." So: "the pFDA analysis of Fabbri et al. is unable to infer anything about spinosaurid foraging."
- That reply preprint: [Fabbri reply 2022](fabbri-reply-2022.md).

Bone ballast logic:
- "denser bones lead to a denser body, which can facilitate diving and compensate for larger lung capacity, but the increased mass also makes animals less maneuverable."
- "high skeletal Cg is more common in slow swimmers or bottom walkers, including sirenians, sea otters, and hippos." "increased bone density is typically not found in fast swimmers or pursuit predators."
- Cg examples: dugong 0.994, manatee 0.977, sea otter 0.908, Bryde's whale 0.611, harbor seal 0.436. So dense bones would argue against a fast pursuit predator, whichever way they cut on diving.
- [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md) R2 is 17.2% and 10.8%. "about 17.2% of the total variation in Cg."
- Individual variation: median 18.6% in Cg between individuals in the same taxon, range 1.6 to 46.1%. "the variation among individuals could be larger than the differences in Cg that Fabbri et al. found." Up to ~35% along a single *Nothosaurus* rib.

Spinosaurid numbers:
- Neotype FSAC-KK 11888 femur: Fabbri 0.968. Here, from the thin section: Cg 0.991 unfilled, 0.998 cracks filled. So Myhrvold finds it *denser*. "We have not suggested at any point that there is a medullary cavity in the neotypic femur."
- CMN 41869 (= NMC 41869 in Sereno 2022), "a second subadult specimen" with a medullary cavity: Cg 0.804, 0.849, 0.888 by threshold. Fabbri's reply gives 0.914 for it.
- "Only two subadult femora are available for Spinosaurus--too few to generalize whether such variation is common or rare."
- CMN 50382, juvenile femur, 11.8 cm, an animal "approximately ~2.0 m." Large cavity, Cg ~0.695. "Ontogenetic infilling of the medullary cavity does not appear to have been initiated."
- *Baryonyx* NHMUK 9951: Fabbri 0.876. Here, 0.767 to 0.887. Mean 0.773 with wider masking.
- *Suchomimus*: Fabbri 0.682. Here "that value seems artificially low." Thin section MNBH GAD99.
- Fabbri's "G51" and "G94" are, per this paper, field numbers for holotype MNBH GAD500 and subadult MNBH GAD70. "Neither of these specimens were sectioned, however." "We do not believe these thin sections pertain to Suchomimus." "One of us (PCS) made a four-part thin section" from MNBH GAD99.

Statistics:
- Correct classification 83-85%, so "a misclassification rate of 15-17%."
- Threshold should be the lower 95% CI bound, not the median, per their heuristic.
- "the datasets are not statistically distinguishable from a uniform random distribution." The body text says "some portions of the dataset" (p8), and the conclusion says "the datasets" (p66). Use the narrower wording if quoting.
- "we discovered a flaw in the pFDA code" (the matrix is transposed).
- Table 9, read from flattened text, check p57: even on their alternative Cg values, *Spinosaurus* medians run 0.81 to 0.98 and *Suchomimus* 0.31 to 0.50 in the F0D0 vs F0D2 runs. So the *ranking* doesn't flip under their own values. Their argument is that those numbers don't mean what Fabbri says. Fair to say in class.
- Most generous reading: "*Spinosaurus* and *Baryonyx* (but not *Suchomimus*) have a slight statistical affinity with animals that have a range of semiaquatic adaptations."

Buoyancy claim:
- "The quantitative impact of vertebral pneumaticity in Spinosaurus is so strong that calculations of body density from 3-D flesh models have found specimens of this taxon to be unsinkable." Those calculations are by these same authors (Henderson; Sereno 2022).
- Tail test critique: Ibrahim's flume tail was "submerged to a depth equal to or greater than half the length of the tail," so at full scale "at least 5 m below the surface." "Although they never used the words 'dive' or 'diving' in the paper, there is little other means to fully submerge."
- Dense bone alternative: "The very large body mass of this species offers one obvious alternative explanation."

Conclusion:
- "The unusual constellation of so many different problems allows us to confidently dismiss those findings."
- "our results by themselves do not settle the debate or add new independent lines of evidence."
- "semiaquatic piscivore but not an aquatic pursuit predator."

Useful history (p2-5): short, fair-looking summary from Charig & Milner to 2022, with long quotes. Good source for the deck's chronology, though written by one side. Charig & Milner on *Baryonyx*: "It probably crouched on the banks of lakes, creeks and rivers or waded in the shallows."
