# Cooper 2026, "New study revealing evidence of salt glands in spinosaurs adds to the great 'aquatic' debate"

*Historical Biology*, published online 16 Jun 2026. DOI 10.1080/08912963.2026.2686503. File: `spinos/papers/New study revealing evidence of salt glands in spinosaurs adds to the great  aquatic  debate.pdf` (note double spaces in the filename), 4 pages. PDF p1 is the cover. An editorial on a paper in the same journal.

Secondary source. The paper it describes is now in the folder: [Cau 2026](cau-2026.md). Use that for anything specific. This note records Cooper's framing.

## Claim (Cooper's account of Cau)

Derived spinosaurs had salt glands in the skull roof, comparable to marine birds, tied to a shift into saline or brackish habitats.

## Facts and quotes

- Material: "spinosaur cranial material from three different museum collections, including at least one specimen of the genus Spinosaurus, further investigating CT datasets and published imagery." No specimen numbers given. Compared with marine iguanas and extant birds.
- Methods: "a Bayesian phylogenetic analysis and ancestral habitat reconstruction," and "a 3D recreation of the spinosaur skull roof using photogrammetric data."
- Evidence: "paired supraorbital fossae on the frontal bones, and a set of orbital notches and ventral grooves interpreted as salt-gland duct traces." So the glands themselves don't fossilize. The argument rests on bone traces of them.
- Distribution: "appearing to be unique to derived spinosaurs including Spinosaurus and Irritator."
- Limits, as reported: "the authors do note that this finding does not necessarily confirm that Spinosaurus or its relatives were fully aquatic, diving pursuit predators or even hunted like wading birds on the shoreline; it does suggest that these dinosaurs were well adapted to live within brackish waters."
- Why it might matter: "perhaps also serving as a key reason for why marine exploitation by dinosaurs was otherwise so limited... constrained by evolutionary limitations within osmoregulatory systems."
- Convergence: "These salt glands appear to have evolved independently, potentially revealing a case of convergent evolution with birds."

Points to weigh:
- Salt glands bear on salinity, not on swimming or diving. They'd help a brackish-water wader as much as a diver.
- [Sereno 2026](sereno-2026.md) has inland *S. mirabilis* 500 to 1000 km from the sea. [Cau 2026](cau-2026.md) treats that as compatible: "a flexible osmotic regime." No frontal is listed for the *S. mirabilis* holotype, so the glands are untested in that species.
- Also relevant to [Arden 2019](arden-2019.md): Arden describes the same frontals' dorsal concave fossae and reads them as raising the eyes. Cau reads frontal fossae as salt-gland sites. Same bone, different function. Cau cites Arden's specimens directly.

Places where the editorial is loose, so don't quote these as fact:
- "a near-complete Spinosaurus specimen from Morocco." FSAC-KK 11888 is a partial skeleton: Ibrahim 2014 calls it "a partial skeleton," and [Fabbri 2022](fabbri-2022.md) Fig 1b shows the gaps.
- "the giant 14 m theropod." That's Sereno 2022's adult composite. Ibrahim 2014 says over 15 m.
- Says Hone & Holtz 2021 and Sereno 2022 "refute... this hypothesis of a semi-aquatic lifestyle." Both papers explicitly conclude that it was semiaquatic: Sereno 2022, "a semiaquatic bipedal ambush piscivore." What they reject is the pursuit-predator or fully aquatic model. Useful for class as a demonstration of how the terms blur.
- Says *Spinosaurus* was in 1915 "interpreted as a terrestrial, sail-backed, predator." Terrestrial and fish-eating aren't exclusive, and the papers here disagree on what Stromer said about diet. [Sereno 2022](sereno-2022.md): Stromer "highlighted the remarkable adaptations in the jaws and neural spines of S. aegyptiacus for piscivory and ostentatious display." [Hone & Holtz 2017](hone-holtz-2017.md): "Stromer himself (1915) did not speculate on any particular difference in diet." Stromer 1915 isn't in the folder, so this can't be settled here.

Also cites, not in the folder: Vullo et al. 2016, Sakamoto 2022. [Myhrvold 2024](myhrvold-2024.md) is now in the folder.
