# Sereno et al. 2026, "Scimitar-crested Spinosaurus species from the Sahara caps stepwise spinosaurid radiation"

*Science* 391, eadx5486 (19 Feb 2026). DOI 10.1126/science.adx5486. File: `spinos/papers/science.adx5486.pdf`, 12 PDF pages. PDF p1 is the Research Article Summary (journal p782). PDF page = article page + 1 (article "2 of 10" is PDF p3). Summary art, Figs 1-5 viewed at full page. Supplement not in folder (fossil materials, table S1 specimen list, table S2 sizes, skeletal reconstruction, fig S18).

## Claim

A new *Spinosaurus* species with the tallest crest of any theropod came from an inland river setting 500 to 1000 km from the sea. So it was a wader and "Given its detailed resemblance to contemporary S. aegyptiacus of northern Africa, it is unlikely the latter was a diving pursuit predator in marginal marine waters."

Note the inference chain. Evidence on the habitat of species A is used for the lifestyle of species B. It holds only if the two species share a lifestyle.

## Facts and quotes

The species:
- *Spinosaurus mirabilis* sp. nov. "mirabilis, meaning 'astonishing' in Latin."
- Holotype MNBH JEN1: "an associated right premaxilla, both maxillae, the base and the bottom half of the fused nasal crest with portions of both prefrontals, the alveolar edge of the right dentary, and five maxillary teeth."
- Referred: "Jenguebi (MNBH JEN2-9) and Iguidi (MNBH IGU11, IGU25, and IGU38)." Fig 3 also figures MNBH IGU32 and IGU40, which aren't in this printed list. Check table S1.
- Locality: Sirig Taghat, "meaning 'no water, no goat' in Tamasheq," Jenguebi area, Agadez Region, Niger, Farak Formation. Cenomanian ~95 Mya.
- Diagnosis: low snout with parallel margins, wider spacing of posterior maxillary teeth, scimitar-shaped nasal-prefrontal crest with a lateral eminence, cervical pleurocoel proportions, tibial depression.
- Growth: "We estimate the subadult skeletal length of the holotype at ~8 m, with adult length in S. mirabilis uncertain given the immaturity of all known individuals." Holotype "~15% smaller than the neotype subadult skeleton of S. aegyptiacus, or ~61% the size of the largest known specimen of S. aegyptiacus."
- Crest: "relatively taller than in any other nonavian dinosaur, exceeding the height of the rest of the cranium at the orbits." "If S. mirabilis grew to the size of S. aegyptiacus, then the bony cranial crest scaled isometrically would measure ~40 cm in length and perhaps >50 cm in life with a keratinous sheath."
- Sheath is inferred from striations, based on *Casuarius*, *Macrocephalon*, *Numida*.
- Crest asymmetry: "present in all three crests," "With only three examples from disparately aged individuals, it is not possible to assess crest dimorphism."

Function: "The prominent sagittal cranial crest in S. mirabilis adds to the evidence that visual display, rather than aquatic propulsion, was the primary function of the heightened trunk and caudal sails." Crest evidence for tail function is indirect.

Spinosaurus diagnosis: "skeletal length ~10 to 14 m." "interdigitating mid and posterior maxillary and dentary teeth with festooned alveolar external margins."

Habitat:
- "Multiple specimens of S. mirabilis were found in fluvial sediments near rebbachisaurid and titanosaurian sauropod skeletons indicative of a riparian habitat."
- "~500 to 1000 km distant, respectively, from the closest marine margin in the Benue Trough."
- Finding this "could provide key evidence for a semiaquatic lifestyle, because all large-bodied (>1 tonne) secondarily aquatic tetrapods, living and extinct, are marine." Sereno 2022 worded the size rule as >2 m long and as <3 m for the origin of aquatic life. Now by mass. Same argument, different cutoff.
- Sereno 2022 said *Spinosaurus* fossils were inland, and this paper reassigns the ground. 2022 abstract: "New fossils also show that Spinosaurus ranged far inland." 2026 summary: S. aegyptiacus "bones and teeth have been found only in coastal deposits near marine margins." 2026 intro: "To date, Spinosaurus fossils are known only from nearshore deposits." The 2022 inland centrum MNBH IGU11 is now referred to *S. mirabilis*. Fossils from 2022's Égaro North (MNBH EGA1) aren't in the printed referred list here. Check the Supplement. So *S. aegyptiacus* itself no longer has inland fossils under this taxonomy, and Sereno's own 2022 inland argument for it now runs through the other species.
- Also, "provisionally referred to S. aegyptiacus" appears for North African material, softer than in 2022.
- [Cau 2026](cau-2026.md) cites this paper for "both freshwater and brackish depositional settings" and sees no conflict with salt glands in the Moroccan forms.

PCA (predatory modes):
- 43 taxa, 7 measures: cranium length, cranium height, occiput width, midsnout height, midsnout width, neck length, hind limb length.
- "PC1 captures overall size variation and accounts for most of the variance (91%)." Plot shows PC2 4.32% and PC3 2.51%, so it is drawn on about 6.8% of the variance.
- Spinosaurids "between semiaquatic long-necked, long-limbed birds such as herons... and the least modified of aquatic divers such as darters." Then: "Spinosaurid skeletal form is therefore the most consistent with predation of fish through wading in shallow waters." On the plot they lie just outside the wader hull and close to the diver hull, nearer the two than to anything else. "Between" doesn't by itself give "wader." Authors argue via the hindlimb axis.
- Both spinosaurid points depend on reconstructed neck and hindlimb from composites. *Spinosaurus* numbers come from Sereno 2022's model.
- Hindlimb: "greater absolute length than any avian wader, facilitating fish capture in water more than three times as deep (>150 cm)."
- Dismisses earlier skull-only work: "measurements of skull form with no other consideration have not been able to clearly distinguish spinosaurids" (cites [Hone & Holtz 2021](hone-holtz-2021.md) among others).
- Caption vs plot: caption numbers variables 1 cranium length through 7 hind limb length, while the arrows on the plot read 1-neck length, 2-hind limb length, 3-cranium length, etc. The text follows the plot. Use the plot labels.

Evolution, 3 phases:
- I. First ~35 Myr, Jurassic to earliest Cretaceous, "currently known only from isolated teeth."
- II. Early Cretaceous circum-Tethyan diversification.
- III. Final ~15 Myr. Spinosaurini, northern Africa and South America, "shallow water ambush specialists."
- Extinction: "fast-paced global transgression (~94.5 Mya)," "appears to have resulted in the extinction of these large-bodied piscivores." Note the logic: rising water kills a water-margin predator. Testable, but stated with "appears."
- New undescribed "Brazil spinosaurine (UFMA ITA1)... a partial skeleton under study."

Tooth analog: *Mecistops* (slender-snouted crocodile) has the same interdigitation and festoons. Conger comparison dismissed: "could not be more different."

**Fig 3A (PDF p5): SKELETON, MOSTLY NOT THE NEW SPECIES.** "Composite skeletal silhouette showing preserved bones (blue) of S. mirabilis with skeletal length of ~8 m scaled to the size of the subadult holotype (MNBH JEN1). - Blue (from a 230 dpi crop): snout and dentary, patches on neck, one anterior dorsal, a mid dorsal spine section, a posterior dorsal, two caudals, proximal femur, tibia, one pedal phalanx.

- Gray = *S. aegyptiacus*, from Sereno 2022's composite. That gray is itself upsized holotype + neotype + referred + other genera, here downsized to mirabilis. No sail shape is known: "a trunk sail of unknown shape." The sail and tail in this figure are borrowed.
- So "very similar to S. aegyptiacus in skeletal form" is hard to read from this figure, since most of the figure is *S. aegyptiacus* by construction.
